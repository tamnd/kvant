If mathematics is the art of avoiding calculations, then theoretical physics is the art of calculating without mathematics. Arkady Migdal

We need mathematical calculations to compare the results of applying ideas with measurements of the quantities with which we operate in our experiments. James Clerk Maxwell

...in the exact meaning, any theoretical scheme must necessarily be brought to a number, at least such a possibility must be provided for. Boris Bolotovsky

Never start a calculation until you know the answer. Precede every calculation with an estimate... John Archibald Wheeler

P.L. Kapitsa showed us... the full power of the method of estimating the order of magnitude, based on common sense and simple physical considerations... Nikolai Karlov

Are you so familiar with calculations and estimates?

If the conditions of a physical problem contain numerical data, please bring its solution to a number! And for this you will have to rummage thoroughly in your mathematical arsenal, since you may need the ability to operate with ordinary and decimal fractions, percentages, proportions, square roots and other seemingly simple computational wisdom. Then you need to remember in what units the numerical answer should be presented and check its reasonableness. Do not entrust all such actions to a calculator, because tinkering with the calculations yourself is by no means an excessive burden. Often this work even allows you to find a rational solution “in general” or suggests a new, personal way to get through the numerical jungle. If you get carried away and get good at assessment tasks, then there will be no price for you, and this will certainly affect your educational and Olympiad achievements. Try your computing power on several examples that we hope are quite accessible to you.

Questions and tasks

1. At the admission exams for graduate school, Academician Landau asked applicants to continue the sequence of letters “p”, “d”, “t”, “ch”, “p”... What could it mean? 2. The International Space Station flies at an altitude of 300 km above the Earth's surface. Are there enough cubes with volume $1 мм^{3}$ contained in $1 м^{3}$ to build a tower of this height? 3. Can you estimate at what distance from you the lightning struck? 4. A third hand was attached to the axis of a correctly running clock, which at each moment of time bisects the angle between the hour and minute hands. How many revolutions will the third hand make in a day if all three hands coincide at midnight? 5. Estimate the minimum permissible length of a day for a planet of mass M and radius R. 6. How would you estimate the pressure of a ballpoint pen on paper when writing? 7. Do you know how Archimedes came up with the idea of ​​“weighing” the number S?
