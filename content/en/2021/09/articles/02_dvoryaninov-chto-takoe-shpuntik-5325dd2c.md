What is a tongue and groove?

S. DVORYANINOV

YES, THIS QUESTION MAY arise from readers of Nikolai Nosov’s wonderful book “The Adventures of Dunno and His Friends.” The heroes of this fairy tale have telling names. Each name indicates the character's character or his occupation. Pilyulkin is a doctor in the Flower City, Tube is an artist, Guslya is a musician (the gusli is an ancient musical instrument). Everything is clear about Grumpy and Silent. The names Donut and Syrup speak about lovers of sweets. Two friends Vintik and Shpuntik are inventors, jacks of all trades: they can work with metal and wood. Everything is clear with a screw - it is a threaded cylinder that is screwed in and out with a screwdriver; it is used to connect various parts. There is also a propeller for an airplane and a propeller for a motor boat. But we don’t often deal with tongue and groove in everyday life. This diminutive word apparently comes from the word tongue. So let's talk about it. Let us note right away that a screw is a separate object; it can exist on its own. In the mentioned book, Shurupchik and Gvozdik later appear. You'd think the author might call the little guy Boltik. But the root of this word is bolt, and then the verb to chat, the noun chatter, which have a negative semantic connotation, immediately come to mind. Boltik is no good, and that’s why Shpuntik appeared. A tongue is a protrusion on the edge of a board that fits into the corresponding groove of another board, which is also called a tongue (Fig.1). A board with tongue and groove is called tongue and groove. These boards are used to lay the floor. A floor made from individual boards results in a single piece.
