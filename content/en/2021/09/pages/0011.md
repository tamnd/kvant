Inventing the slide rule

DV.KLEPTSYN

SINCE AT LEAST the 1960s, a slide rule was an indispensable tool for an engineer. It made it possible to quickly perform various actions - multiply, raise to a power and much more, which is now done for us by a computer, calculator, or sometimes even an application on the phone. How was it arranged? Let's figure it out in the most reliable way: we'll come up with it ourselves - and at the same time learn how to handle it. Let's start with a much simpler question: how could we make something that would allow us to add (small) numbers in one motion? Let's imagine that we took two (real, wooden!) rulers, and on one of them the scale is at the upper limit (as is usually the case), and on the other at the lower limit (and this almost never happens, but you can also transfer the divisions from one ruler to another). Let's apply them to each other so that the zeros coincide - then the scales on them coincide (Fig.1). Let us now move one of them, the bottom one, to the right - say, 2 cm. What will then be opposite the 3 cm mark on the same ruler? This mark is 3 cm from the zero on the bottom ruler - which itself is 2 cm from the zero on the top. This means that this mark is located at a distance of 2 cm 3 cm 5 cm from zero on the top ruler, and opposite the 3 cm mark we see a 5 cm mark. Hurray! With just a paragraph of reasoning and two rulers, we learned how to mechanically add 2 3 and get 5 as an answer. But the hard way is the beginning! How to add two-digit numbers?
