GAMES AND PUZZLES

Inexhaustible tangram

(see the beginning on the 2nd page of the region)

In «Kvant» the tangram has already been discussed several times (see, for example, A.P. Savin’s article “Cutting Problems” in «Kvant» No. 7, 1987 and A. Panov’s article “The Riddle of the Figure No. 51” in No. 12, 1982), but this puzzle game is so rich that you can talk about it again and again. The tangram has not just seven elements, but a deeply thought-out system that contains a number of patterns and reveals several levels of understanding of the relationships between them. The areas of the elements are powers of two. If the area of ​​the small triangle is taken to be 1, then the areas of the three middle figures are 2, and the areas of the large triangles are 4 (Fig.1). The total area of ​​all elements is also a power of two - it is equal to 16.

Fig. 1

The three middle elements - square, parallelogram and triangle - represent all possible combinations of the two small triangles. If we want the sides of the figures to fit exactly in length, then the hypotenuse of the small triangle must be applied to the side of the middle triangle, and the hypotenuse of the middle triangle to the side of the large one. If you do this sequentially, then each time the isosceles right triangle will double (Fig.2). By connecting the remaining elements of the tangram, this process can be continued. Similarly, you can construct a sequence of doubling squares. Of all the tangram elements, how can you add the largest square to figure 3? The parallelogram included in the tangram set is different from the other elements: its properties change when turned over (Fig.4). To make sure of this,

fold a large square from all the elements, then take out the parallelogram, turn it over and try to put it back - it will not fall into place, i.e. it will be a different parallelogram. This causes difficulties when assembling some figures, since sometimes it is necessary to turn the parallelogram over. By the way, this is where the manufacturers of the magnetic tangram “got burned”: they glued the magnet to only one side of the parallelogram and, thereby, impoverished the assembly capabilities.
