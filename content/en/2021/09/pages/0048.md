Selected problems of the XXVI Tournament named after A.P. Savina

1 (grades 6–8). The court was presented with 100 identical-looking coins, some of which were counterfeit. The court knows that all real coins weigh the same, counterfeit ones also weigh the same, but are lighter than real ones. The lawyer knows which coins are actually counterfeit. He answers the court's questions and proves his answers by carrying out weighings on cup scales without weights. However, the lawyer is bound by the obligation not to disclose any coin, whether it is counterfeit or real: he has no right to make weighings from which such information can be logically inferred. a) The court learned that the number of counterfeit coins is 22, 66 or 88. How can a lawyer prove that there are 66 without violating his obligations? b) The court learned that the number of counterfeit coins is 20 or 80. How can a lawyer prove that there are 20 without violating his obligations? A. Shapovalov

2 (5–8 grades). On each face of the cube, one or both diagonals were drawn in red ink. It turned out that not a single triangle with red sides was formed. What is the greatest number of diagonals that could be drawn? M. Evdokimov

3 (6–8 grades). a) There are 13 spiders in a jar. It is known that in each of their pairs at least one offended the other and that each spider offended all the offenders of their offenders. Prove that everyone has offended everyone. b) There are several (more than one) spiders in the jar. It is known that in each of their pairs at least one offended the other and that each spider offended all the offenders of their offenders. What is the smallest number of spiders that can be used to say that each one offended each other? S. Tokarev

4 (5–8 grades). Petya wrote his apartment number 100 times in the line, separated by a space. Masha must put arithmetic signs between each two numbers, and brackets where she wants. She claims, without yet knowing Petya’s apartment number, that she can get any natural number from 1 to 2021 as an answer. Is she right? D. Shnol

5 (7–8 grades). n 2 gnomes with masses 1, 2, 1, 2, 3, 4, …, n stones approached the cabin of the cable car going up the mountain. There is no caretaker, and in automatic mode, both up and down, the cabin moves with two or three passengers, while the passengers sit on two benches so that the masses on the benches are the same. At what n! 2 Can they all climb the mountain? A. Shapovalov

6 (8 grades). There are three different integers written on the fence. It is known that: x one of the numbers is equal to half the sum of the other two; x the square of one of the numbers is equal to the product of the other two; x one of the numbers is 2021. Find the other two numbers. N.Nakonechny, I.Raskina
