Let's look at the drawing from different sides

In ancient times, it was customary to present facts of elementary geometry in the form of drawings, without text. A. Akopyan’s book “Geometry in Pictures” (MCNMO, 2017), very popular among schoolchildren, was written in the same vein. Many teachers use in their practice solving problems using ready-made drawings. But some drawings can be interpreted in different ways, i.e. on the same drawing you can see different facts. Let's give a number of examples, starting with the simplest. The justifications for the stated facts are in most cases clear from the notes on the drawings, but sometimes they require brief $комментариев.^{1.}$ a) The midlines of the triangle divide it into four equal triangles, similar to the original one with a coefficient of 0.5. b) If through each vertex of the triangle we draw a straight line parallel to the opposite side, then the pairwise intersection points of the straight lines form a triangle similar to the original one with a coefficient of 2.

This type of triangle is sometimes called a “double” triangle.

2. a) Point H is the point of intersection of the heights (orthocenter) of triangle ABC. b) Points A, B and C are the orthocenters of triangles VNS, ANS and ANV, respectively.

The four points A, B, C and H are called orthocentric.

3. a) The orthocenter H of the acute triangle ABC is the point of intersection of the bisectors (the center of the incircle) of its orthotriangle A 1 B 1 C 1. b) The vertices A, B and C of the acute triangle are the centers of the incircle.
