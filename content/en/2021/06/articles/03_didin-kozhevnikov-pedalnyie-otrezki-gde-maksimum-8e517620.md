Pedal segments: where is the maximum?

M.DIDIN, P.KOZHEVNIKOV

Let a triangle ABC and a point X be given on the plane. We denote the projections of the point X onto the sides BC, CA, AB, respectively, by $X_{a}, X_{b}, X_{c}$. As is known, points $X_{a}, X_{b}, X_{c}$ will lie on the same line if and only if X lies on the circumcircle of triangle ABC (in this case, line $X_{a} X_{b} X_{c}$ is called Simson's line). Next, we assume that X does not lie on the circle (ABC), then we draw a circle through the points $X_{a}, X_{b}, X_{c}$ - the so-called pedal circle of the point X (Fig.1). We denote the second intersection points of the circle $X_{a} X_{b} X_{c}$ with the sides of the triangle as $Y_{a}, Y_{b}, Y_{c}$. We call the pedal segments of a point X the segments $X_{a} Y_{a}, X_{b} Y_{b}, X_{c} Y_{c}$. In this short article we will study which pedal segment will be the maximum

Based on the results of this small study, we will consider some interesting special cases and give the solution problem M2621 from the “Problem Book «Kvant» (see «Kvant» No. 9 for 2020). The author's solution to this problem was based on calculating the lengths of the pedal segments. We will use the following well-known fact (see, for example, the article by P. Kozhevnikov “Isogonally conjugate points” in «Kvant» No. 1 for 2016): perpendiculars to lines BC, CA, AB, drawn at points $Y_{a}, Y_{b}, Y_{c}$ respectively, will always intersect at one point Y, isogonally conjugate point X, i.e. at point Y such that lines AX and AY are symmetrical with respect to the bisector of angle BAC, lines BX and BY are symmetrical with respect to the bisector of angle ABC, lines CX and CY are symmetrical with respect to the bisector of angle ACB. In other words, points X and Y in our considerations are equal; they have a common pedal circle. We will also prohibit point Y from lying on the circle (ABC) (in this case X is not defined, or, as they say, “went to infinity”), except for the case when X lies on one of the straight lines AB, BC, CA - then the pedal circle of point X is defined and passes through the vertex of triangle ABC, and Y coincides with this vertex. The main idea will be as follows. We will find the locus of points X for which there are two equal pedal segments; this GMT will turn out to be a union of several straight lines and circles that will divide the plane into regions. Next, let the point X move continuously along the plane without intersecting the “critical circle” (ABC); in this case, the lengths of the pedal segments will change continuously, which means that as long as X is inside one of the areas defined above, the ordering along the length of the pedal segments $X_{a} Y_{a}, X_{b} Y_{b}, X_{c} Y_{c}$ will remain.
