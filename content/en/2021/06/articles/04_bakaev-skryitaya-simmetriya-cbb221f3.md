Hidden symmetry

E. BAKAEV

This issue provides the solution problem M2644. The beauty of this problem is that, despite the completely different rules of movement for the two players, it turns out that you can look at the situation from such an angle that symmetry works as a strategy in a direct geometric sense! We will present three more games with a similar twist. In problems 1 and 2, players follow the same rules, but still the position of the pieces on the board does not have symmetry - we suggest thinking about how to achieve this symmetry. In problem 3, the players walk completely differently, even no arrangement of objects is specified, but even there, nevertheless, having seen the hidden symmetry, you will solve the problem. 1 (A. Tolpygo). The white rook is on square b2 of the 8 u 8 chessboard, and the black one is on square c4. The players take turns, each with their own rook, and White starts. It is forbidden to place your rook in the battle of another rook, as well as on a square where some rook has already been. The one who cannot make a move loses. Which player can ensure victory no matter how the other plays? (During a move, the rook moves horizontally or vertically by any number of cells, and it is considered that it has visited only the initial and final cells of this move.) 2 (A. Buchin, A. Ivanishchuk). Two players take turns placing one checker on the board 65u65. In this case, there should not be more than two checkers in any line (horizontal or vertical). Whoever cannot make a move loses. Who wins when played correctly? 3 (E. Bakaev). Petya and Vasya play such a game. First, there are 11 piles of 10 stones on the table. The players take turns, Petya begins. Each move the player takes 1, 2 or 3 stones, but Petya always chooses all the stones from any one pile, and Vasya always chooses all the stones from different piles (if there is more than one). The one who cannot make a move loses. Which player can ensure victory no matter how his opponent plays?
