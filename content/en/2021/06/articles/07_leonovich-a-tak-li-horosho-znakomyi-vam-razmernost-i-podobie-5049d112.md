Are you so familiar with dimension and similarity?

Translation of measures, measurements, dimension are one of the first terms encountered at the very beginning of the study of physics. They are based on the concept of MEASURE, which is worth making strong friends with both as a necessary tool for competent problem solving, and as a support for the qualitative analysis of various physical situations. To understand what “holds together” the quantities that describe a phenomenon, what lies in the foundation of the systems of units you use, what is the balance between intuition and calculation - this and a lot more comes from mastering the method of dimensions and the closely related principle of similarity. A conversation about this will take more than one issue of Kaleidoscope. In the meantime, like our mentors from the epigraphs, let us develop a sense of proportion in physics.

It's interesting that...

... since ancient times, the Indians, when purchasing land, took as a unit of measurement the territory that a person could walk around in one day. Therefore, buyers usually hired the fastest runner for this purpose. ... the dimensions of many monumental structures, including the famous Stonehenge, are multiples of a special measure of length called the megalithic yard and equal to 82.9 centimeters. ... Using dimensional analysis, Galileo first came to the conclusion that the amount of safe load per unit volume of a beam is inversely proportional to its length. ... Galileo's solution to the problem of pendulum oscillations already contained considerations about the similar behavior of such systems. The principle of similarity was first formulated in a clear form by Newton and Hooke. ... the concept of the dimension of a physical quantity and the principle of homogeneity in dimensions was first used by the French scientist Jean Fourier in 1822, and developed and disseminated by James Maxwell. ... in 1832, the German mathematician and physicist Carl Gauss created an absolute system of measures, introducing three basic units for three basic quantities - time, length and mass. And in 1881, an International Congress was convened in Paris, which for the first time established generally accepted units of measurement for physical quantities, giving them names in honor of great physicists.

... to this day, some non-systemic units have not lost their relevance and convenience and continue to be actively used - such as “horsepower”, “astronomical unit of length” and “millimeter of mercury”. ... Max Planck, who introduced his famous constant in 1900, noted that his constant, together with the speed of light and Newton’s gravitational constant, form an absolute system of units. It is important that the modern values ​​of measurement standards are closely related to the precise determination and fixation of the values ​​of fundamental constants. ... the fundamental discovery of quantum physics - the idea of ​​quantization - was born on the basis of Niels Bohr's analysis of the dimensions of physical quantities characterizing the movement of an electron around the nucleus of an atom. ...a NASA spacecraft approaching Mars in 1999 passed too low above the surface of the planet and burned up in its atmosphere. The accident was caused by a shameful mistake: one group of specialists used British units of measurement (pound-force) in the calculations, and the other used metric ones (newton).
