PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2654, M2655, M2657 were offered at the final stage of the XLVII All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics. Problem M2656 was offered at the XLII Tournament of Cities.

M2656. The increasing sequence of natural numbers $a_{1} a_{2}$ ... is such that for every n! 100 number $a_{n}$ is equal to the smallest natural number greater than $a_{n} 1$ and not divisible by any of the numbers $a_{1}, a_{2}$, … $, a_{n} 1$. Prove that there is only a finite number of composite numbers in this sequence. P. Kozhevnikov

M2655. Given an infinite checkered plane. A teacher and a class of 30 students play a game, taking turns taking turns - first the teacher, then all the students in turn, then the teacher again, etc. In one move you can paint a single segment, which is the border between two adjacent cells. You cannot paint sections twice. The teacher wins if, after the move of one of the 31 players, there is a checkered rectangle 1 u 2 or 2 u 1 such that its entire border is colored, but the unit segment inside it is not colored. Prove that the teacher can win. M. Didin, A. Kuznetsov
