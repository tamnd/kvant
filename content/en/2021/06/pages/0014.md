M2644. Petya and Vasya play on the board 100 ¥ 100. Initially, all the squares of the board are white. With each of his moves, Petya paints one or more white squares in a row diagonally black. With each of his moves, Vasya paints one or more white squares standing vertically in a row black. Petya makes the first move. The one who cannot make a move loses. Who wins when played correctly?

Answer: Petya wins. Let's give one of the possible winning strategies for Petya. He will always make moves parallel to one of the diagonals of the board, let's call it the main one. With his first move, Petya will paint all the squares of the main diagonal, after which the board is divided into two identical “ladders” (Fig.1). Let’s mentally make each ladder symmetrical relative to the vertical line by moving each horizontal row in it, except the first, by half a cell relative to the previous one. Fig. 1 previous row (Fig.2).

As a result of the shifts, both the former verticals and the former diagonals parallel to the main one became inclined rows. In this case, the “verticals” of one ladder are symmetrical to the “diagonals” of the other. This means that for each of Vasya’s moves, Petya can respond with a symmetrical move to another ladder (two such responses are shown at figure 2). Then, after each Petya’s move, the situation on the “shifted” picture will remain symmetrical, which means Petya will always be able to make a move according to the description.
