The core of this task is this. There is a ball of known size that is initially at rest. A body of much smaller mass flies along a straight line passing through its center and collides with this ball. The force acting between the bodies is considered known. What are the next developments? The mass condition means that the large ball is at rest, or more precisely, it moves much more slowly than the small body. Further, if the force is zero (very often this is the easiest way to start considering), then there is no interaction - the big one is at rest, the small one is flying, surprised that its speed has not changed. We begin to increase the force - an exchange of impulses begins, and we can also use the law of conservation of energy, subtracting from the kinetic energy the work of the force A FD acting between the bodies, where D is the diameter. With a further increase in force, we arrive at a “absolutely inelastic impact,” when a small body stops inside a large one. You can write the corresponding equations yourself and get the solution. If we abandon the condition on the mass ratio, then we will have to take into account that the large body also moves, and then the movement of the small body occurs not until it stops, but until the velocities coincide. You can also consider a situation where a large body has an initial speed; this will not complicate the problem much. This model has several nice complication options, some of which are found in problem books. For example, a small body can fly in a straight line that does not pass through the center of a large body. In this case, the force F has a moment, and the large body obviously begins to rotate. It is less obvious that in this case a velocity component appears in the large body, directed transverse to the initial direction of motion of the small body. Another complication option is if the initial speed of a large body is not directed along the straight line along which the small body initially flies, but, for example, perpendicularly.
