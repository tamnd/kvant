KONK URS IM ENI A.P.S AVINA

The USSR will flounder helplessly in one place, trying to get a breath of air that passes through it without stopping? I don’t even remember about food and water. Answer: it's a little more complicated. Professor Wagner's body in its normal state is attracted not only to the Earth, but also to the Sun, Moon, the center of the Galaxy, clusters and superclusters of galaxies. And if the professor at some point simultaneously makes his body permeable and not attracted to other bodies, then it will indeed move uniformly and rectilinearly, but not in the reference system associated with the Earth or the German castle in which the professor was kept, but relative to some not entirely clear “inertial reference system.” The castle rotates around the Earth’s axis in a day, the Earth returns around the center of mass of the Earth-Moon system in a month, and around the Sun in a year. The trajectory of the castle in this same inertial reference frame will differ significantly from the straight line.

⟦column⟧

From the point of view of those who watched the disappearance of the professor in the fireplace, the picture should look like this: the professor irradiates himself with mysterious rays, after which he begins to choke, floundering in the air, and rises higher and higher with increasing speed. If he tries to say something, then it is still impossible to hear him - after all, his throat, lungs, vocal cords and everything else calmly pass through the air, without causing it to vibrate, i.e. without causing sound waves. Probably, we don’t need to go into other details. But in conclusion, readers are invited to independently understand another signature trick from the arsenal of science fiction - time travel. It is not asked how this can be arranged, but rather where exactly the time traveler will end up, having moved forward or backward along this very time, but finding himself, according to science fiction writers, “in the same place.” And Super Professor Wagner's trajectory will help you think about this question.
