But S. Korn, Ya. Zeldovich and S. Hawking showed that a black hole must correspond to a certain absolute temperature and it must emit energy. At first it was not clear how to connect this with the main property of a black hole - not letting go of anything, being completely invisible. S. Hawking developed a quantum mechanism for black hole mass loss. According to quantum concepts, virtual photons can appear near the event horizon of a black hole due to the uncertainty principle. In this case, the probability of transformation of such a photon into a particle-antiparticle pair may be non-zero. And according to the laws of conservation of momentum and energy, one of them will return to the black hole, and the other will rapidly move away and will be able to part with it forever. The black hole will “get thinner.” Hawking called this hypothetical phenomenon "black hole evaporation." This achievement of Hawking can be considered epoch-making in the development of physics. It was confirmed that the laws of physics cannot be considered in isolation. To explain the purely macroscopic phenomenon associated with black holes, it was necessary to involve quantum mechanics and the standard model, which were developed for the microworld. It turned out that a black hole is an unstable, nonequilibrium system. The concept of absolute temperature is not entirely accurate for it. But roughly it can be estimated as $T 10^{29} M$, where M is the mass of the black hole (in kilograms). It is easy to see that for black holes with masses on the order of the solar mass, the absolute temperature is a fraction of one kelvin, and for giant black holes it is equal to $10^{7}$ K, i.e. practically equal to absolute zero. But even if these hypotheses turn out to be correct, it is easy to calculate that the lifetime of such objects will be so insignificant that they cannot pose a danger to humanity. Astronomers have noticed a strange phenomenon in suspected black holes. When the so-called accretion disk is formed - a huge plasma disk of matter rotating in a spiral, sucked in by a black hole, thin rods of plasma were discovered along the axis of its rotation, rapidly (at speeds close to the speed of light) moving away from the black hole. This phenomenon slows down the growth of the black hole's mass. At some distance from the hole, these jets became unstable and broke up into separate sections. The phenomenon is called black hole jets. The theoretical explanation of jets consists of considering the complex alternating magnetic field generated by the accretion plasma disk.
