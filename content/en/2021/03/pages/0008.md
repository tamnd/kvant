Scenarios for the emergence of black holes have also been developed. The main scenario is the passage of the extinction cycle of a fairly massive star through the stages: supernova - white dwarf - neutron star - black hole. This is the origin of black holes with masses of the order of 100 solar masses. One option is the formation of a double star, when either both stars or one of them become black holes. In our galaxy alone, the Milky Way, we can theoretically expect tens or even hundreds of millions of black holes, about one for every thousand stars. Several hundred such “candidates” for black holes have accumulated. Today we can consider them no longer candidates, but full members of the black hole family. Another type of black holes are relics of the early stages of the birth of the Universe. The theory of this stage of the birth of the World is just beginning to be created, but astrophysicists are increasingly inclined to believe that at the center of each galaxy there should be a supermassive black hole - with a mass of the order of $10^{7} –10^{8}$ solar masses. But the closest such black hole in the center of the Milky Way was discovered only in recent years? It’s understandable, because it was reliably hidden from observation by the objects that it attracts, primarily dust and gas clouds, through which it was very difficult to measure the parameters of the motion of stars near the center of the galaxy. But still, two groups of astronomers managed to cope with this task. After almost twenty years of work, it was proven that many stars near the center of our galaxy are moving under the influence of a powerful attracting object, itself invisible. Their orbits obey the law of gravity. There is no doubt that this center is a black hole. Its mass has also been determined - 4.3 million solar masses. The light from it reaches us after 26 thousand years. It is assumed that closer to the “edge” of the Universe, where the earliest witnesses of its origin are located, there should also exist single black holes that have not yet become galaxies. And, as mentioned above, numerous microscopic black holes can exist (be born in natural “accelerators” and die). Ten years ago a strange situation arose. Astronomers could not yet guarantee that their observations reliably indicate the existence of black holes. And astrophysics theorists worked on the theory of their origin and predicted new features of these objects. And there were many scientists who did not believe in black holes, and considered theoretical work about them to be erroneous. Only they could not find specific errors in these works; for this they had to very seriously study the general theory of relativity. And all that remains is to admire the tenacity and courage of those theorists who studied black holes. They were inspired by the hard work of astronomers, who, together with engineers, creators of astronomical equipment, including satellite equipment, in the second half of the last century began a global assault on the sky, studying every part of it in more and more detail.
