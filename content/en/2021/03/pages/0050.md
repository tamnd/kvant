Description of equipment

“Gray box” is a square box of small thickness with hard, opaque gray walls. figure 10 shows the side wall of the box. In one of the corners of the box there is a hole A. In the opposite corner there is a hole B, into which a rotating fitting with a transparent tube attached to it is placed. The fitting and tube are not removable, but the tube can be rotated to position 1 or position 2. Medical syringe with a volume of 100 ml with a division value of 1 ml. Syringe needle. A strip of graph paper 1 cm wide and 15 cm long. A plastic glass (volume 200 ml) with a tinted liquid that does not wet the tube and the walls of the box well. Empty plastic glass (200 ml volume). Stationery scissors. A small roll of narrow tape.

You are required to process the measurements below and draw conclusions. As

Rim the distance between the dotted lines, as well as from the dotted lines to the walls of the box. All these distances are 10 mm each. Experience No. 2. To measure the outer diameter of the tube, measure its circumference. To do this, wrap a strip of graph paper around the tube and make 2 turns. The length of the wound part of the paper is 8.9 cm. Experiment No. 3. To measure the inner diameter of the tube, draw liquid into the syringe, then attach the syringe (without a needle) to the tube and squeeze some of the liquid into the tube so that the liquid forms a solid cylinder without air bubbles. The volume of squeezed out liquid is 8 ml. Using a strip of graph paper, measure the length of the part of the tube filled with liquid. It is equal to 8.0 cm. Experiment No. 4. Let's place the box on a horizontal table so that the arrow drawn on it points upward (Fig.11,a). Let's take 100 ml of liquid into a syringe and pour it into the box in portions through hole A so that for each portion the water level in the tube rises by 5 mm. We will measure the level h of the liquid in the tube from the bottom wall of the box using a strip of graph paper glued to the box. We will enter the obtained measurements $h V_{4}$ into the table. If, with a sufficiently large increase in the volume of liquid in the box, the level in the tube does not change, then we write in the table the two extreme values ​​of the volumes corresponding to this level. Experience No. 5. Pour out all the liquid from the box. At the same time, we note that after simply turning the box over, not all of it flows out.
