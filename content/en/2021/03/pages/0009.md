BLACK

HOLES

The first suspicions that black holes do exist appeared back in 1964, when a suborbital high-altitude probe detected strong X-ray radiation emitted by a source called Cygnus X3 in the constellation Cygnus. As it turned out from numerous later observations, this object was a system of a black hole with a mass of 15 solar masses and a blue supergiant with a mass of 19 solar masses rotating around a common center of mass. This pair is located at a fairly large distance from us, 6,700 light-years, but still closer to the center of our galaxy (26,000 light-years). What was it that emitted the X-ray photons? Most likely, the inner part of the accretion disk before being absorbed by a black hole. According to theorists, the temperature there reaches millions of degrees. For equilibrium radiation (according to Wien's law), the maximum radiation falls precisely in the X-ray range. But it is possible that jet radiation also contributes to this radiation. In the 1960s, the mystery of quasars arose. By 1964, several of these mysterious celestial objects had already been found, located very far away (according to the first estimates, at a distance of more than 10 billion light years), but nevertheless observable (mainly by radio telescopes). The term quasar is a rough abbreviation of their first official name, quasi-stellar radiosource. The development of radio astronomy has made it possible to discover (and number) hundreds of thousands of quasars. It is difficult to name the most powerful of them, since observation processing can hardly keep up with automatic registration. As of today (early 2021), this is a quasar registered on June 17, 2020, with a very long registration designation containing 23 characters: SDSSJ140821.67+025733.2. This source emits more energy in one second than, for example, our Sun emits in several hundred years! This is the only reason why it can be noticed, although it is located at a distance of over 13 billion light-waves from us.

Few people believed them then. After all, they preceded the official recognition of black holes by more than fifty years! Today, the radiation of quasars is explained (according to Zeldovich) by the radiation of accretion disks around the center of a young galaxy, which is a powerful black hole. Many of them “eat up” all the matter around them and then become completely unobservable objects. The galaxy with the above long registration number was actively “working” 13 billion years ago. And it was truly huge - its event horizon, or what is almost the same thing, the internal diameter of its accretion disk, was comparable in size to the size of the Solar system. Twenty-three years ago, two groups of astronomers began studying the supposed black hole at the center of our galaxy. One of them was headed by the German astrophysicist Reinhard Genzel. In 1987, construction began on a radio telescope complex in the Chilean high-altitude Atacama Desert at an altitude of 5058 meters. Genzel's group (European and Chilean astronomers) started working on just a few installations, and today there are 66 parabolic antennas there! The success of the observation was ensured by the enormous accuracy of their installation - the thirty-meter distances between the antenna foci were determined with an accuracy of hundredths of a millimeter.
