For the first time, the term “black holes” appeared in the texts of the Nobel Prize justifications. This should be considered the final recognition of the existence of these objects in the Universe. From the history of theoretical substantiations of black holes, it follows that there were quite a lot of theoretic astrophysicists who contributed to this substantiation. And it's hard to figure out which of them made the biggest contribution. Moreover, Penrose's 1965 paper with the final presentation of his results was presented in only two and a half journal pages and did not contain mathematical proofs. Perhaps R. Kerr or S. Hawking did more. And Penrose himself, a few years later, began to study completely different theoretical issues. But the fact is that today, of the entire galaxy of theorists who previously worked on black holes, only R. Penrose, R. Kerr and K. Thorne remain. But K. Thorne, “the leading expert on the general theory of relativity,” became a Nobel laureate in 2017 “for his decisive contributions to the development of the LIGO detector and the observation of gravitational waves” (see, for example, «Kvant», 2018, No. 3). But astronomy is now so closely connected with physics that the Nobel Committee has already begun to award prizes for astronomical work. This first happened in 1967 (H. Boethe, “for his contributions to the theory of nuclear reactions, especially for his discoveries concerning the sources of stellar energy”). Since then, this has happened 10 times, 4 of them in the past decade (2011, 2017, 2019, 2020). Over the entire existence of the Nobel Prizes in Physics, out of 214 laureates, 24 are astrophysicists. But over the last decade, almost half of the 27 laureates are astrophysicists (12). This is the area to which the center of interest in physical science has now moved! Of course, this is due, first of all, to progress in the design of telescopes, especially radio telescopes. And, of course, with the installation of telescopes on orbiting space stations. Among the large army of designers, engineers and technicians of this equipment, it is impossible to single out a few (according to the tradition of the Nobel Committee, no more than three) most worthy of the Nobel award.
