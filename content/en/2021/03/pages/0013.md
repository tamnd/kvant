In conclusion, let us dwell on the question of what was observed in the famous “black hole image” published on April 10, 2019. This can be called an “image” very conditionally, but a “photography”, as this picture began to be called in Internet and television news, is even more impossible. This is actually a graphic illustration of a report from a decade of work studying the supermassive black hole in the M87 galaxy using a system of interference radio telescopes. The main goal of this work was to understand as best as possible the characteristics of the radiation of the matter that had already come very close to the event horizon of the black hole. Therefore, the entire project was called Event Horison Telescope - EHT. 8 large radio telescopes were used in Chile, Spain, Hawaii, Mexico, the USA and even at the South Pole; the system included 9 interference arms several thousand kilometers long. The angular resolution of this system was a record - 20 millionths of an arcsecond. As A. Ponyatov writes in the journal “Science and Life” (2019, No. 5), this corresponds to the possibility of seeing a matchbox on the lunar surface. The international team consisted of more than 200 astronomers from 60 research organizations. Over four days in April 2017, UNT carried out measurements of the central region M87. All data was fed into supercomputers - one at the Max Planck Institute for Radio Astronomy, the other at the American Highstack Observatory. Computer processing of a huge amount of data made it possible to imagine the size and nature of the accretion disk of a black hole, against which the “shadow” of the black hole was determined - an area several times larger than the event horizon, from which light could no longer escape and therefore the brightness of the accretion disk radiation here is weak. The processing of several petabytes of information (a petabyte is $10^{15}$ bytes) obtained as a result of the operation of all radio telescopes was carried out using the latest computational methods developed specifically for this purpose by the project participants. It lasted two years. Therefore, the results of the work were published only in 2019. Russia was not a participant in this international consortium primarily because we do not yet have millimeter-wave radio telescopes. There are two projects, one of them is space, the other is ground-based (together with Kazakhstan). But it all comes down to financing these projects. Why was this particular galaxy chosen for the Event Horizon Telescope? The closest black hole to us in the center of the galaxy is “our” hole, the center of the Milky Way. And the second closest one with a clearly visible central black hole is M87 (the constellation Virgo). And although the distance to it is 55 million light years, which is 2100 times greater than the distance to our center, it is much brighter, since its mass is 1500 times greater. The size of its event horizon exactly corresponds to the resolution of the UNT. In addition, it is also important that this celestial object is clearly visible in both the Northern and Southern Hemispheres. This made it possible to use telescopes from both hemispheres in the project. The main result of all the work done is not the picture itself, but the parameters on the basis of which the computers “composed” it. They agreed well with both measurements based on results from other observatories and theoretical predictions. And of course, this was yet another proof of the existence of black holes. Black holes have moved from the category of “terrible monsters” to the list of “legitimate” space objects, the study of which confirms modern ideas about the Universe.
