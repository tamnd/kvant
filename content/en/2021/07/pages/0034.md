Are you so familiar with physical measurements?

How much paramount importance were given to measurements by outstanding scientists! Indeed, measurements are an integral attribute of physics. From the very first steps of its study at school, we are introduced to such terms as unit of measurement, division value, error, the difference between the procedures of direct and indirect measurements is explained, and we are taught to formalize the results of laboratory work. True, to some, laboratory work seems unnecessary and boring, but to many, who are inclined to work not only with their heads, but also with their hands, there is clearly not enough of them at school. Let's try, at least partly, to make up for this deficiency and carry out several measurements, sometimes using what is called improvised means. Perhaps, despite all the apparent simplicity, these practical actions will encourage you to move further in mastering the concept of “measurement”. Then we are waiting for you in the next issue of Kaleidoscope.

Measurement tasks

1. First measure your height using a ruler, marking it on the door frame, then measure the distance between the ends of the fingers of your outstretched hands, using the wall. Compare your measurements. What will you notice?

2. Extend your straight arm in front of you and, spreading your index and middle fingers, form the letter V. This way, amateur astronomers can measure angles of interest to them. Why? 3. There is a closed matchbox on a rectangular table. Can you use a ruler and pencil to measure the distance between its two most distant points? 4. How to determine the circumference (and therefore the diameter) of a soccer ball using a wooden ruler? 5. Is it possible to find the volume of one specific bean from a pile if you only have a beaker? 6. A closed, thin-walled, rectangular bottle with a flat bottom is partially filled with water. How can you determine its capacity without opening the bottle and having only a ruler? 7. Try to find the internal volume of an irregularly shaped vessel using scales and a set of weights. 8. Can you (following Archimedes) weigh an elephant? 9. How to determine using a dynamometer the mass of a cube whose weight is greater than the measurement limit of the dynamometer, but not more than twice?
