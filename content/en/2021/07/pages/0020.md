A few words about applications Thermoelectric devices have a wide range of applications, ranging from power generation to thermoelectric radiation receivers. The reliability and simplicity of thermoelectric devices allows them to be used even when traditional energy sources are more efficient. In addition to thermocouples, devices operating on the principle of the Peltier effect are quite widely used: coolers for various products, ranging from optoelectronics, small refrigerators, cooling systems for heated seats in cars. A semiconductor pair is usually used as a working material in such devices. $Bi_{2} Te_{3} –Sb_{2} Te_{3}$. there could be a thermoelectric battery that works due to the difference in water temperatures at different depths. Modern nuclear submarines, as we know, use more traditional steam turbines. But spacecraft that are sent into the depths of space actually have thermoelectric energy sources. The fact is that outside the planet Mars, sunlight is too weak to power the spacecraft with solar panels. Therefore, the electrical energy necessary for the operation of on-board systems is provided by converting the heat released during the radioactive decay of $Pu^{238}$ into electricity using thermoelectric couples. Such radioisotope thermoelectric generators have been used by NASA on various missions such as Apollo, Pioneer, Viking, Voyager, Galileo and Cassini. Voyager's power supplies, with no moving parts, are still working, allowing the spacecraft to continue scientific exploration after more than 35 years in service. A radioisotope thermoelectric source of electricity weighing 45 kg, producing a power of $в^{110}$ W, is also on the Perseverance rover, which recently began studying Mars.
