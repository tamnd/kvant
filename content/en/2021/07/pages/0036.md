In an inertial reference frame, the plane in which the pendulum oscillates remains constant, but in reality, say, if the experiment was carried out in Moscow, the plane of oscillations slowly rotates. Another example is that rivers flowing in the northern hemisphere wash away the right bank. And if the system associated with the Earth were inertial, both banks would have equal rights. But these are all rather subtle effects, observed either with very precise measurements or over very long periods of time. In our case, the reference system associated with the Earth can be considered inertial. This means that any other frame of reference that moves uniformly and rectilinearly relative to the Earth can also be considered inertial. Our boat is just such a frame of reference. And then, according to Galileo’s principle of relativity, all the laws of physics in the lifeboat system look the same as in the system associated with the Earth. And having jumped in the boat, you will land at the same point on the boat from which you started, just as it would be on the shore.
