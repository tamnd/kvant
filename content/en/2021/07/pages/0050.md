The final stage of the XLVII All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics

The final stage of the All-Russian Mathematical Olympiad for schoolchildren was held in Tyumen from April 16 to April 23, 2021. Let us remind you that the previous final stage took place exactly two years ago, and in 2020 the final stage was not held due to the pandemic. These circumstances influenced the selection structure for the final stage of this year; in particular, all schoolchildren of non-graduating classes who scored at the regional stage in 2020 the number of points required to pass to the final stage were automatically invited to Tyumen in 2021. All this was reflected in the number of participants in the Olympiad - it amounted to 516 people, which is almost one and a half times higher than the usual figures for recent years. The pandemic has made other adjustments as well. This year’s program for the Olympiad participants was much shorter than usual: the presentation of works and the closing ceremony took place in a remote format, and some delegations left Tyumen almost immediately after the end of the second round. The Olympics were held at the Tyumen Industrial University. The organizers tried their best and worked harmoniously. The Olympiad program traditionally included popular science lectures from representatives of the country's leading universities. The Olympics was pleasing, first of all, because it took place, and in a full-fledged in-person format. Both schoolchildren and heads of delegations missed events of this level and tasks of such complexity. By the way, this year’s assignment options turned out to be far from the simplest, even in comparison with the final stages of previous years. The following table can help to quantify the difficulty of the Olympiad problems (here we consider the participant who has received 5, 6 or 7 points out of 7 possible for this task to have solved the problem).

Problem conditions 9th grade 1. There are 1000 points marked on a circle, each colored in one of k colors. It turned out that among any five pairwise intersecting segments, the ends of which are 10 different marked points, there are at least three segments, each of which has ends of different colors. At what minimum k is this possible? S. Berlov

2. Let n be a natural number. An integer $a_{>} 2$ is called n-decomposable if $a_{-}^{n} 2^{n}$ is divisible by every number of the form $a_{+}^{d} 2^{d}$, where d is a natural divisor of n different from n.
