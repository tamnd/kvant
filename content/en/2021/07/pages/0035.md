10. It is known that the weight of a body on the Moon is 6 times less than on Earth. What measuring instruments should you take with you to the Moon to check this fact experimentally? 11. Two identical household thermometers differ only in the shape of the containers: one is spherical, the other is cylindrical. Which of these thermometers “responds” faster to changes in temperature and why? 12. How to measure the resistance of a given ammeter if there is another ammeter whose resistance is known? 13. How to measure the resistance of a given voltmeter if there is another voltmeter whose resistance is known? 14. How can you use a ruler to determine the height of a building or tree on a sunny day without climbing on it?

It's interesting that...

... the accuracy and reliability of measurements has been monitored since ancient times. Thus, in the charter of Prince Vsevolod (XII century) punishments for violators of the unity of measurements were mentioned, up to and including the death penalty. ... the inhabitants of Ancient Egypt had measuring ropes broken into pieces, which they used as rulers and compasses. With their help, the Egyptians could approximately measure the length of any segment or the size of any angle. ... various, albeit approximate, measurements can be performed literally with bare hands, if you know your own dimensions: the width of the nail of some finger, the length of the middle joint of the index, the distance between the ends of the thumb and the little finger of the outstretched hand, “your” meter - from the end of the outstretched arm to the edge of the other shoulder, and so on. ... accurate temperature measurements carried out by J. Joule in multiple experiments made it possible to establish a connection between mechanical work, heat and energy changes that had not been obvious for a long time. All these physical quantities today have a common unit of measurement - the joule.

⟦column⟧

... thermometers and barometers, originally based on the same physical principles - the equation of state of gas - were practically indistinguishable from each other. By the way, the famous humorist Mark Twain was the first to draw the attention of physicists to this circumstance. ... according to the theory of relativity, when bodies move uniformly, their lengths shorten and the rhythm in which all processes occur slows down. However, these changes can only be measured using instruments at rest. As soon as the instruments begin to move along with the objects of observation, they turn out to be “powerless” to notice such changes. ... measurements of the speed of sound in air carried out at the end of the 20th century required important amendments to be made to reference books. The fact is that in previous experiments uncertainties were allowed that did not take into account the latest values ​​of constants and parameters describing the properties of air, as well as dubious results of converting units of measurement from one system to another. ... modern measurement methods have unusually expanded the range of studied quantities. Thus, today it is possible to find distances that differ from each other by about 40 orders of magnitude - from intergalactic scales to the sizes of elementary particles.
