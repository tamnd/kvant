NIKOLAI NIKOLAEVICH KONSTANTINOV

(02.01.1932 – 03.07.2021)

Nikolai Nikolaevich Konstantinov passed away. Mathematician, physicist, teacher, outstanding organizer, who has done an incredible amount for mathematical education. It was he and his associates who started math classes in Moscow and introduced a system of teaching using leaflets. He came up with the multi-subject Tournament named after M.V. Lomonosov, International Mathematical Tournament of Cities and its Summer Conference, Independent Moscow University. He traveled with students and schoolchildren to labor camps, where work was combined with scientific studies. And all this is just part of a long list of ideas and projects of N.N. Konstantinov, who changed our world. He loved and valued communication; his interesting stories on a variety of topics (mathematics, biology, music, life...) attracted many listeners. Now all that remains are books, articles, interviews... Nikolai Nikolaevich worked at school for more than 50 years, he himself was the author of brilliant problems in mathematics and physics. He wrote:

⟦column⟧

“I know no greater joy than clear mathematical reasoning. They can be incredibly beautiful, but only if there are no mistakes in them. I feel sorry for people who don’t know this beauty. And I'm trying to open the eyes of those who don't see it, that's why I teach. The basis is kindness - words from Krylov’s fable: “Whoever is kind, excesses are a burden to him, since he does not share them with his neighbor.” But not everyone can understand this beauty, and I feel sorry for those who cannot. Although, of course, in some cases other beauty is available to them - in music, in paintings, etc. This other beauty is comparable to mathematical beauty, and just as in mathematics, it contradicts show off, originality, and violation of internal naturalness. Any such violation is like an error in solving a problem.” Nikolai Nikolaevich helped a huge number of people find themselves. Many like-minded people and students of N.N. Konstantinov is now continuing his work. Happy memory!
