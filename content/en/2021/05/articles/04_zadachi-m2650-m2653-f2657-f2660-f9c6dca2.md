Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Problem M2650–M2653, F2657–F2660 M2650. For what natural numbers are there n consecutive natural numbers whose product is equal to the sum of (maybe other) n consecutive natural numbers? B.Frenkin

M2651. There are several children and a pile of 1000 candies in the room. Children take turns approaching the pile. Each person who comes up divides the number of candies in the pile by the number of children in the room, rounds up (if it is not a whole number), takes the resulting number of candies and leaves the room. In this case, boys round up, and girls round down. Prove that the total amount of candy the boys have when everyone leaves the room does not depend on the order of the children in the queue. M. Didin

Tourists can agree on their actions in advance, after which they take turns to check in: everyone checks the rooms in any order, finds the first available room that is not under renovation, and stays there overnight. M2653*. Let p and q be relatively prime natural numbers. The frog jumps along the number line, starting at point 0. Each time it jumps either p to the right or q to the left. One day the frog returned to 0. Prove that for any natural number d p q there are two numbers visited by the frog that differ exactly by d. N. Belukhov
