SCHOOL AT KVANT

Lunar brake, collision with Theia and the law of conservation of angular momentum

S. PARNOVSKY

“Nothing lasts forever under the moon,” is sung in a famous operetta. Many things change, including the length of the day. Accurate time standards claim that it increases by 23 microseconds per year. In other words, the Earth's rotation is slowing down, albeit very slowly. This slowdown was predicted at the end of the 19th century by the English astronomer George Darwin (son of Charles Darwin). He pointed out the culprit - the same Moon, under which everything does not last forever. The Moon causes tides on Earth, which create a moment of force that causes our planet to slow down. In this case, its angular momentum is transferred to the Moon, which due to this moves away from the Earth. The distance between the Moon and the Earth, averaged over the period of the Moon's revolution, increases by 38 millimeters per year. This is clearly not a lot on an astronomical scale, but if you think that over billions of years the changes will become very large, then they should not be taken lightly. The fact that tides transfer angular momentum from the Earth to the Moon is described in detail in the article by L. Aslamazov “Lunar Brake” in «Kvant» No. 8 for 1987 (which is easy to find in the archive of old issues) or in the book by L. G. Aslamazov and A. A. Varlamov “Amazing Physics”.
