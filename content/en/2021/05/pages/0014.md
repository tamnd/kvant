KVANT 2021/№5

A.D. Sakharov at a seminar at FIAN, December 1986 (photo by Yu. Rost)

R. Reagan and D. Bush, French President F. Mitterrand, British Prime Minister M. Thatcher. These statesmen considered it an honor to personally meet A. Sakharov. In March 1989, Sakharov was elected People's Deputy of the USSR from the Academy of Sciences. More than one hundred thousand people supported this nomination with their signatures. The first Congress of People's Deputies of the USSR took place in May-June 1989 in the Kremlin Palace of Congresses. And on June 2, a terrible scene played out when 7 deputies spoke from the rostrum of the congress, one after another, with vile persecution of Sakharov for his interview with a Canadian newspaper, in which Sakharov announced the tragic fate of a group of Soviet soldiers in Afghanistan, when they, surrounded by the enemy, were shot by Soviet helicopters so that they could not surrender. Sakharov was accused of humiliating the honor and dignity of the soldiers of the “limited international contingent,” as it had been customary in the USSR to call Soviet troops in Afghanistan for ten years of war. The party-aggressive majority of deputies drowned out the attempt with shouts and whistles.

⟦column⟧

Sakharov should say something about the general inadmissibility of the Afghan war. Moreover, this shameful scene was directly broadcast on radio and television. Hunched over, literally crushed by humiliation, Sakharov left the hall. At the congress there was a small regional group, on whose instructions Sakharov began to prepare a draft Constitution (not yet for Russia, but for the USSR). It was based on real individual rights and the right of all peoples to state self-determination. This project was published in only one Vilnius newspaper. In this constitution there was no hint of the rigid presidential vertical of power that was established several years later by the current Constitution of the Russian Federation, adopted by popular vote on December 12, 1993. But Sakharov did not live to see this time. On December 14, 1989, he spoke at a meeting of the interregional group, and a few hours later his heart gave out. The party-aggressive ruling majority killed him - that’s what his friends and supporters thought. Sakharov's funeral was an event that showed that the people themselves in Moscow were no longer an obedient-aggressive majority. The authorities failed to resist the expression of the people's will - hundreds of thousands of people saw off Sakharov on his final journey. The Academy of Sciences and the leadership of its scientific institutions were in full force. But there were no representatives of the party leadership and the country's government. Sakharov was not buried in government pomp at the Novodevichy cemetery. He is buried at the Vostryakovsky cemetery, where even now, more than thirty years later, there are signs on the alleys “To the grave of A.D. Sakharov” and there are always fresh flowers on the grave. Streets, avenues, boulevards and squares are named after Sakharov in 60 cities of Russia and 14 cities of other countries. And every year the greatness of this man - a great physicist and a great humanist - becomes clearer and clearer!
