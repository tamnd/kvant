ANDREY

DMITRIEVICH

A.D. Sakharov during an interview with V. Gubarev, March 1970 (photo by Yu. Rost) solved the most difficult mathematical problems, he often did it intuitively, without the mathematical rigor of all proofs that mathematicians love and consider necessary. He often could not explain how he found the solution he needed. This was one of the reasons why, in his senior year, he moved from the mathematics club at Moscow State University to the physics club and became seriously interested in physics. Apparently, his father also played a big role in this. Having only excellent grades in all subjects on his high school graduation certificate (matriculation certificates were introduced later), in 1938 Sakharov entered the physics department of Moscow State University and, despite the loss of time during the evacuation of the faculty to Ashgabat, defended his thesis with honors in 1942. As the best student in the graduating class, he was offered a postgraduate course. But Sakharov refuses it and, during difficult war times, works at military factories in Kovrov and Ulyanovsk, thus taking part in the national struggle against Hitlerism. At the cartridge factory in Ulyanovsk, he makes a number of valuable rationalization proposals, develops an original scheme for checking the quality of the internal part of armor-piercing cartridges (which the Germans called “Faust cartridges”). If during the manufacture of this cartridge core (special hardening) there were additional

technological errors were made, then it had reduced strength properties and the cartridge could rupture while still in the barrel of the gun. To avoid this deadly defect, control tests were carried out from each batch of manufactured cartridges. And if there was at least one case of cartridge rupture during test firing, the entire batch was destroyed. It was impossible to distinguish a defective cartridge by its appearance - after all, the defect was hidden in the inner part of this core. Sakharov came up with a way to detect this invisible defect using several magnetic fields through which the core passed sequentially, sliding inside a curved copper pipe. When the core stopped, the last magnetic field showed whether there was a defect inside or whether the product was of good quality. The worker saw this result by the position of the arrow on the dial and could easily remove the defective product. The installation was distinguished by its simplicity of design and reliability of control. Sakharov, with his not very skillful hands, made the first prototype of the installation (a characteristic touch is that he found the necessary curved copper tube in a factory dump). But to substantiate the physical essence of the phenomenon and calculate magnetic fields, considerable knowledge of the magnetic and mechanical properties of solids was required. This means that the young graduate of the Faculty of Physics already had them. But, perhaps, the most important thing that was already contained in this work of Sakharov and manifested itself in his future activities was the ability to deeply penetrate into the essence of a physical phenomenon and translate an idea into a specific installation. Sakharov was awarded a bonus (the bonus in the amount of the then 3,000 rubles was equal to four monthly salaries). In his “Memoirs”, 40 years later, he writes that he was surprised to learn that the description of his device was included in textbooks on military technology, and the device itself, which he made with his own hands, is in the factory museum.
