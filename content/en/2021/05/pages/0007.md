But it was Sakharov, back in 1948, who put forward the idea of ​​holding the plasma column not by an electric field (which turned out to be impossible), but by special magnetic fields - they were called toroidal and poloidal by him. Sakharov carried out calculations of the shape of electromagnet plates to create such fields and the possibility of obtaining the required initial temperature (“match”). These calculations were, of course, much more complicated than those that he made when creating a magnetic flaw detector in his first scientific work during the war years at the cartridge factory in Ulyanovsk. A few years later, Sakharov’s idea of ​​confining plasma with special magnetic fields began to be implemented and was embodied in a device called “tokamak” (a complex abbreviation of the official term “toroidal chamber with magnetic coils”). The work was based on Sakharov’s theoretical calculations and his design ideas about creating special magnetic fields. At first, this work, carried out under the leadership of I.V. Kurchatov and L.A. Artsimovich, was top secret. But in 1956, Kurchatov achieved their declassification and made a report on this work at an international conference at the English nuclear center in Harwell. For the entire scientific world (and not only the scientific one) this became a sensation. Unfortunately, Kurchatov could not mention Sakharov’s name - it was highly classified. The mystery of antimatter A.D. Sakharov was not only involved in thermonuclear fusion. While still the Chief Theorist of the Nuclear Center, he became closely involved in cosmology, mastered its achievements and formulated new ideas that became fundamental for all modern cosmology. One of his first ideas was related to the absence of antimatter in the Universe. Physicists have not doubted the existence of antiparticles for a long time (almost 100 years). In 1927, P. Dirac, based on the special theory of relativity and quantum mechanics, predicted the existence of an antielectron. In 1934 it was discovered experimentally and called, however, not an antielectron, but a positron. A positron has exactly the same mass and electrical charge as an electron, only this charge is positive, not negative. Other characteristics of the positron (lepton charge and spin orientation) are also opposite. But the main property of antiparticles is annihilation. When a positron meets an electron, both of these objects disappear, but two gamma quanta appear (two - according to the law of conservation of momentum).
