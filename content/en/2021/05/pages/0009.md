We will nevertheless mention one of the hypotheses. We are talking about proton instability. For Sakharov's conclusions about baryon asymmetry, the proton must be unstable with an average lifetime of $10^{50}$ years. In other words, the baryon charge may not be conserved. There are no exact experimental data on the fact of proton instability; the probability of its transformation is too low. But experimenters are working hard to detect this phenomenon. Since the number of protons in a kilogram of matter is not less than $10^{27}$, there is hope to “catch” this process. The difficulty is to distinguish this process from the many other phenomena that cause recording devices to operate. For more than 25 years, a huge reservoir (50,000 tons of water) has been monitored in a Japanese underground laboratory (to protect itself from cosmic radiation) using hundreds of instruments. The result so far is this: if there is proton decay, then its average lifetime is more than 1.6 $10^{34}$ years. Well, this does not disprove Sakharov’s hypothesis. And in many theoretical works the principle of proton instability is included among the initial postulates.

The cosmological arrow of time and the multi-leaf Universe The second group of Sakharov’s cosmological works relates to global issues of the fate of the Universe in time. In the 1980s, Sakharov’s works came out one after another with such exciting titles: “Cosmological models of the Universe with a turn of the arrow of time” (1980), “Multi-leaf models of the Universe” (1982), “Cosmological transitions with a change of the arrow of time” (1984). All these articles were devoted to one global topic - what our world is. Is there one Universe in it with a single metric of space-time or is there a finite or infinite number of Universes? Is the direction of the cosmological arrow of time changing or not (expansion - compression of the Universes)? Are the laws of nature the same in the Universes? Sakharov

put forward the concept of the Megaverse as a collection of different Universes with different numbers of dimensions, different signatures (direction of the arrow of time), different topologies. And perhaps with a quantum superposition of these Universes. Any of Sakharov’s works represents a rigorous substantiation of his hypotheses and is replete with mathematical proofs that are not easy for a non-specialist to understand. Moreover, he does not cite many things that seem extremely clear to him in his articles at all (as was the case during his school years of studying mathematics). It is characteristic that this feature of Sakharov - instantly performing complex mathematical transformations - was also manifested in his activities in the theoretical management of design work. His colleagues remembered a case when, to solve one of the problems, several groups of physicists and engineers were created, each of which was headed by a prominent scientist. The results were obtained after several weeks of work. When Sakharov became familiar with this task, he completed it in a few minutes on one piece of paper. At the same time, he was upset that, not knowing about the work of other groups, he unwittingly “framed” them with the apparent simplicity of the solution. Academician A.B. Migdal said about this extraordinary feature of Sakharov: “Talent hits a target that no one can hit, and genius hits a target that no one can see.” And here are the words of Sakharov’s closest colleague in work at KB-11 and on cosmological problems, also three times Hero of Socialist Labor, Academician Ya.B. Zeldovich: “My brain is a computer that works 10 times better than the brain of an ordinary person. Sakharov’s brain cannot be classified; it is structured differently.” Sakharov's work on the multi-leaf Universe is also full of rigorous mathematics, like all his other works.
