KVANT 2021/№5

Just before the end of the war, Sakharov entered graduate school at the Physics Institute of the Academy of Sciences under the head of the theoretical department, Igor Evgenievich Tamm, and two years later brilliantly defended his Ph.D. thesis on theoretical physics of elementary particles. During his graduate studies, Sakharov carried out mandatory teaching work, lecturing on physics at the Moscow Energy Institute. After defending his dissertation, Sakharov received a number of job offers in secret organizations dealing with the effects of radioactive radiation on humans. But he refused these offers, although they suggested a significant improvement in his financial situation (he had to live in a hostel with his wife and little daughter). Sakharov was happy working in I.E. Tamm’s theoretical department and working, as he put it, “at the cutting edge of theoretical physics.” But in June 1948, Sakharov was included in I.E. Tamm’s small group to create thermonuclear weapons (hydrogen bomb). At that time it was the most secret and most important scientific and practical work in the country. Sakharov, and even Tamm himself, were no longer asked for consent to work on this topic. And a year later they began working in the top-secret KB-11 (now the science city of Sarov, the All-Russian Research Institute of Experimental Physics). Soon he becomes one of the two Chief Theoreticians of this design bureau. Subsequently, it was for his work in leading the creation of thermonuclear weapons that A.D. Sakharov became three times Hero of Socialist Labor (1953, 1956, 1962), laureate of Lenin and three State Prizes. It is characteristic that, being the Chief theoretician of the problem, Sakharov also proved himself to be a remarkable designer and inventor, which was noted in the review of his work made by three academicians, scientific leaders of the atomic problem, in 1953 after testing the first Sakharov design of a hydrogen bomb.

Controlled thermonuclear fusion

⟦column⟧

But, as the academicians said in their appeal to the government, simultaneously with the intense work on creating nuclear weapons, Sakharov achieved major results in the most important areas of physics. It was about very secret work at that time on using the energy of thermonuclear fusion not only to explode a hydrogen bomb, but also to create a thermonuclear reactor and generate electricity with its help. The idea of ​​this reactor, formulated by Sakharov together with his senior colleague I.E. Tamm, was thoroughly calculated by him to the level of engineering implementation. This idea consisted of a way to create and contain high-temperature (up to 150 million degrees) hydrogen plasma using special magnetic fields. The fact is that the thermonuclear reaction of helium fusion from hydrogen isotopes can only begin at a temperature of about 20 million degrees - due to the need to overcome the Coulomb repulsion of nuclei and force them to approach almost closely. Only then will nuclear attractive forces begin to act and a fusion reaction will occur with the formation of an alpha particle and a neutron, which have a temperature (energy) much higher - hundreds of millions of degrees. This is exactly what happens in stars, where the initial temperature, the “match,” is achieved by the compression energy of the enormous mass of the star. In a hydrogen bomb, such a “match” is a chain reaction of a nuclear explosion of uranium (or plutonium), the electromagnetic radiation of which, instead of stellar gravitational energy, compresses hydrogen to the required density and temperature. The idea of ​​the need to create a controlled fusion reaction has been expressed repeatedly. In 1949, amateur physicist Oleg Lavrentyev, a sailor of the Pacific Fleet, sent a letter to the Academy of Sciences, where he expressed the idea of ​​​​creating a high-temperature plasma circular cord (torus), kept from flying apart by an electric field.
