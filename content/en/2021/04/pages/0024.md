2) Let us recall the option that “nature itself is looking for” in the case when a soap film is stretched over a wire structure and spontaneously “selects” an arrangement in which the area of ​​this film will be minimal. We will discuss this option in detail. Let's imagine three long wire structures parallel to each other, two of which are motionless, and the third can move forward and without friction in a direction perpendicular to itself (in this case, each point of this moving wire moves strictly in a straight line). This third wire, one might say, slides without friction along the guides (along the main gas pipeline). Naturally, the structure will stop in such a position that the three sections of the soap film will form dihedral angles of 120° with each other. In this case, the film stretched over a movable wire will be perpendicular to the guides along which this movable wire can slide (the corresponding general section of the GND will be perpendicular to the main gas pipeline pipe). Well, now you can do the calculations. The sections of the main road, each going from the bifurcation point to its own village, make angles of 30° with the main road. If you draw such lines through Aniskino and Boriskino (considering them points), then the intersection of these lines will occur at a distance of 12 km from the high-pressure gas pipeline. Thus, the GND section common to the two villages is 12 km long.
