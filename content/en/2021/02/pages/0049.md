Flat mirrors are glued to the opposite walls from the inside. A laser is attached to the side wall of the box, the beam of which, having been reflected repeatedly from the mirrors, hits the photocell, moving all the time in the same horizontal plane. When the cone falls inside the box, the laser beam is guaranteed to be blocked and the voltage on the photocell changes. The distance from the beam of the upper laser to the plane formed by the beams of the lower laser is 23.0 r 0.5 cm. The photocells are connected in an electrical circuit in series with each other and connected to a USB oscilloscope, which converts the value of the total voltage on the photocells into digital form and transmits it to the laptop. The laptop has a program installed that displays a graph of voltage versus time with an accuracy of 0.5 ms. Using the $полученного^{Рис.} 14$ graph, you can determine the time of movement of the cone between two intersections of laser beams. To determine the mass of cone 9, electronic scales 1 are used. To change the mass

Procedure for carrying out measurements 1. A certain amount of plasticine is placed inside the cone and the total mass of the cone with plasticine is determined on an electronic scale. 2. Place the cone, point down, approximately 60–70 cm above the top laser and release. A distance of 60–70 cm ensures that when the cone approaches the upper laser, its movement will be almost uniform. 3. In the program window, based on the dependence of the total voltage on time (Fig.14), the following are determined and entered into the table: $T_{1}, T_{2}$ – the start and end times of the first dip; $T_{3}, T_{4}$ – start and end times of the second dip; $U_{0}$ – constant voltage level; $U_{1}, U_{2}$ – voltage levels corresponding to the first and second dips.
