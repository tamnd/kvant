Grades 8–9 1. (4) For every convex quadrilateral, is there a circle intersecting each of its sides at two interior points? A.Perepechko

2. (7) See problem M2630 “Problem book «Kvant»”. 3. Petya and Vasya play such a game. Each move Petya names an integer, and Vasya writes on the board either the named number or the sum of this number and all previously written numbers. Will Petya always be able to ensure that at some point on the board among the written numbers there are a) (3) at least one hundred numbers 5; b) (4) at least one hundred numbers 10? A. Arzhantsev

4. (7) The pentomino “cross” consists of five squares 1 u 1 (four squares are adjacent to the fifth). Is it possible to cut out nine such crosses from a 8 x 8 chessboard, not necessarily in squares? (At figure it is shown how three crosses can be cut out.) A. Gribalko

5. (8) Are there 100 natural numbers, none of which are identical, such that the cube of one of them is equal to the sum of the cubes of the others? M. Evdokimov

6. (10) See problem M2633a of the Problem Book «Kvant». 7. 6 6 See problem M2631 “Problem book «Kvant».

Grades 10–11 1. (4) Given n natural numbers. For each pair of these numbers, Borya wrote down their arithmetic mean on the black board, and their geometric mean on the white board, and for each pair at least one of these two means was an integer. Prove that all numbers on at least one of the boards are integers. B.Frenkin

⟦column⟧

2. (5) Baron Munchausen came up with a theorem: if the polynomial $x^{n} ax^{n} 1 bx^{n} 2$ ... has n natural roots, then in the plane there are a straight lines that have exactly b points of intersection with each other. Is the Baron mistaken? F.Ivlev

3. (6) Circles D and E with centers at points A and B, respectively, intersect at points C and D. Segment AB intersects circles D and E at points K and L, respectively. Ray DK intersects circle E a second time at point N, and ray DL intersects circle D a second time at point M. Prove that the point of intersection of the diagonals of the quadrilateral KLMN coincides with the center of the incircle of triangle ABC. K. Knop

4. (7) See problem M2633a of the Problem Book «Kvant». 5. (7) Is there a rectangle that can be cut into 100 rectangles, all of which are similar to it, but no two of which are alike? M. Murashkin

6. (10) See problem M2632 “Problem book «Kvant»”. 7. (12) The white “beetle” piece stands in the corner square of the board 1000 u n, where n is an odd natural number greater than 2020. In the two corners of the board closest to it there are two black chess bishops. With each move, the beetle either moves to a cell adjacent to the side, or moves like a chess knight. The beetle wants to reach the opposite corner of the board without passing through the squares occupied or attacked by the bishop, and having visited each of the other squares exactly once. Show that the number of paths a beetle can take does not depend on n. N. Belukhov
