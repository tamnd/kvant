How much does the extreme point A of the rightmost disk shift when heated? Do you understand what the “trick” of these tasks is? During thermal movements, the bodies themselves, and with them their fixed points, can change during the course of action. So, in problem 2, the square and the rectangle, when heated, behave like one large rectangle, and the fixed point during heating is the center of this large rectangle - point O (Fig. 7,a). When cooling, the square and rectangle shrink as separate figures, and their individual centers are the fixed points (Fig. 7, b). In task 3, on the contrary, the disks were first heated “individually”, and then as one large body.

But we return to our main question. In the three problems considered, considerations of symmetry immediately pointed us to fixed points. These were the geometric centers of bodies of regular shape. What condition determines a fixed point in the general case?

General condition defining a fixed point

It seems that everything is very simple - in the general case, the words “geometric center of a system of bodies” should be replaced with the words “center of mass of a system of bodies.” After all, the thermal expansion of bodies is the result of the interaction of its atoms. And from mechanics it is known that internal interactions between parts of a body cannot lead to a movement of its center of mass. Thus, we have formulated the condition for finding a fixed point for absolutely free bodies that, when heated, do not interact with surrounding bodies. We could observe the expansion of such bodies on board an interplanetary space station. But I would like to obtain a similar condition for more real cases, for example, for cases when the heated body lies on a certain surface, as was the case in the problems considered. Such a body can no longer be called an absolutely free body - when its parts shift, friction forces act on these parts. But it has one property that makes it very similar to a completely free body - it can still perform free thermal expansion (deformation obeying the fundamental principle) if it chooses a suitable fixed point. We will call such bodies $квазисвободными^{1}$.

1 Quasi (from Latin quasi) - almost, supposedly, as if. Quasi-terms are working concepts of professional physicists (quasineutrality, quasi-equilibrium, quasiparticles, etc.). These concepts have one peculiarity - “they are not very to the liking of a mind brought up on mathematical logic” (M. Planck). So you need to specially study how to work with such concepts.
