XLII Tournament of Cities

Objectives of the autumn tour

Basic option

Grades 8–9 $1. (3)^{1}$ There are 100 points marked on a circle. Is it possible that there are exactly 1000 right triangles, all of whose vertices are marked points? S.Dvoryaninov

2. A group of eight tennis players played the cup once a year according to the Olympic system (players are divided by lot into 4 pairs; the winners are divided by lot into two pairs playing in the semi-finals; their winners play the final game). A few years later it turned out that everyone played with everyone exactly once. Prove that a) (2) everyone has been to the semi-final more than once; b) (3) everyone was in the final. B.Frenkin

3. (5) In a pile of n stones, two people are playing. During a move, you can take a number of stones from the heap, either equal to the prime divisor of the current number of stones in the heap, or equal to 1. The one who takes the last stone wins. For what n can a beginner play in such a way as to always win, no matter how his opponent plays? F.Ivlev

4. (5) Given an equilateral triangle with side d and a point P, the distances from which to the vertices of the triangle are equal to positive numbers a, b and c. Prove that there is an equilateral triangle with side a and a point Q, the distances from which to the vertices of this triangle are b, c and d. A. Evnin

5. (5) The director of the zoo purchased eight elephants with numbers 1, 2, ..., 8. He forgot what their masses were, but remembered that

How can he find this elephant in two weighings on cup scales without weights or make sure that it is just a rumor? (He knows that not a single elephant gained weight, and at most one could lose weight.) A. Gribalko

Grades 10–11 1. (3) Each of the quadratic trinomials P x, Q x and P x Q x with real coefficients has a multiple root. Do all these roots necessarily coincide? B.Frenkin

2. (4) On the straight line we marked the points $X_{1}$, … $, X_{10}$ (in exactly this order) and constructed isosceles triangles with angle D at the vertices on the segments $X_{1} X_{2}, X_{2} X_{3}$, … $, X_{9} X_{10}$ as on the bases. It turned out that all these vertices lie on a semicircle with diameter $X_{1} X_{10}$. Find D. E. Bakaev

3. (5) The natural number N is a multiple of 2020. In its decimal notation, all the digits are different, and if any two of them are swapped, the result is a number that is not a multiple of 2020. With what number of digits in the decimal notation of the number N is this possible? S. Tokarev

4. (5) The sides of the triangle are divided by the bases of the bisectors into two segments each. Is it necessary to form two triangles from the six resulting segments? L. Emelyanov

5. There are 101 coins in a circle, each with a mass of 10 g or 11 g. Prove that there is a coin for which the total mass of k coins to the left of it is equal to the total mass of k coins to the right of it, if a) (3) k 50; b) (3) k 49. A. Gribalko
