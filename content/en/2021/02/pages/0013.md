THERMAL

Property 3. During thermal expansion, figures drawn on a plane turn into similar figures. This property follows from the first two properties. It tells us that when the temperature changes, individual regions of the plane can move and rotate (relative to external bodies, for example, the surface of a table), increase and decrease their size, but their shape does not change. From this property it also follows that figure 1,c correctly illustrates the change in the shape of the ring during thermal expansion. From the first two properties, another, most democratic property of thermal expansion also follows. Property 4. All “internal” observers (observers sitting on a heated plane) perceive thermal expansion in exactly the same way - as an isotropic expansion of the Universe. Namely, every point on the plane visible to them will move away from them at a speed directly proportional to the distance to it. Indeed, if the observer does not see the surrounding external world, for example, the surface of the table on which the plate lies, then he does not notice either his shift relative to the table or his rotation. He sees only the points of the heated plate moving away from him, the angles between which remain constant. And this is the picture of the isotropic expansion of the Universe. Let us now discuss the “external” geometry of thermal expansion, i.e., we will find out how the thermal expansion of the plane is seen by an observer located outside the plate. Property 5 (technological). An external observer can restore the new position of any point of the heated body if he is given a new position of at least one segment. More generally, using one segment and its image, you can reconstruct the entire picture of thermal expansion. Property 6 (parameters of accomplished thermal expansion). The accomplished thermal expansion has two universal parameters (they are the same for all geometric figures): the coefficient of linear expansion k and the angle of rotation M. These parameters are uniquely determined if its image is given for a certain segment. Indeed, if the picture of thermal expansion is completely reconstructed from the known image of a single segment, then the parameters that define the image of the segment are also the parameters that completely define the accomplished movement. The image of a segment is specified by three parameters: the displacement vector of one of the ends of the initial segment, stretching the segment k times and rotating it through a certain angle M. The first parameter, the displacement vector of one of the ends, is not universal - it is different for each point (see Fig. 4), but the last two parameters are the same for all segments, so we can take them as parameters of the accomplished movement.
