Yes, yes, don’t be surprised that the usual “plus” has been replaced by a different mathematical sign - after all, the conversation will not just be about the addition of the efforts of two scientific fields, but about their multiplication. Of course, in school practice, geometric constructions and formulas are often perceived only as a useful aid in the study of physical problems, especially in kinematics, statics and optics, and even, perhaps, in astronomy. But even within these limitations, one can notice how problems “come to life”, how their conditions become more visual, and how solutions become both rational and incomparably elegant. Geometry, combining living contemplation and strict logic, permeates physics, sometimes revealing a new, harmonious dimension in it. This close connection has lasted for thousands of years and is brilliantly embodied in many scientific discoveries and engineering solutions. Let us also try to select from a scattering of examples those that reflect the effective interpenetration of sciences, resorting today to the help of PLANIMETRY. It's interesting that...

...the names of geometric figures originally meant specific objects. So, “rhombus” came from a non-circular tambourine, “trapezoid” - from a table, “line” - from linen thread. ...the ancient Greek Anaximenes was helped to understand the nature of eclipses by the guess that the Moon and the Sun are two balls moving in the same plane. ...another ancient Greek scientist Thales came up with a geometric method for measuring distances to a ship at sea from the shore. Now a stereo tube operating on the same principle is used for this purpose.

⟦column⟧

...back in the third century BC, Archimedes proved new mathematical facts using the properties of the center of mass. For example, in this way he derived a theorem about the intersection of three medians of a triangle at one point. ... the theory of conic sections, which arose within the framework of “pure mathematics” and was developed in detail by the ancient Greeks, found its most important applications in the 17th century in the works of Newton and Kepler on celestial mechanics. ...having established the law of motion of a body thrown at an angle to the horizon, Galileo did not publish it for a long time, realizing that this result was obtained in the “flat Earth” approximation, and he did not know what the shape of the trajectory would be in the case of sufficiently high velocities. ... the relationships between distances in space are studied in geometry, and the properties of time - in chronometry. Therefore, the kinematics that they begin to study in school is nothing more than a unification of these sciences, or “space-time geometry.” ... modern research tools have made it possible to reliably establish that huge buildings several thousand years old, like Stonehenge, obeyed strict geometric relationships and were used by our ancestors for astronomical observations.
