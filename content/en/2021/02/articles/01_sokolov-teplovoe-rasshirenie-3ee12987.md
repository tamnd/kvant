Thermal expansion

E.SOKOLOV

WHEN HEATED, BODIES EXPAND, everyone agrees with this. (There is an important exception to this rule. Water in the range from 0 q C to 4 q C contracts when heated. Thanks to this, our reservoirs do not freeze to the bottom in winter.) Many can even give a formula for changing the length of the rod when heated: $l l_{0} 1 D' t$. But on the question of how the shape of a body changes when heated, there is no longer unanimity (Fig. 1).

⟦figure⟧

Fig. 1. Thermal expansion of the ring: a) initial shape of the ring; b and c) “swelling” and “stretching”

Some represent thermal expansion as the “swelling” of a heated object in all directions (see Fig. 1, b), while others - as its “stretching” in all directions (see Fig. 1, c). Probably, when the former do figure, they imagine a spreading ink stain, and the latter imagine a rubber film stretching in all directions. The question arises: which of these pictures correctly conveys the change in the shape of a body during thermal expansion? The well-known formula does not give us an answer to this question: it tells us only about the lengths of the rods and says nothing about the radii and thicknesses of the rings. To learn how to answer such questions, we need to try to formulate the law of thermal expansion in the field.

in general form. Namely, it is necessary to formulate it for such a geometric figure and in such a way that results for rings, circles, triangles, etc. would be obtained from it automatically, as special cases. This is not difficult to do. Let us turn to the property of heated bodies, which is familiar to anyone who has cut with a jigsaw. If we cut a circle in a plate heated to 20 q C with a thin, thin file, the circle will easily fall out of the plate. This means that, having become free, the circle did not increase its size. And if, on the contrary, we want to insert it back, then it will easily insert and completely take its old place. In other words, having become free, the circle did not reduce its size. It turns out that the state of a body at a given temperature is the same regardless of whether it was heated as a separate figure or as part of a plate. And this will happen not only at a temperature of 20 q C, but also at 37 q C, and at 156 q C and at any other temperatures. Consequently, all figures expand equally, regardless of whether they are heated by themselves or as part of the plate. This observation allows us to take an infinite plane as a general case of a heated body: drawing various figures on it, we, knowing how the points of the plane move, will always find how the points of the drawn figures move and how their shapes change. It remains to take the last step - from the well-known law on increasing the lengths of rods, obtain the law of motion of points on an infinite plane. This is also easy to do: we can consider any two points of the plane to be the ends of a drawn infinitely thin rod, so the distance between them is determined by the formula $l l_{0} 1 D' t$.

Property 3. During thermal expansion, figures drawn on a plane turn into similar figures. This property follows from the first two properties. It tells us that when the temperature changes, individual regions of the plane can move and rotate (relative to external bodies, for example, the surface of a table), increase and decrease their size, but their shape does not change. From this property it also follows that figure 1,c correctly illustrates the change in the shape of the ring during thermal expansion. From the first two properties, another, most democratic property of thermal expansion also follows. Property 4. All “internal” observers (observers sitting on a heated plane) perceive thermal expansion in exactly the same way - as an isotropic expansion of the Universe. Namely, every point on the plane visible to them will move away from them at a speed directly proportional to the distance to it. Indeed, if the observer does not see the surrounding external world, for example, the surface of the table on which the plate lies, then he does not notice either his shift relative to the table or his rotation. He sees only the points of the heated plate moving away from him, the angles between which remain constant. And this is the picture of the isotropic expansion of the Universe. Let us now discuss the “external” geometry of thermal expansion, i.e., we will find out how the thermal expansion of the plane is seen by an observer located outside the plate. Property 5 (technological). An external observer can restore the new position of any point of the heated body if he is given a new position of at least one segment. Property 6 (parameters of accomplished thermal expansion). The accomplished thermal expansion has two universal parameters (they are the same for all geometric figures): the coefficient of linear expansion k and the angle of rotation M. These parameters are uniquely determined if its image is given for a certain segment. Indeed, if the picture of thermal expansion is completely reconstructed from the known image of a single segment, then the parameters that define the image of the segment are also the parameters that completely define the accomplished movement. The image of a segment is specified by three parameters: the displacement vector of one of the ends of the initial segment, stretching the segment k times and rotating it through a certain angle M. The first parameter, the displacement vector of one of the ends, is not universal - it is different for each point (see Fig. 4), but the last two parameters are the same for all segments, so we can take them as parameters of the accomplished movement.

But we return to our main question. In the three problems considered, considerations of symmetry immediately pointed us to fixed points. These were the geometric centers of bodies of regular shape. What condition determines a fixed point in the general case?

General condition defining a fixed point

It seems that everything is very simple - in the general case, the words “geometric center of a system of bodies” should be replaced with the words “center of mass of a system of bodies.” After all, the thermal expansion of bodies is the result of the interaction of its atoms. And from mechanics it is known that internal interactions between parts of a body cannot lead to a movement of its center of mass. Thus, we have formulated the condition for finding a fixed point for absolutely free bodies that, when heated, do not interact with surrounding bodies. We could observe the expansion of such bodies on board an interplanetary space station. But I would like to obtain a similar condition for more real cases, for example, for cases when the heated body lies on a certain surface, as was the case in the problems considered. Such a body can no longer be called an absolutely free body - when its parts shift, friction forces act on these parts. But it has one property that makes it very similar to a completely free body - it can still perform free thermal expansion (deformation obeying the fundamental principle) if it chooses a suitable fixed point. We will call such bodies $квазисвободными^{1}$.

Conclusion

We fully admit that after reading the article, a thoughtful reader may have legitimate questions. –You know, I have no doubt about the correctness of the answers received above. But the logic you used to solve problems seems very unusual. In your problems, inanimate plates themselves choose fixed points, a fixed stop causes an expanding square to rotate, velocities generate forces and these forces are directed against the velocities. All this is very different from our ordinary World. This is not Newton's World, in which forces generate changes in speeds. This is not Aristotle’s World, in which forces generate velocities directed along the forces. This is a completely different World. – You are right, much in our reasoning was unusual. But there are no mistakes in them. Imagine a mechanical puppet theater made by a skilled mechanic. The dolls in this theater move, dance, meet and separate. The movement of the dolls is controlled by a dozen springs and rods hidden by the master behind the scenery. And, of course, if we look closely at the operation of the mechanism, we will find here Newton’s laws, Hooke’s law, and our usual reasoning. But we humans also have another ability. We can forget about the mechanisms and see before us only the heroes of a fascinating play, beautiful ladies and gallant gentlemen who live according to their own laws. According to laws that we will not find in mechanics, but which we very often find in life.
