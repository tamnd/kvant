Where can I get polarized light?

M.STARSHOV

RECENTLY IN ONE OF THE METHODOLOGICAL physics journals I came across such a scientific phrase: “... it is very difficult to create natural sources of partially polarized light.” Perhaps there is no need to create natural sources. And not because it’s not easy, but because they surround us all the time. It is known, for example, that bees “know”, recognize and use polarized light. And one can guess that Nature provides them with partially polarized light. It is much less common to find fully polarized, as optics say, linearly or plane polarized light. And most likely it will be light created by man. One cannot help but admire the Nicol prism, invented and manufactured by the Scotsman William Nicol in 1828. To make this device, it was necessary to know almost everything about the polarization of light, and most importantly, to understand that it would be good to have two of these devices. Then you can not only convince everyone that they work, but actually make them work. However, the scientist made a second prism, very similar to the first, only in 1839. Isn't it strange? So, the Nicolas prism, or simply nicole, makes almost completely polarized light from ordinary light. Light falls on it with vibrations in any plane containing the direction of propagation of the light beam, and it transmits only vibrations in one plane, if we speak “on the fingers”. A second identical prism can convince the observer that the light coming out of the first one was polarized.

This is beautiful and wonderful, only these prisms are made from rare, and therefore very expensive, Icelandic spar crystals. But about twenty years ago, when I started working in the “Constellation” shift of gifted children, in the group of young physics lovers there was a wonderful boy Dima from the city of Rtishchevo. At one of my lectures, I talked about the polarization of light, methods for producing and using such light. When the lecture ended, all the boys ran to play football, and this Dima walked through the territory of the children's camp, along old abandoned buildings, occasionally bending down to the ground. Then he went to the electrician and asked for some insulating tape, which he used to connect the assembled pieces of old window glass. It was funny to watch how he invited first one, then another comrade to look through his homemade product at the sky: “Look like this, and now turn it this way!” Well, do you see how the sky is changing? It was he who made the so-called Stoletov foot, which very simply and inexpensively makes it possible to obtain or detect linearly polarized light. And so, Dima became convinced that the sky sends us light in varying degrees of polarization depending on the position of the sun. Honestly, for such an experiment you don’t have to do anything with your hands at all, just open your eyes while walking around the city or in nature. Here is a school building in front of me (photo on figure 1), but why are there such different glasses in its windows: some are gray, while others are completely black, as if there was no glass at all? Looking with curiosity at the similar windows of a Parisian palace in the rays of the setting sun, the young Frenchman Etienne Malus discovered the polarization of reflected light for humanity at the beginning of the 19th century. Glare on water or on an expensive limousine and shine in general also indicate the polarization of reflected light. True, this does not apply to metal handles and other parts of the car; only paint and glass “work” (with metals everything is more complicated). Anyone who becomes a physicist or designer will probably use this.

With 99 percent confidence, we can say that among the devoted readers of the magazine «Kvant» there is not a single one who does not use polarized light every day. While typing this text, I stop to correct a mistake in a word, pick up Stoletov’s just photographed foot (photo on figure2) and look through it at the screen of the liquid crystal monitor of my laptop. I see the text and begin to rotate the box around the direction of the screen. Suddenly the picture weakens, becomes cloudy and dark, I can’t see the letters at all. When you turn further, everything is restored. It is not difficult to guess that polarized light comes from the screen to us, and the foot checks this.

But you can do it even simpler; just get a piece of smooth, clean glass the size of half a notebook sheet or even less. Holding it to the side of the monitor at eye level, I turn it so that I can see the reflection of the screen on it, like in a mirror. I slowly move my hand with the glass forward and up, without losing the reflection of the screen. And the image suddenly weakens, almost to the point of disappearing. I continue moving in the same style - the image appears again. And it disappears again when the glass is at the level of the screen and eyes, only on my left side. Isn't it cool? If I were you, I would immediately start looking for glass to check what I read. If you use a mirror instead of glass, you won’t get this effect. The mirror layer is a metal, an amalgam with a high coefficient of reflection, and its protective glass reflects weakly, and the eye does not feel changes. Or maybe, wherever you look, you will see partially polarized light? And what beautiful observations you can make with him!
