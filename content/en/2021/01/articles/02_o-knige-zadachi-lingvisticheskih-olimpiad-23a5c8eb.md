To the attention of our readers

You probably noticed that in this issue of the magazine, on the second page of the cover, a new section appeared - “Linguistic tasks”. We take these problems from a wonderful book (Problems of Linguistic Olympiads. 1965 - 1975. 2nd edition, revised/ed. by V.I. Belikov, E.V. Muravenko, M.E. Alekseev. - M.: MTsNMO, 2020), which contains the problems of the Olympiads in linguistics and mathematics. Such Olympiads have been held in Moscow since 1965. One of those thanks to whom the genre of linguistic tasks itself arose is academician A.A. Zaliznyak, great Russian linguist. And the Olympiads arose on the initiative of another outstanding linguist, A.N. Zhurinsky, and has continued for many years since then.

Linguistic problems are in some sense close to mathematical ones: they can be solved based on given conditions; all the material necessary for the solution is contained in the problem statement or can be completed by the solver on the basis of his intuitive ideas about the structure of his native language. Solving problems is an interesting activity, but the reader not only gets pleasure, but also learns a lot of new things, independently “discovering” the structural elements of the language and the laws of its structure. In addition, the tasks use many different languages ​​of the world as material, which the reader may not have heard of before, and yet these languages ​​are spoken by millions of people.

M2623. In a one-round football tournament, all teams scored different numbers of points. If a victory were given not three, but two points, then all teams would also have a different number of points, but the place taken by each team would be different. With what minimum number of commands is this possible?
