“There is no current in the lake, the water stands still,” Buratino responded. – Just as a ship floats in the ocean, so a plane floats in the ocean of air. – Don’t be mistaken in terms, my dear Pinocchio. Ships do not float, but walk, as real sailors say. According to Archimedes' law, balloons, aerostats, airships, and even children's balloons filled with helium float in the ocean of air. And planes fly. But first they must take off. And a force called lift lifts them off the ground and lifts them up. It occurs when the air flow hits the plane,” Papa Carlo continued. – But when I ride a bicycle, I feel this oncoming flow very well. But at the same time I don’t rise up. And even a car that has picked up a lot of speed continues to roll along the ground and doesn’t fly anywhere,” Buratino objected. – What you say is true. But the plane and especially its wings have such a special shape that leads to the appearance of a lifting force directed upward. The greater the speed of the oncoming flow, the greater the lifting force becomes. Finally, it overcomes the force of gravity acting on the aircraft, and then the aircraft lifts off the ground. – But if the lifting force is greater than the force of gravity, then the plane will rise higher and higher without limit. “But that doesn’t happen,” Buratino tried to argue again. - Of course not. When necessary, the airplane pilot, by changing the angle of attack of the wing, ensures that the two forces - lift and gravity - become equal, and then the plane flies at a constant altitude,” concluded Papa Carlo. – Now compare all the facts that we just talked about and explain why it is easier for a plane to take off against the wind. - Yes, I can explain! - Buratino exclaimed, - here it is, a logical chain. To lift an aircraft, sufficient

When the wind blows towards an airplane on the runway, the airplane's speed relative to the air is equal to its own speed plus the wind speed. In this case, the required speed value v is obtained at a lower natural speed of the aircraft. Like this. “That’s right, well done,” concluded Papa Carlo. “That’s why they say that it’s easier for a plane to take off against the wind.” This was especially important in the early days of aviation, when engines were not very powerful. Modern airplanes can take off with a tailwind, but in any case, pilots take into account both the wind speed and its direction.
