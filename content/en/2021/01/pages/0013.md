One might even think that opening a chamber with a cat, which we find alive, will instantly, at a distance, cause the death of the dog, which until now was only “half-dead.” Of course, it is impossible to predict the outcome of the opening of the chamber, because we will find a cat alive or dead with the same probability of 1 2, as well as a dog; however, we understand that Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen were puzzled. At the end of the article, they expressed their opinion about the need to develop new quantum mechanics. It could be based on the assumption of the existence of hidden parameters, i.e. parameters that are inaccessible for experimental verification and are not included in the theories of Schrödinger and Heisenberg.

Bell's inequalities and Aspe's experiments on the EPR paradox were disputed by many researchers, including Bohr. Other prominent scientists, including Louis de Broglie and David Bohm, like Einstein, would prefer to restore determinism. The discussion lasted a long time and had a philosophical overtone. include some measurable inequalities that contradict the usual form of quantum mechanics. Bell's inequalities were verified by Alain Aspe and his collaborators in Paris in 1982. They reproduced a situation similar to the one we described a little earlier. Obviously, the scientists did not use a cat and a dog, since quantum mechanics is not applicable to macroscopic objects, but photons, the polarization of which (i.e., the direction of oscillation of the electric field) can have two mutually orthogonal directions, just as a cat and a dog can be in two equally probable states (alive and dead). The researchers set out to find a correlation between photon polarizations. And this is just one of the many difficulties of the experiment. Another difficulty was related to the fact that photons move at enormous speed, and during their movement a lot must be done. Ultimately, Aspe's experiments led to the conclusion that Bell's inequalities could not be verified. This means that quantum mechanics, as presented in textbooks, is correct and cannot be replaced or supplemented by the theory of hidden parameters. Aspe's experiment turned the thought experiment of Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen into a real experiment.
