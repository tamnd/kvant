Why does a plane take off against the wind?

S. DVORYANINOV

“I see you’re thinking hard about something?” – Papa Carlo asked his Pinocchio, noticing that he had been leafing through the pages of a physics textbook for a long time. – Yes, today in class the teacher said that it is easier for a plane to take off against the wind. And we need to explain why this is so. I looked through the entire textbook, but did not find the answer. “Well, let’s think logically,” Papa Carlo shook off the shavings from his apron, walked away from the workbench and sat down at the table. – What does it mean to “reason logically”? Logic is when B follows from A. But in this physical problem there are no A and B,” Buratino said disappointedly.

“You’re wrong,” Papa Carlo objected. – And now you will see for yourself. To answer your teacher's question, we will build a logical chain. I will ask you questions, and you will answer them. So, let's start. – What happens to the plane before it leaves the ground and is in the air? “The plane is accelerating, moving along the runway, that’s clear,” Buratino answered. – And relative to what is the plane moving? – Regarding the land, of course, and regarding what is on it. This, for example, is the airport terminal building, people watching the planes, trees... - Well, what’s the most important thing? Relative to what is the plane moving? The plane accelerates relative to the air! Let us first assume that the weather is windless, completely calm, such that not a single leaf on the tree moves. So, relative to this still air, the plane moves faster and faster along the runway. Agree? – Papa Carlo clarified. – Yes, I agree, it looks like the accelerated movement of a motor boat on a lake.
