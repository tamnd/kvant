Without loss of generality, we will assume that a cycle of length greater than 2 exists in the black partition. Let us call an internal figure of type O from a partition that lies entirely inside our cycle. Let us call a boundary figure of type O from the tiling whose two black cells belong to the cycle we have chosen. The segments in the cycle alternate: after the segment connecting the black cells of the figure of type O, there is a segment connecting the black cells of the figure of type Z, which means that the length of our cycle is 2k, where k N. Let us denote the number of black and white cells lying strictly inside our cycle by b and w, respectively. Any black cell inside a cycle belongs to an internal figure of type O, any white cell inside a cycle belongs to either a boundary figure of type O or an internal one. There are k boundary figures in total, and each of them belongs to exactly one inner white cell. On the other hand, all internal figures of type O contain exactly b black cells, and therefore exactly b white cells. Thus, w b k. Now let's calculate the relationship between b and w in a different way. Let's look at the new grid. The nodes of the new grid will be the centers of the former black cells. The centers of the new cells will be the centers of the former white cells.
