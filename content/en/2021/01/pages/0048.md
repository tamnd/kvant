Let us show that then all the segments intersect. To do this, we prove the following lemma. Lemma 3. A line is given several segments, any two of which intersect. Then all segments have a common point. This statement is not as obvious as it may seem - in other situations similar to this, a similar statement may not be true. An example of such a situation is given in Exercise 2. Proof of Lemma 3. Consider the middle - the gap between points with numbers n and n 1 (the points are still numbered in order from 1 to 2n). Let us show that it is contained in all segments. Let's see how the left and right ends of the segments are located. If to the left of the middle not all ends are left (Fig.4), then on the left there is a right end of some segment, and on the right there is a left end of some segment. Then these two segments do not intersect. This means that on the left all the ends are left, and on the right all the ends are right (Fig.5). Thus, all segments contain a midpoint, and Lemma 3 is proven, and thus Lemma 2 is proven.
