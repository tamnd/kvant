Hint: See what current-voltage characteristics you can get by connecting a diode and a resistor in series and parallel. Regarding nonlinear resistances, it is useful to understand one more thing. Nonlinearity of resistance, i.e. its disobedience to Ohm's law can be of several types. For example, it may be related to the properties of the medium itself through which the current flows - this is the case of a semiconductor diode. But another situation is also possible - nonlinearity can also be associated with the properties of the medium, but depending on the presence and magnitude of the current flowing through it. This is the case of a gas discharge, a vacuum device, sometimes a semiconductor, a dielectric, and even just metal! For a gas discharge the situation is this: when there is no current, the medium is almost a dielectric, i.e. almost just gas. In a vacuum, if there is no current, there is almost nothing at all - this is a vacuum! In a semiconductor and dielectric, under certain conditions, it is possible to increase the number of free electrons that can carry charge. And the simplest situation is in metal, as Joule and Lenz teach us - the metal is heated by current, and its resistance increases. The medium changes under the influence of current. Another situation: the current may not change the properties of the medium, but its magnetic field can compress it towards the axis of the conductor. In this case, the cross section through which most of the current flows decreases, and the resistance (you remember that R U L S) increases. This effect is more noticeable in semiconductors.
