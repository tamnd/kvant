Three plane theorem

Y. BLINKOV

THIS ARTICLE WILL CONSIDER one important statement that can be formulated and used immediately after studying the axioms of stereometry. Note that in the form of a theorem it is found only in the book [1] and is tied to the topic “Parallelism of a line and a plane.” Sometimes this statement appears (in a simplified or other formulation) in the form of problems (see, for example, [2], [3], [4]), but attention is not focused on it. We will try to partially fill this gap. So, the wording. Three plane theorem. If three planes intersect along three different lines, then these three lines are either parallel to each other or intersect at one point. Proof. Let planes D and E intersect along straight line c, E and J – along straight line a, D and J – along straight line b. Since each pair of lines (for example, a and b) lies in the same plane, they either intersect or are parallel. If no two of the indicated lines intersect (Fig. 1), then they are pairwise parallel and the condition of the theorem is satisfied. Let some two of the lines intersect, for example, a and b intersect at point C (Fig. 2). Then point C belongs to the planes E and J, since it belongs to them
