if the space between the plates does not contain dielectric, there is a risk of the plates touching - this is determined by the manufacturing accuracy, the rigidity of the plates and vibrations. Therefore, the distance between the plates must be chosen so that they cannot touch during operation. If the capacitor is designed to operate with high voltages, this distance also depends on the voltage. Let, for example, the breakdown strength of air be 30 kV cm, and the possible deviation of the plates be 0.5 mm. Then it is impossible to make a gap less than 1 mm at any voltage, but at a voltage of, for example, 3 kV, you will have to make a gap of 2 mm. To avoid problems when the plates touch, a thin film of dielectric is sometimes inserted into the gap between the plates. If the film is thin, it does not affect the capacity, but protects it from touching the opposite plates. In some problem books there is a problem - what is the capacitance of a capacitor in which there is an additional conductive or dielectric plate between the plates, all surfaces are parallel, edge effects are neglected.
