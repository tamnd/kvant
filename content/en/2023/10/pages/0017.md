As you know, sliding contacts may not behave well when handling high currents. The second way is to move the coils. This is the most ancient path, about a century old. It seems that the easiest way is to use something like a spring as inductance - when it is compressed, the turns move closer together, and the inductance increases. However, such designs were not used - it is difficult to ensure that the turns are fixed and move in a controlled manner, and if they are left free, such inductance will respond to vibrations and shocks and generally depend on the position of the equipment in space. It is possible that the coil was divided into two, they were located on the same axis and could move closer and further away from each other. But an option with two coils was used - one inside the other, the coils were switched on in series and the inner one rotated (Fig. 14). If the direction of the current in the turns of the coils coincided, then the inductance increased; if the coil was rotated by 180° and the currents flowed in the opposite direction, then the inductance decreased significantly. This situation was realized without switches and contacts, which can be significant in the case of high currents.
