VARIABILITY

On figure 2 two variable capacitors are presented, the capacitance of which changes with the rotation of the axis on which the plates are fixed. On the left capacitor, the movable (P) thinner plates “run” onto the thick fixed one (H), i.e. changes $площадь^{S,}$, which is included in the formula for the capacitance of the capacitor. Pay attention to the shape of the plates of the right capacitor - these are not sectors, as in the previous case, i.e. the designer clearly wanted to obtain some other dependence of capacitance on the angle of rotation. What do you think, which one and why? (Hint - remember Thompson's formula.) At figure 3 we see two sets of 4 capacitors each. The lower (in the picture) capacitors, in which all movable plates (12 pieces) are installed on a common shaft, are ordinary capacitors with a change in the overlap area of ​​the plates. For the upper capacitors, the fixed plates are designated H (they have them in common with the lower capacitors), and the movable plates are designated P. These are trimming capacitors, they are also called “trimmers”, from $английского^{trim}$ - to put in order (trimmer resistors are also called that). The capacity of trimmers changes very rarely, usually during the initial setup of the equipment, and possibly during the repair and replacement of some parts (for example, vacuum tubes). Trimmers on figure 3 differ in that they change not the covering area of ​​the plates, but the gap between them.

In general, the situation of changing the gap is quite rare, so we will give another example. For a figure 4 capacitor, the top plate moves closer to the bottom plate when the screw is screwed in. Everything “adjustable” has several important differences from just “variable”. At the end of the adjustment, the value should be fixed (either by itself, or by a special part), and in the future it should not change on its own. But trimmers are not required to be able to change the value of capacitance or resistance multiple times, and the required range of change is usually smaller. For all variable capacitors, the smoothness of the change in capacitance is important; it is determined by the smoothness of rotation of the screw or axis (for the axis, the quality of the bearings). In a simple school model of friction, when the maximum static friction force is equal to the motion friction force, there are no jumps when moving. In fact, everything is more complicated, and in practice there may be jumps, since the maximum static friction is greater. If you have ever moved something heavy, then you have come across the fact that it is more difficult to move than to drag (there is almost a school problem here - why is it easier to move than to drag). But motion transmission systems in variable capacitors are designed to ensure smooth movement.
