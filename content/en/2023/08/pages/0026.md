MATHEMATICAL CIRCLE

Sages on the graph

K.KOKHAS, A.LATYSHEV

LET'S START BY REMINDING readers of the riddle about hats, which is very well known and has almost become a classic. Mystery. Two wise men sit opposite each other. Each of them is put on a black or white hat so that each does not see his own hat, but sees his partner’s hat. Without communicating or transmitting any signals, they must, on command, simultaneously name the intended color of their hat. The sages were warned in advance about the rules and had the opportunity to consult. How should they act so that at least one of them guesses their color? The answer is surprising and beautiful. It doesn't take a sage to predict that at this show the hats will be either the same color or different. Let the first sage test the first hypothesis, using the strategy of what I see, that’s what I say, and the second - the second: I name the color opposite to the one I see. Obviously, one of them (and only one) will definitely guess. Let us immediately note one subtlety: although one of the wise men will certainly guess, at the moment of guessing they themselves do not know and cannot know who exactly this lucky person will be - after all, both do not have any information about the color of their hat. Not long ago, «Kvant» already talked about a generalized hat guessing game and considered the question of how n wise men should act in a similar test if they are given hats of n (or even more) colors (see the article by K. Kokhas “Bring more hats!” in «Kvant» No. 11 for 2019). In this article we will look at another generalization of this puzzle. Namely, we will solve the following general problem. Task. Then they are subjected to a test similar to that described in the riddle. Each wise man is given a black or white hat so that everyone does not see his own hat, but thanks to optical cables he sees the hats of his neighbors. Without communicating or transmitting any signals, the sages must, on command, simultaneously name the expected color of their hat. Before the test, the sages hold a meeting where they agree on their strategies. The question that interests us is: what is the largest number of wise men who can certainly guess their color?
