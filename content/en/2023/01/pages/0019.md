Problems in mathematics and physics

PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses.

Problem M2730–M2733, F2737–F2740 M2730. On each square of the board 3 and 6 there is one coin. It is known that some two coins lying on adjacent cells are counterfeit, i.e. weigh equally, but are lighter than real ones, and the rest are real. How to find both counterfeit coins in three weighings on a two-pan lever scale without weights? K. Knop

M2731. 2023 natural numbers are written in a row. The first number is 12, and each number, starting from the third, is either equal to the product of the previous two numbers, or equal to the previous number increased by 4. What is the greatest number of perfect squares among the given 2023 numbers? Based on the British Mathematical Olympiad

M2732. Let O be the circumcenter of triangle ABC. Let us plot on the rays AC and BC, respectively, the segments $AC_{a}$ and $BC_{b}$, equal in length to AB (Fig. 1). Let $O_{c}$ be the center of circle $( CC_{a} C_{b} ).$ Similarly, we define points $O_{a}$ and $O_{b}$. Prove that point O is the orthocenter of triangle $O_{a} O_{b} O_{c}$. A. Zaslavsky
