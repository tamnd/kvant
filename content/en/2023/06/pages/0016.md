At the beginning, he ensured that this work was not secret, so that scientists from all countries could jointly solve this most difficult problem, as is now being done in the work on creating ITER (International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor). Everyone who was lucky enough to communicate with Kurchatov remembered him as an active, never-tiring scientist and leader, incredibly friendly to everyone with whom he had to work - from a simple mechanic to ministers and generals. With the same greeting “Fizkult-hello!” he addressed both high-ranking participants in government meetings and colleagues. Even, for example, to us, “green” researchers, fresh from university, when he communicated with us at the nuclear test site near Semipalatinsk. Here are a few touches. Test site, Sunday, break from testing. After breakfast we go to the hotel. And Beard is rapidly coming towards us, almost running (that’s what everyone called Kurchatov in absentia). He is in a hurry to the next meeting, despite it being Sunday morning. “Physical education, hello, boys!” - he throws at us as we go. And we feel embarrassed for our intention to go for a walk on the green floodplains of the Irtysh on a day off. The scientific center of the test site, our workplace where we analyze the results of nuclear tests. Beard enters, accompanied by the generals and our scientific director. At this time, he is concerned about the problem of recognizing nuclear explosions by their seismic effect on the earth's interior - whether it is possible to distinguish this effect from natural earthquakes. And our group is doing just this both in Moscow and at the test site in the period between tests of “RDS products.” Kurchatov tirelessly talks with us for several hours, getting acquainted with the results of our work. And we are amazed at how quickly he penetrates into the very essence of our research, how he instantly evaluates what took us a lot of time. And at the same time, not only does it not “rise” above us, but somehow not-

in a subtle way shows that we already knew his thoughts without him, and he only needs our confirmation. It was this extraordinary kindness towards people, especially young scientists, that other leaders of the Atomic Project perceived from him. Kurchatov passed away on February 7, 1960 after a third stroke. One of the reasons for the tragedy was, of course, the huge integral dose of radiation that he received in his contact with uranium, plutonium and the radioactive products of their decay. However, at that time they still did not know how to properly determine the biological doses of the radiation received. Another reason was his disagreement with the country's leadership over increasing the energy of hydrogen bombs during testing at the Novaya Zemlya nuclear test site. And Kurchatov was in a hurry with research on the peaceful use of the energy of the thermonuclear reaction of fusion of hydrogen nuclei. He called one of the first structures “DOU-3,” which stood for “before the third stroke,” since two strokes had already occurred. Didn't have time. Kurchatov's ashes are buried in the Kremlin wall. Squares and streets in many Russian cities bear his name. Monuments to him were erected not only in his homeland, in the town of Sim, or in Moscow, but also in many other cities of Russia and other countries, near nuclear power plants. The city that arose in the Kazakh steppe near Semipalatinsk, which was once a scientific center and headquarters for nuclear tests, bears the proud name - the city of Kurchatov. Today, the institutes of the Kazakhstan Nuclear Research Center are located there. The city has an interesting museum of the history of nuclear testing at the Semipalatinsk test site, in which the office where Kurchatov worked during the testing periods was recreated with respect and love. There is a city of Kurchatov in Russia - in the Kursk region, where one of the largest nuclear power plants in the country operates. The memory of the scientist who gave his life to the creation of the nuclear shield of the Motherland, which saved the world in those years from the threat of nuclear war, will remain for centuries.
