According to Fuchs, the fissile material for the first American bomb was plutonium-94, which practically does not exist in nature. It can appear as a result of the addition of a neutron to the uranium-238 nucleus and two subsequent beta decays. This can occur in a slow neutron nuclear reactor that uses enriched uranium (where the content of the fissile uranium-235 isotope that produces the required neutron flux is significantly reduced).

Kurchatov had to independently fully develop the entire idea of ​​​​producing plutonium and the principles of operation of a nuclear reactor. And it is no coincidence that it was in our country, in Obninsk near Moscow, that the world’s first atomic (nuclear) power plant began operation - on June 26, 1954, the industrial launch of this power plant took place. Kurchatov congratulated everyone present in the control room of the power plant with a humorous “Have fun, friends!” And seven and a half years before that, on December 26, 1946, on the outskirts of Moscow, on the territory of Laboratory No. 2, an experimental reactor for producing plutonium was launched by his hand. But in order to obtain 20 kg of plutonium for the first bomb, it was necessary to build several grandiose plants. At some plants, natural uranium was purified and enriched, at others, reactors were built to produce plutonium. This, perhaps, became Kurchatov’s main concern at the initial stage of the Atomic Project. An important task was the construction of a special test site for the bomb and recording systems for measuring all types of radiation from a nuclear explosion. When work began in Great Britain in 1946 on creating its own nuclear bomb, 20 specialists, including Klaus Fuchs, arrived from Los Alamos, the American nuclear center.
