In 1908, the family moved to Simbirsk, and in 1912 they moved to Crimea - their daughter, the eldest child in the family, was diagnosed with pulmonary tuberculosis, from which she was never cured in Crimea. Igor is survived by his younger brother Boris (also a future scientist, physicist and radiochemist). The Kurchatovs lived on the outskirts of Simferopol, not far from the gymnasium where both brothers began to study. The building of this gymnasium now houses Simferopol Gymnasium No. 1 named after I.V. Kurchatov. The father traveled around the Crimea, performing land surveying work, and during the holidays he took his sons with him as his assistants. The family's financial situation was difficult, especially after the outbreak of the World War. Igor tried to earn at least something with his head - through tutoring, and with his hands - in a locksmith's workshop. He began to prepare himself for engineering work. The mathematics teacher predicted a great future for him, but the literature teacher also encouraged his humanitarian inclinations. Taurida University, where many remarkable scientists began to teach. The fact is that at that time many scientists, artists, and writers moved to Crimea from the capitals to escape the Moscow and Petrograd hunger and cold. The first rector of the university was the great V.I. Vernadsky, he was succeeded by the prominent chemist academician A.A. Baykov. In 1920, Igor Kurchatov graduated from high school with a gold medal (however, there was no gold for the medal, and he only received an award certificate). He entered the university's Faculty of Physics and Mathematics. Academician A.F. Ioffe and his student I.E. Tamm taught physics there at that time. In three years, Kurchatov completes the entire university course and defends his thesis. But for now he dreams not of physics, but of shipbuilding - he fell in love with ships while visiting the Crimean shores with his father. Kurchatov wants to become a shipbuilder and goes to Petrograd. He is accepted into the third year of the shipbuilding faculty of the Polytechnic Institute. To earn money, he gets a job at a meteorological laboratory in Pavlovsk and here he carries out his first scientific work, “On the radioactivity of snow.” At the same time, he showed a desire to make measurements with the highest possible accuracy and reliability of the results. Was it not this work that determined his future path - to become a nuclear physicist? But this is still a long way off. In the meantime, I have to leave my studies at the institute (a decision was made to ban second higher education). Kurchatov comes to his parents in Crimea, where his former physics professor persuades him to go with him to Baku - as an assistant at the physics department of the Azerbaijan Polytechnic Institute. Since September 1924, Kurchatov has been in Baku. He no longer needs to look for side jobs. An assistant's salary is enough for life, books, and decent clothes.
