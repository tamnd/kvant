From point C, tangents CA and CB are drawn to a circle with center O. Points $D,^{D} c$ are symmetrical with respect to O. From point C, a perpendicular CH is drawn to straight line DO. Circles DAH $и^{D} c BH$ intersect for the second time at point Q. Prove that points C, H, Q lie on the same line. Sasha: Did you manage to draw a drawing? Dima: Yes, but point Q does not fit into the picture (Fig. 1).

Fig. 1

Sasha: Maybe it’s not needed. Let's prove that CH... Dima: Perpendicular to the line of the centers of the blue circles. Or that the centers of the blue circles are equidistant from the green line and lie on one side of it. Sasha: Eh, mark the centers... Let's first erase anything unnecessary from the picture. Dima: Apart from point Q, what is unnecessary here? Sasha: Why, for example, is there a red circle in the picture? Dima: To show that AO VO, and the angles SAO, SVO are right. Sasha: This can be noted without the circle. By the way, aren’t there too many right angles here: SAO, SVO, SNO. This makes me think... Dima: What does it cost to construct a circle with a diameter of CO? Sasha: And at the same time erase point C and all the straight lines coming out of it (Fig. 2).

Dima: Let's remember what we needed in the problem. It is necessary to mark the centers of the blue circles $T и^{T} c$. To construct T, we need perpendicular bisectors. Sasha: Two out of three bisectoral perpendiculars are enough. Which of the three is the odd one out?

Dima: The perpendicular bisector to DH is useful to calculate the distance from T to DH. The perpendicular bisector to AN passes through the center of the I red circle. Sasha: If the centers $T и^{T} c$ are already built (Fig. 3), the blue circles are not needed. Let's erase A and B again.

Dima: If we erase A and B, where will we drop the perpendiculars from I? Sasha: A and B are arbitrary points on the red circle such that AO OB.
