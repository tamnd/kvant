The NOC is 36, 18. This is the smallest number that is divisible by both 36 and 18. In the second problem, the volume of the pool must be mentally divided into 60 equal parts. Here the number 60 is the LCM of 20, 30. School textbooks teach in such problems to take all the work (or the volume of the pool, or the distance) as a unit. But such mathematical modeling of work or volume is unreasonable, since students then have to struggle with ordinary fractions. The work and volume should be divided into a suitable number of parts. This is exactly what physicists do most often. This approach is applicable to tasks related to work, swimming pools, and movement. Problem 3. Two pedestrians left points A and B at the same time towards each other. They met 40 minutes after leaving. 32 minutes after the meeting, the first pedestrian arrived at B. How many minutes after the meeting field did the second pedestrian arrive at A? The first (I) pedestrian covered the NE segment in 32 minutes, the second (II) pedestrian - in 40 minutes (see figure). Let's choose a suitable number to model this NE segment. Such a number is the LCM 40, 32 160. We assume that the segment CB consists of 160 (one hundred and sixty) equal pieces (we use the word piece instead of the word part). Pedestrian speeds are then expressed as integers. The speed of the first is 160: 32 5 pieces per minute, the speed of the second is 160: 40 4 pieces per minute. The distance from A to the meeting place is covered by the first pedestrian in 40 minutes, and it is equal to 5 kus min 40 min 200 kus. Now we find how long it takes the second pedestrian to cover this distance: 200 kus: 4 kus min 50 min. And we solved this problem using only integers. We did without fractions.
