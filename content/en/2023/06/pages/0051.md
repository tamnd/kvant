In pursuit of the beauty of geometry

In school textbooks, geometry is sometimes presented as a long string of theorems, steadily flowing from one another. But from the book of Voldemar Petrovich Smilga, a famous Soviet theoretical physicist and popularizer of science, we learn a completely, completely different story - $настоящую.^{1}$ In Euclid’s Elements, an axiomatic method took shape: the basis of geometry is a set of axioms and postulates; all other facts follow from them. One of Euclid's postulates, the fifth, looked clumsy, and many felt that there was something wrong with it. It seemed that he had gotten into this company by mistake, that he could be proven and removed from the rest. The postulate teased: there was an impression that a little more, an insignificant trifle, and the proof would be in hand.
