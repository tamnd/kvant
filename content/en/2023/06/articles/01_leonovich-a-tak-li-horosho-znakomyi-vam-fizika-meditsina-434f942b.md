PHYSICS + MEDICINE

Unusually intensive progress in medicine is happening before our eyes, increasingly absorbing the latest achievements not only of digital technologies, but also of fundamental physical discoveries. Clinics sometimes resemble interiors from science fiction films about laboratories of the future, striking with the abundance of diagnostic and therapeutic equipment, and the “people in white coats” working with it - the research scientists. Radiography, laser scalpels, proton therapy, ultrasound, MRI... - this list is only growing, only emphasizing the growing role of science in the noble cause of healing, preserving health and prolonging human life. We all had (and still have!) to turn to doctors one way or another. Let's try to look at the area of ​​our interaction with them from the stages of a school physics course.

It's interesting that...

...at least three thousand years ago artificial body parts appeared. Today, the oldest known of these is a prosthetic foot made of wood and leather and discovered on an ancient Egyptian mummy. ... the court physician of the English kings, William Harvey, who discovered blood circulation at the beginning of the 17th century, likened man to a miniature model of the Universe. Blood, like the planets, must move in a circle, in the center of which, like the Sun, is the heart. ... back in the middle of the 18th century, a professor of physics at the Universities of Turin and Paris, Abbot Nollet, described an interesting experience: at the slightest damage to the skin of an electrified person, a stream of tiny droplets of blood began to flow from the wound. It was possible to comprehend this phenomenon only a century and a half later, after the appearance of Lord Rayleigh’s work on the stability of a highly charged drop subject to the action of surface tension forces. ... many famous physicists were doctors by training. For example: one of the creators of the wave theory of light, Thomas Young; Julius Mayer and Hermann Helmholtz who formulated the law of conservation of energy; Jean Foucault, who experimentally proved the rotation of the Earth around its axis...

...the discovery of electron paramagnetic resonance in 1944 (see epigraph) was followed by a chain of discoveries of ever new varieties of magnetic resonances, which made it possible to obtain a three-dimensional image that did not violate the integrity of the object, and became one of the most important diagnostic methods in medicine. ... an artificial pacemaker - an electrical device that helps the heart beat at the desired frequency - was first installed in a patient back in 1958. Today, more than a million pacemakers are implanted in people around the world every year... calcium, the heaviest chemical element found in our bodies, is predominantly located in the bones, making them clearly visible on X-rays. This information is extremely important for astronauts losing calcium in microgravity conditions and for developing special loads on bone tissue for them. ... the successes of low-temperature physics and new technologies created on its basis already make it possible to extend the shelf life of organs intended for transplantation and to freeze reproductive cells. True, the problem of water crystallization, which breaks cellular structures during deeper freezing, cannot yet be solved. ... a “bionic ear” was recently invented, containing an antenna tuned to radio waves combined with living cells. Such a device not only expands the hearing range, but also allows, for example, remote monitoring of the condition of prostheses.
