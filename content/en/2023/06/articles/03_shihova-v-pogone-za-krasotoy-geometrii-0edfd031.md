In pursuit of the beauty of geometry

In school textbooks, geometry is sometimes presented as a long string of theorems, steadily flowing from one another. But from the book of Voldemar Petrovich Smilga, a famous Soviet theoretical physicist and popularizer of science, we learn a completely, completely different story - $настоящую.^{1}$ In Euclid’s Elements, an axiomatic method took shape: the basis of geometry is a set of axioms and postulates; all other facts follow from them. One of Euclid's postulates, the fifth, looked clumsy, and many felt that there was something wrong with it. It seemed that he had gotten into this company by mistake, that he could be proven and removed from the rest. The postulate teased: there was an impression that a little more, an insignificant trifle, and the proof would be in hand.

Euclidean geometry reigned in mathematics for more than two thousand years, but now we know: there are many geometries. And the point is not even that there are many of them - they form a community with complex relationships. The book by Alexey Bronislavovich Sosinsky, a recognized popularizer of mathematics, is exactly about how this community is structured $геометрий.^{2}$ In this book, geometry is a special mathematical object, which is a set of elements with groups of transformations acting on them. This approach was formulated by Felix Klein in the Erlangen Program. In this sense, our school, Euclidean geometry is a plane (or space), and transformations are symmetries, rotations, parallel translations. The author introduces readers to various geometries, discusses their structure, but not only that. It shows how different geometries relate to each other, what their relationship is and how close it is. A novice mathematician will also see the influence of algebra on geometry: how the language of morphisms allows us to look at all geometries from a bird's eye view. And at the same time, he will get a taste for the geometric approach to working with algebraic objects. As befits an educational book, it all starts with very simple, toy examples in which you can get familiar with the basic concepts on your fingers, play with them and get used to them. After this, more formal definitions are introduced, and they are immediately filled with content thanks to the studied examples.
