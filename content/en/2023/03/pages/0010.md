Together with members of the Berkeley Laboratory, Seaborg identified more than 100 isotopes of fourteen transuranium elements, developing sophisticated methods for studying the properties of these isotopes. He won the 1951 Nobel Prize in Chemistry (shared with Edwin Macmillan). And therefore, element No. 106 was deservedly given the name seaborgium (for the first time during the life of the scientist-discoverer). The production of new elements began with plutonium atoms bombarded with alpha particles accelerated in cyclotrons. This is how element No. 95 (americium) was obtained. Its bombardment with alpha particles yielded the next element - No. 96 (curium). Then berkelium (No. 97) and californium (No. 98) were synthesized. These 4 elements are widely used in scientific research and in a wide variety of fields (from military affairs to medicine). Today they are obtained in reactions with neutrons in nuclear reactors specially built for this purpose. During long-term operation of reactors, neutron fluxes gradually, one after another, transform plutonium into americium, americium into curium, curium into berkelium, and berkelium into californium. The main difficulty and enormous amount of work is associated with the extraction of new elements from their mixture with uranium fission products in the spent nuclear fuel of the reactor. It is clear that the cost of new isotopes is very high. For example, one gram of Californian costs 60 million dollars. This is the most expensive metal in the world. For comparison, a gram of gold (depending on the market) has a price of 40–50 dollars. The following elements, #99 (einsteinium), #100 (fermium), #101 (mendelevium), #102 (nobelium) and #103 (lawrencium), are characterized by the fact that they do not have long-lived isotopes. Maximum half-lives do not exceed a few hours, and most isotopes have millisecond half-lives. This greatly complicated the study of the chemical properties of isotopes and their spectra. By

Interestingly, einsteinium and fermium were first discovered in sediments after the first American prototype hydrogen bomb device was tested in 1952 at Eniwetak Atoll in the Pacific Ocean. And only 10 years later, an insignificant amount of einsteinium was obtained at the Berkeley synchrotron. The second group of transuranium elements, from No. 104 (rutherfordium) to No. 112 (copernicium), is characterized by the fact that they were synthesized not on the American continent, but in Europe - at the Heavy Ion Institute in Darmstadt and at the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research in Dubna, Moscow Region. The idea of ​​obtaining all these elements was born at the Dubna Institute (G.N. Flerov and Yu.Ts. Oganesyan). This is the collision of the nuclei of elements at the end of the periodic table with the nuclei of the middle of the periodic system, namely bismuth and chromium, lead and iron, lead and nickel. It is important that the nucleus of one of these atoms be especially strong and have a high nucleon binding energy, which follows from the theoretical shell model of atomic nuclei.
