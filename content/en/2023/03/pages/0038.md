Mathematical Olympiad for schoolchildren. The annotation to the collection, in particular, said: “The tasks of the Olympiad, in addition to a solid knowledge of the school mathematics course, require ingenuity and ingenuity. Therefore, to prepare for the Olympiad, this collection is being published. Many of the problems presented in the collection were proposed at previous olympiads and evening mathematics school competitions. The collection presents both relatively easy and more difficult problems. The collection also contains short notes on problems (as a rule, not exhaustive solutions, but only indications of the main idea).” Let us turn to one of the tasks of this collection. Problem 3. Given half a glass of water and half a glass of wine. A spoonful of water was poured into the wine, and then a spoonful of the resulting mixture was poured into the water. Which ended up being more: water in the wine or wine in the water? A little further we read the explanation: “The same amount of wine was added to the first glass as water was poured from it.” Let's go even further in time. $В^{1946}$ in the sixth issue of the News of the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences the article “Principles of selection and composition of arithmetic problems” was published. Its author is I.V. Arnold. Here we find such a version of the problem of a mixture of two liquids. Problem 4. One spoon was poured from a glass of red wine into a glass containing the same amount of white wine. After stirring, a spoonful of the mixture was poured back into the first glass. The question is, is the concentration of white wine in red in the first glass greater, or red in white in the second? To solve the problem, it is enough to ask yourself the question: where did that red wine go that was replaced by white from the first glass? This question directs us to consider the corresponding figure. And figure encourages such answers to the last two problems. $Задача^{3:}$ there is as much wine in the water in the first glass as there is water in the wine in the second glass. Task 4:

There is as much white wine in red as there is red in white. $В^{2008}$ I.V. Arnold’s article was published in the form of a separate book (M.: MTsNMO). If you read this book carefully, you can find out what happens to volume when mixed: “A mixture of two equal volumes of water and alcohol occupies a smaller volume than the sum of the volumes of both substances being mixed. A liter of such a mixture has a mass of 936 g, while a liter of pure alcohol has a mass of 729 g, and a liter of water $–^{1}$ kg. How much water and alcohol do you need to take to get a liter of mixture? So, both transfusion problems are incorrect, and figure leads to incorrect answers. The conditions of the problems say nothing about how the volume changes when mixed, and in fact, the volume of a mixture of two solutions is not equal to the sum of the volumes of the original solutions.
