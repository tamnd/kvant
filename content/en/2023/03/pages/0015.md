TRANSURANAN

One of the Dubna accelerators' work was then considered top secret. And a month earlier in Moscow, representatives of 11 countries signed an Agreement on the establishment of JINR with the aim of “combining their scientific and nuclear potential to study the fundamental properties of matter.” This institute arose on the basis of the Institute of Nuclear Physics of the USSR Academy of Sciences, located in the town of Dubna near Moscow, near the Volga, flowing in this place from the Moscow Sea (Ivankovo ​​Reservoir). There was already an accelerator there and, under the leadership of V.I. Veksler, work was underway to create a 10 GeV proton synchrophasotron, then the largest in the world. JINR, as a collaboration of researchers from many countries, was created by analogy with the emerging European Center (CERN) for countries not included in it. The financial contribution of the USSR was 50%, the PRC 20%. The remaining 30% came from the other 9 founding countries - Czechoslovakia, Poland, Romania, Bulgaria, Greece, Vietnam, Egypt, Cuba and Mongolia. The first director of the institute was Professor D.I. Blokhintsev, who created the Physics and Power Engineering Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences and the world's first nuclear power plant in Obninsk near Moscow. In the very first years of JINR’s work, progress was made in understanding the quark structure of hadrons, chromodynamics (“color” of quarks), neutrino

cillations, etc. This was the cutting edge of microworld physics. A laboratory of nuclear reactions was created under the leadership of Academician G.N. Flerov, and then Academician Yu.Ts. Oganesyan. Already in 1957, success was achieved in this laboratory - one of the isotopes of Nobelium, the 102nd element, was synthesized. Over the past 60 years, the laboratory has discovered over 200 radioactive isotopes of transuranium elements, and created unique installations to determine their physical and chemical characteristics. The theoretical developments of laboratory scientists have received worldwide recognition. Today JINR is a complex of 7 large laboratories employing more than 1,200 scientists, including full members and corresponding members of the National Academies of Sciences, doctors and candidates of science, about 2,000 technicians and engineers. Experimental base - primarily 5 research reactors and 3 accelerators. Construction of the world's only ion accelerator collider is nearing completion. Today (as of January 1, 2023) 13 states finance JINR and their scientists work there. These are Azerbaijan, Armenia, Belarus, Vietnam, Greece, Egypt, Kazakhstan, Cuba, Moldova, Mongolia, the Russian Federation, Slovakia and Uzbekistan. Two years ago, a Russian physicist, 45-year-old academician Grigory Vladimirovich Trubnikov, was elected director of the institute. Dubna has long received the status of a science city. The high scientific potential that JINR possesses and its experimental base allow us to hope for further revelation of the secrets of the atomic nucleus. And the synthesis of transuranium elements, which do not exist in nature, is evidence of the power of human thought, which is not afraid of daring interference in the laws of nature that govern our world.
