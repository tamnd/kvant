We call a polygon whose area (measured in $см^{2} )$ is numerically equal to the perimeter (measured in cm) good. Note that the original triangle is good: it is rectangular with legs 5 cm and 12 cm, so its area is equal to $30 см^{2}$ and numerically coincides with its perimeter, equal to 5 12 13 30 cm. If some polygon 3 is divided into good polygons, then the area 3, equal to the sum of the areas of all the polygons of the partition, would coincide numerically with the sum of the perimeters of the polygons of the partition. But the sum of these perimeters is greater than the perimeter 3 (by twice the sum of the lengths of the common parts of the boundaries of the polygons of the partition).
