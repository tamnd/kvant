Often, when you first look at a drawing, several reasonable ideas arise for constructing an auxiliary triangle. It is curious, however, that almost always each of the methods that come to mind allows (with varying degrees of complexity) to come to the answer. This is true, for example, for the following several problems. Problem 3. Inside triangle ABC there is a point M such that AM BM, MBA 10 q, MBC 20 q and MAC 40 q. Find angle MCA. Solution. So, what kind of triangle might be useful here (Fig. 3)? On the one hand, if we construct an equilateral triangle ABK with base AB so that points M and C are inside the triangle, then BC will turn out to be a bisector, and CAK MAB MBA 10 q. You can also use equal sides AM and BM by constructing equilateral triangles AMN or BML. In each case, in addition to sides equal to AM and MB, angles equal to those given in the condition also appear.
