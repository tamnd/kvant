Problem 1. Along a pipe Two light, small elastic balls move inside a smooth massive pipe, closed at both ends, located horizontally. At the moment of time when the balls are in the middle of the pipe, their speeds relative to the ground are equal to v and 2v, and the pipe begins to move at a constant speed of 0.1v, as shown in figure 4. The length of the pipe is 2L.

1) After what period of time $W_{1}$ will the left ball collide with the end wall of the pipe for the first time? 2) After what period of time $W_{2}$ will the right ball collide with the end wall of the pipe for the first time? 3) After what period of time W will the balls collide with each other for the first time? 4) Find the velocities of the $u_{1}$ left and $u_{2}$ right balls relative to the ground just before their first impact. Task 3. Back and forth Experimenter Gluck conducted an experiment. Gluck set a ten-liter pan half filled with water at room temperature $t_{0} 20 q C$ to warm up on an electric stove. After some time, he added an unknown volume of water at room temperature to the pan without removing it from the stove. And after some more time, he poured the same volume of water from the pan, also without removing it from the stove. Then he measured the temperature of the water in the pan twice - after $W_{1} 8$ minutes and $W_{2} 9$ minutes from the start of heating and obtained values ​​$t_{1} 45 q C$ and $t_{2} 50 q C$. In total, the tile worked for 10 minutes. 1) What temperature $t_{к}$ would the water in the pan have at the end of the experiment if its mass did not change during heating? 2) Determine the smallest possible value of the mass $m_{min}$ of water added by Gluck during the experiment. 3) Find the earliest time point $W_{min}$ from the start of heating when water could be drawn from the pan. Heat losses and heat capacity of the pan are negligible. Consider that when you change the mass of water, its temperature changes instantly, and when you add water, it does not pour out of the pan.
