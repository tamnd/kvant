THE ABILITY TO DO ADDITIONAL constructions has always been the key to successfully solving complex geometric problems. It is no secret that it is very difficult to formulate precise and clear instructions for selecting and applying this or that construction, but often the problem statement contains “beacons” - hints that allow you to guess which additional lines should be drawn. Thus, practice shows that it can be useful to extend the median of a triangle by its length, and special addition of the midpoints of the segments and subsequent consideration of the midlines of the corresponding triangles help solve the problem. Such typical additional constructions can also safely include the method of “sum rectification”, standard constructions in a trapezoid, the auxiliary circle method, etc. A more complex technique that requires more ingenuity and appears in Olympiad problems is the search and construction of regular polygons or their elements. Interested readers will see this idea, for example, in the article by E. Bakaev and A. Blinkov “Auxiliary squares” («Kvant» No. 4, 2016).
