Light reflection of a dark spot

Frosty day. It’s cold not only outside, but also in the entrances of buildings. In the pavilion at the entrance to the metro, all the glass is covered with thick frost. This frost can be melted with the touch of your hand, and then an amazing picture appears: the melted “window” in the frost looks like a dark spot on a white background. And in the marble window sill, shining like a mirror, it is reflected... like a light spot on a dark background! Not just a reflection, but a “complementary reflection” - replacing a negative image with a positive one. This effect is the result of the interplay between the transmission and reflection of light, the properties of the surfaces on which it falls, and the location of the light sources. The polished surface of marble works like a normal mirror: through a more transparent “window” in the frost, more light falls on it than through the matte frosted surface of the glass. Therefore, the figure of the palm is reflected in it as a lighter spot. This light falls from above, from the side of the bright sky. But when we look directly at the glass, our gaze is directed through this “window”, rather downwards, to the ground or to some buildings surrounding the entrance to the metro. But for frosty frosted areas of glass, this is not a problem: no matter from which direction the light comes to them, they will still scatter it in all directions, including ours. Therefore, these areas appear lighter than a transparent palm print. The fact that this is exactly the case can be easily seen by looking at the photograph, where several handprints are simultaneously present.

It can be seen that those that are located higher and projected onto the sky (or at least closer to it) are lighter. It would seem that such simple optical phenomena, but what an unusual and beautiful figure they create! V. Ptushenko
