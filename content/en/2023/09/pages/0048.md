Problem 4. Uranium ball A uranium ball of thermal conductivity k and radius R, placed in a stream of water at a temperature of $T_{0}$, is irradiated by a stream of neutrons so that, as a result of the nuclear fission reaction, volumetric power q is released. Find the dependence of the established temperature T of the ball on the distance r to its center. A. Chudnovsky

Problem 5. Gas tee A small symmetrical tee connects three identical vessels with rarefied helium. All tubes of the tee are symmetrically filled with porous material, penetrated by many parallel channels, the diameter of which is much less than the free path of the molecules. In the center of the tee there is a small space free of porous material. The entry of a molecule into a channel from any side means that the molecule has a certain probability of escaping from this channel back through the same hole and an additional probability of escaping through the opposite hole of this channel, and these probabilities do not depend on temperature and are the same for all channels. Initially, the helium in all three vessels had a pressure of $p_{0}$ and some of the same temperature. Then, maintaining the same temperature in the first vessel and inside the tee, they increased the temperature in the second vessel twofold, and in the third - threefold. What pressures $p_{1}, p_{2}, p_{3}$ and $p_{4}$ will be established, respectively, in three vessels and in the free space inside the tee after a long time? S.Varlamov

Problem 7. Flow spreading A uniform flow of charged particles with a total current strength $I_{0}$ falls on a round plate of small thickness h and made of a material with resistivity U. The cylindrical boundary of the plate is grounded and has zero potential. Find the potential $M_{0}$ at the center of the plate and the thermal power $P_{0}$ released in the plate. M. Ermilov

Problem 8. Mercury mirror of a telescope The famous American experimental physicist Robert Wood built an original reflecting telescope in 1908. At the bottom of a well in one of the cowsheds, he installed a dish of mercury rotating around a vertical axis as a telescope mirror and observed the starry sky through a hole in the roof. Using such a mirror in one of his experiments, he obtained an image of the Moon on a photographic plate with a diameter of d 3 5 cm. At what angular velocity Z did the dish with mercury rotate in this experiment? The lunar disk is visible from Earth at an angle of J 0 5 q. Gravity acceleration g 9 8 m $с^{2}$. V.Efimov
