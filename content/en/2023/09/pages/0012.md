Kirchhoff died in Berlin on October 17, 1887. His memory is preserved in the names of streets in Berlin, Heidelberg and Bad Dürknheim, as well as in the names of a lunar crater and an asteroid. COMPETITION NAMED AFTER A.P. SAVINA

We are starting another competition for solving mathematical problems. The tasks are designed mainly for students starting from grades 8–9, and we advise younger students to try their hand at the magazine competition «Kvant» (see website kvantik.com). Send solutions to problems you can solve to: savin.contest@gmail.com. In addition to your first and last name, indicate the city, school and class in which you study. We welcome participation in the competition not only by individual schoolchildren, but also by teams (in this case, one work is sent with a list of participants). You can participate starting from any tour. The winners will receive diplomas from the magazine «Kvant» and prizes. Assignments, solutions and results are published on sites.google.com/view/savin-contest We wish you success!

1. What is the sum of the smallest and largest angles of one triangle? Provide many possible values.

E. Bakaev

2. Felonius Gru has 33 minions, all of them weigh the same. One day one of them stole Gru's banana and ate it, but Gru doesn't know who did it. He has a large scale without weights that he can use to weigh any number of minions. However, if minions end up on the same side of the scale, they quarrel and cannot be placed on more than one side at a time. How can Gru find a thief in four weighings if after eating a banana he weighs more than the rest?

A. Gribalko

3. The mason laid out a wall without holes and cavities from identical bricks 1 u 1 u 2. But he laid some bricks along, some across, some vertically, i.e. the long edge of a brick parallel to one of three directions. Could it turn out that there are equal numbers of bricks of each of the three types if the wall dimensions are: a) 3 u 8 u 10; b) 3 u 9 u 10?

M. Evdokimov

4. The table 100 u 100 is filled with integers. For each pair 1 d i d 100, 1 d j d 100 there is a rectangle i u j with a zero sum of numbers. What is the smallest number of zeros that can be in a table?

E. Bakaev
