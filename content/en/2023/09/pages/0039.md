more convenient for home conditions using laser-iron technology. First of all, we designed a stencil for the future printed circuit board in the Altium Designer computer-aided design system. Next, the resulting stencil drawing of our board was printed on glossy paper from a magazine using a laser printer (hence the word “laser” in the name of the method). The cast was transferred figure from the stencil using an iron. Preparation of fiberglass laminate consisted of cleaning the surface with an abrasive and subsequent degreasing. This was followed by transferring the toner from the stencil to the surface of the workpiece, after which the paper was removed with water and a cotton pad. The etching process took place in a solution of hydrogen peroxide and citric acid with the addition of table salt, which is a catalyst in this reaction. The process took about 30–40 minutes. As a result, all the excess copper was etched away, and what was under figure remained untouched. The result is conductive figure, which is the circuit. Further installation of the elements turned out to be a real pleasure. After we were convinced that the circuit was working, it was time to create the case. We decided to make it black and chose a square shape. The materials used were wood and plastic, so the insides of the pedal had to be shielded. To do this, we covered the inside of the case with aluminum adhesive tape. To avoid interference and the appearance of parasitic capacitance, individual pieces of tape were connected to each other with a conductor (a drop of solder). This is how our beloved Fusik-2000 (Fig.6) was created.
