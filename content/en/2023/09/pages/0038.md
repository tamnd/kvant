KVANT 2023/№9

This article will tell you how we made a real DIY guitar effect pedal. We chose the distortion effect $(англ.^{distortion}$ – distortion). This effect changes the appearance of the original signal, or rather its shape (Fig.2). The original signal, also known as the original one, is a regular sine wave. Next comes clipping, i.e. distortion of the original signal. The amplitude of the processed signal exceeds the permissible voltage value. The sine wave now seems to not fit into the window. The signal is distorted, the set of frequencies increases. The sound is more aggressive. There are two types of clipping: hard clipping - the result of the distortion effect, and soft - the result of the overdrive effect $(англ.^{overdrive}$ - overdrive).

⟦column⟧

The creation of our pedal began with the search for a suitable circuit. On the Internet, we found instructions for creating a Fuzz effect pedal (Fig.3). The first board had to be rejected because one of the transistors turned out to be faulty, and we also figured out how to significantly simplify the circuit by using a microcircuit instead of a transistor stage. The second attempt was successful, the design worked and we were able to hear a warm, crunchy overdrive (clipped signal). But now we were not satisfied with the appearance of the circuit (Fig.4), and we decided to transfer it from the cardboard to a real circuit board (printed circuit board).
