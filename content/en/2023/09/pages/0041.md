OLYMPICS

11 (grades 6–8). The cells of the rectangular table m u n are painted in three colors: blue, red, green. In one move, you can select three cells that form a corner, and at the same time recolor them as follows: blue cells - red, red - green, green - blue. For what m and n can the table be made one-color, regardless of the initial coloring? O. Mangina

12 (grades 6–8). a) There are 54 angles, which are two segments of length 1, connected at the ends at right angles. From these they assembled the frame of a cube with an edge of length 3, each face of which is divided into unit squares. Prove that if each corner is located entirely on one of the faces of the cube, then on some two faces the corners are equally divided. b) The same problem for 96 corners and a cube with an edge of length 4. A. Gribalko

13 (8th grade). On the checkered field 111 u 111 there is an invisible ship 11 u 11. You are allowed to shoot at any square. If the shot hits the ship, it is considered destroyed and the game ends. If the shot was inaccurate, then after it the ship moves one cell horizontally or vertically (within

boards). Is it possible to guarantee the destruction of a ship in a few shots? A. Gribalko

14 (8th grade). An excircle centered at $I_{a}$ is tangent to leg AB of right triangle ABC at $A_{0}$. An excircle with center at point $I_{c}$ is tangent to leg CB at point C 0. Lines $I_{a} A 0$ and $I_{c} C 0$ intersect the hypotenuse AC at points $A_{1}$ and $C_{1}$, and each other at point P. Prove that the midpoint of AC lies on the excircle of triangle $A_{1} PC_{1}$. D. Shvetsov

15 (8th grade). Inside the equilateral triangle, we marked 2022 points and wrote down all the distances from these points to the sides of the triangle. Can the written numbers form an arithmetic progression in some order? A. Gribalko

16 (grades 6–8). There are 20 seemingly indistinguishable coins lying in a circle on the table. It is known that exactly two of them are fake. Real coins weigh the same, fake coins too, but they are lighter than real ones. How can you determine after three weighings on a cup scale without weights whether there are counterfeit coins nearby or not? A. Gribalko, K. Knop, N. Chernyatiev

Refrigerator In a thin-walled cylindrical vessel with an internal radius R, under the piston there is a column of water with a height of $h_{1}$ at a temperature of $t_{0} 0 q C$. The piston and cylinder bottom are thermally insulated. The power of heat loss through the side walls is proportional to the contact area and the temperature difference between the contents of the vessel and the environment: N D S ' t, where D is a known constant. The cylinder is placed vertically in a refrigerator, the initial temperature of which is $t_{1} 0 q C$. Next, the temperature in the refrigerator is changed so that the piston of the cylinder moves at a constant speed. Ice formed in the cylinder does not interfere with the free movement of the piston. Moreover, the ice does not float above the surface of the water, but rests against the pores.
