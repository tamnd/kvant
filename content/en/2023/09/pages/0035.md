...according to legend, in ancient times, near the ancient Egyptian city of Thebes, every morning at dawn, a huge statue, known as the Colossus of Memnon, sounded the tone of A of the first octave, and musicians came to it to tune their instruments. ...Pythagoras experimentally discovered the connection between the height of a musical tone and the length of the string or flute that produces it, and that the harmony of the joint sound is determined by a simple rational ratio of the lengths of these instruments. This is how Pythagoras discovered the first law of mathematical physics. ...the staff of music, whose invention at the beginning of the 11th century belongs to the Italian monk Guido d'Arezzo, is the first graph in history, since it shows the change in pitch vertically, and the passage of time horizontally from left to right. ...the most significant contribution to the solution of the problem of a vibrating string was made by Leonhard Euler, starting with his work $^{1739}$ “An Attempt in a New Theory of Music”, in which he gave a precise meaning to the problem of the musical scale. ...Beethoven, deaf, listened to the piano playing, holding one end of his cane to it, the other end of which he held to his teeth. ...Albert Einstein loved to play not only the violin, but also performed works by Bach, Vivaldi and Mozart on the piano. According to contemporaries, he easily tolerated negative assessments of his scientific theories, but was very offended if someone criticized his style of playing the violin. ...the first domestic electro-musical instrument - the theremin - was designed in $в^{1920}$ by the Russian physicist Lev Theremin. From mechanical instruments - barrel organs and musical clocks - through gramophones, tape recorders - to digital audio devices.
