And Kirchhoff, after many experiments, creates a remarkably simple model of a laboratory absolutely black body - a cavity (cave) with a very small hole. The light entering the cavity through this hole will practically not come back out, but will be reflected repeatedly from the walls of the cavity, “throw around” inside and gradually be absorbed by these walls. This means that the absorption capacity of such a device is equal to unity for all wavelengths and temperatures. And if the device is heated, then the thermal radiation of the internal walls of the cavity in a small fraction will necessarily come out of the hole. Its energy characteristic r O, T will be quite close to the emissivity of an absolutely black body.
