If there are at least two fewer figures of any other shape, then each prisoner sees which figure is the main one. If everyone draws it, among them there will be those who guessed correctly. Let this not be the case and, for example, the main figure is a square, the next in number is a circle, and there are exactly one fewer circles than squares. Then the prisoners with squares on their foreheads will see the same number of circles and squares and will not be able to determine for sure the main figure (the rest, as before, will see which figure is the main one and can draw it). There are several saving algorithms for this case. The first is to name the closest one clockwise of the two candidates for the main figures. Let's prove that at least someone can guess. We will only take into account squares and circles, ignoring the rest of the prisoners. Can each square be followed clockwise by a circle? No, because there are more squares. The prisoner with a square followed by a clockwise square will guess correctly and save everyone. The opposite algorithm also works: of the two candidates for the main figures, name the one that is closest in a clockwise direction. To prove this, we will also consider only circles and squares. In at least one place, the prisoner with the square is followed clockwise by the prisoner with the circle. This captive with a square will draw his figure correctly.
