OLYMPICS

Selected problems of the XXVIII A.P. Savin Tournament

1 (grades 7–8). 28 students were lined up in a circle and randomly given numbers from 1 to 28. Each answered “Yes” or “No” to the question “Is your number divisible by the number of your neighbor on the left?” There were more correct answers than incorrect ones. What is the largest number of students who could answer “Yes”? A. Shapovalov

2 (grades 5–8). What is the smallest number of knights that can be placed on a chessboard so that one of them can be moved to any free cell in no more than two moves? M. Evdokimov

3 (grades 7–8). The Kid had 100 axles on a stand, each with 20 rings. At the same time, 50 axles had only white rings, and another 50 had only black rings. Carlson came and mixed everything in random order. Prove that the Kid can restore order using an additional axis if you can only move rings one at a time between the axes, and you can put a maximum of 20 rings on each axis. V. Novikov

Let's call a hedgehog a figure consisting of seven cubes (the central one and the six adjacent to it). It turned out that each hedgehog contains more white cubes than black ones. Prove that in some cube 2 u 2 u 2 there are more white cubes than black ones. M. Evdokimov

7 (grades 6–8). There are an odd number of houses in the forest, some of them connected by paths. There is a gnome in every house. Dwarves are considered neighbors if their houses are connected by a path. One day the dwarves made exchanges and moved to other houses. At the same time, it turned out that again a gnome lives in every house, and now gnomes are neighbors if and only if they were not neighbors before. Prove that exactly one gnome has not changed his place of residence. A. Gribalko

8 (grades 7–8). A regular hexagon is cut into more than 100 equilateral hexagons, each colored white or black. Is it true that there are two hexagons bordering on a side or part of a side that are colored the same color? M. Evdokimov

4 (grades 7–8). Given a right triangle ABC. Let Z be a circle passing through vertex C and touching the hypotenuse AB at point B. Prove that the distance from point A to circle Z is greater than the distance from point C to line AB. I. Kukharchuk, A. Ershov

9 (6th grade). During the year, the students of the class went to N competitions. Each two participated in a different number of Olympiads, while each of them was at the Olympiad, which the other was not. What is the largest number of students in this class? V. Bufeev, O. Mangina

5 (grades 5–6). In two rooms there were several people, each of whom was either a knight or a knave. From time to time one of them moved from one room to another and, already in the room, said: “Now there are more liars in this room than knights.” Could it be that each person made exactly one transition from room to room? N. Chernyatyev

10 (grades 7–8). The numbers from 0 to 9 are encrypted in some order with the letters from A to J. In one question you are allowed to name several different letters and find out the encrypted record of their sum. For example, if A 5, B 6, C 1, then by calling A and B, you can find out that their sum is equal to CC. Sasha assumes that the numbers are encrypted in order, i.e. A 0, B 1, etc. Can he, by asking three questions, confirm or refute his hypothesis? A. Gribalko, A. Zaslavsky

6 (grades 5–7). A 4 u 4 u 4 cube is made from 64 white and black unit cubes.
