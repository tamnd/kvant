PHYSICS + ARCHITECTURE

When it comes to architecture, as a rule, what first comes to mind is a whole series of harmonious, classical, one might say, souvenir images - palaces, estates, cathedrals. But this is just one, aesthetic side of such a diverse area of ​​human activity as architecture. Another, perhaps not so sublime, earthly side of it is the embodiment of the architects’ plans, which requires thorough engineering and technical training, knowledge of statics and acoustics, geodesy and hydraulics, materials science and lighting engineering. What a unique profession this is - an architect! And in order to master it, you will have to learn not only to draw and draw, but also to develop spatial imagination - and this is geometry, to be able to calculate - and this is algebra and mathematical analysis, and to solve physical problems - where would we be without it! – tasks. There are quite a few of them scattered throughout all sections of the school physics course; all that is required is proper sampling.
