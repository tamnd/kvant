Donald Knuth is known to many as the author of the multi-volume “The Art of Programming” and the creator of the TECH system for layout of mathematical texts. Half a century ago, over dinner, Conway told Knuth about surreal numbers. This set includes not only the real numbers we are familiar with, but also many others, which can conventionally be called “infinities.” The operations of addition, multiplication, root extraction are defined for them (how would you determine the root of an infinite power of infinity?) ... Knuth only had an idea, which Conway sketched on a napkin. Knut wanted to develop it himself and write a book about it - a book that would open the kitchen of mathematical $творчества.^{2}$ In the story, a young man and a girl on a desert island found a tablet with axioms: “In the beginning the earth was empty and formless, and J.H. Conway began to create numbers. Conway said: "Let there be two rules which give birth to all numbers, great and small. Let there be a first rule: To each number there corresponds two sets of previously created numbers, so that in the left set there is no element greater than or equal to any element in the right set. Let there be a second rule: One number is less than or equal to another if and only if in the left set of the first number there is no element greater than or equal to the second number, and in the right set of the second number there is no element less than or equal to the second number." equal to the first number.” And Conway tested these two rules that he came up with, and they were good." The heroes of the book understand axioms, try to deduce consequences from them, make mistakes, think about them and draw conclusions, build hypotheses and study them... - there is a living process of mathematical research.
