At the turn of 2000, L’Huillier managed to break the femtosecond barrier for the first time. Her research group obtained and reliably measured a pulse with a duration of 0.67 femtometers, which became known as 670 attometers. And in 2001, two groups of researchers simultaneously obtained attosecond pulses. The French-American group of Pierre Agostini received a series of pulses with a duration of 250 attoseconds each. But the main application of femtosecond and attosecond pulses is the study of electron dynamics in chemical reactions and the structure of intermediate complex states of molecules. Today the question is being raised about the possibility of such an electronic or photon influence on this intermediate complex so that its collapse occurs in a certain direction. In other words, the possibility arises of controlling the deep essence of atomic and molecular processes, of interfering in the “intimate” atomic-molecular electronic world, which nature has made purely probabilistic and not precisely defined. And this does not contradict the quantum principle about the impossibility of accurately observing objects in the microworld without destroying the structure in which the objects are located. In all femto- and attosecond impacts, the systems are transformed - an electron flies out of the atom, molecules change their structure.
