Qualitatively, the dependence of the phase transition temperature (as the transition of a substance from one state - solid liquid or gaseous - to another) on pressure can be understood based on one of the most general laws of nature - the Le Chatelier-Brown principle. Somewhat simplified, it can be formulated as follows: Any external influence causes changes in the system that reduce the effect of this influence. So, if saturated vapors are in equilibrium with a liquid, then as the volume occupied by the system decreases, the vapor pressure will begin to increase. The density of liquid is greater than the density of vapor. If some of the vapor condenses, the density, and therefore the pressure of the remaining vapor, will decrease. The effect of external influence is partially (and if the temperature is maintained constant, then completely) compensated. Consequently, this compensating process - vapor condensation - will begin immediately. To prevent condensation, i.e. In order to still increase the vapor pressure, obviously, it is necessary to increase the temperature of the system. Thus, relying (except on the Le Chatelier-Brown principle) only on the fact that the density of the liquid is greater than the density of the vapor, we came to the conclusion that a higher temperature corresponds to a higher saturated vapor pressure.
