Problems in mathematics and physics

PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2754 – M2756 was offered at the VIII Caucasian Mathematical Olympiad. The author of problems F2761–F2764 is S. Varlamov.

Problem M2754–M2757, F2761–F2764 M2754. Nazar chose two real numbers a and b and wrote two equations: $x^{4} 2 b^{3} x a^{4} 0$ and $x^{4} 2 a^{3} x b^{4} 0$. Prove that at least one of these equations has a real root. N. Agakhanov

M2755. Pasha and Vova play the game, taking turns crossing out cells on the board 3 u 101. Initially, only the central cell is crossed out on the board. In one move, the player must choose a diagonal (a diagonal can have 1, 2 or 3 cells) and cross out all the uncrossed cells in it. Each move must cross out at least one new cell. The one who cannot make a move loses. Pasha begins. Which player can win regardless of the opponent's moves? I. Efremov

M2757. Given a prime number p. There are p integers around the circle. In one move, you are allowed to choose some integer k and replace the existing set $a_{0},^{…}, a_{p} 1$ with a set of numbers $a_{0} a_{k}, a_{1} a_{1} k,^{…}, a_{p} 1 a_{p} 1_{k}$ (indices are taken modulo p, i.e. a i a i p ). a) Prove that after performing p such moves, all numbers on the circle will be multiples of p. b*) Find all pairs of natural numbers m, n, n! 1, for which the following statement is true: for any initial set of p integers, after performing any m of the described moves, a set of p numbers that are multiples of n will be obtained. P. Kozhevnikov

F2761. Maxwell's pendulum is a structure consisting of a U-shaped rigidly attached to the base of a tripod, two rigidly connected coaxial cylinders of different diameters and different lengths, as well as two threads of the same length, with one end of each thread fixed to a cylinder of a smaller diameter, and the other to a horizontal crossbar of the tripod (Fig. 1). Both cylinders are made of the same homogeneous material with density U 10 g $см^{3}$. The ratio of the diameters of the cylinders is $D_{1} D_{2} 10$, the ratio of their lengths (along the axis of symmetry) is $l_{2} l_{1} 3$. The length of each straightened thread is $10,5 D_{1}$.
