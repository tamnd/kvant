“Wait a little,” the elder answered and divided everything in two. – I think I divided it properly. Do you agree? - Of course not. These are completely unequal parts! - So be it. And which part do you think is larger? - This one! – the younger robber pointed out without hesitation. - Wonderful! Take her. Are you satisfied? - Of course! And you? - Without a doubt. I am sure that both parts are the same: after all, it was I who divided them. The robbers dispersed, satisfied with the division. The next time there were three of them. And again it was necessary to divide the spoils equally, but this time among three. How to do this? At first, the senior robber tried to use the same idea. He divided everything into three (as it seemed to him) equal parts and invited the other robbers to choose their share. The elder hoped that they would choose different parts - then he would take the rest, and everyone would be happy. But both of his colleagues wanted the same part. “Okay,” he said. – Since you both think this is the largest part, choose the smallest one. I’ll take it for myself, and you’ll somehow sort it out among yourselves. It was a good idea, but unfortunately, they now chose different parts. And no one else knew what to do. There were arguments, then shots... The police appeared surprisingly quickly, and the robbers were glad that they managed to escape. But all three continued to think: how could it be possible to divide the spoils fairly, so that everyone was sure that they received at least a third (even if everyone had different points of view on what was a third of the spoils and what was not)? The next time there were ten robbers. But now they had to divide the spoils into 10 parts! The eldest told the rest how difficult it was to divide even into three parts, and suggested: “I think we need to call a mathematician, otherwise we won’t be able to cope.” I have one good friend. – Will I have to pay him? – the others grimaced. - No, no, he will do it for free. He loves to solve complex problems. And he will be pleased if he can write a mathematical article about this. - Without our names? - Without. “Well then, let him try,” the robbers reluctantly agreed. The mathematician really became interested in the problem. After some thought, he proposed the following solution. “Robbers,” he began, “are each of you sure that he (and only he) knows how much 1/10 of this rubbish is?” “Of course,” they confirmed somewhat offended. - Wonderful! Then you,” he pointed to one of the robbers, “separate 1/10 of this.” - He obeyed. “Now you,” he pointed to the other. – Do you think it’s more than 1/10?

“No,” answered the robber. - Amazing! Then you,” he pointed to the next one. – Do you think it’s more than 1/10? “Yes, there’s too much here,” he grumbled. - Then reduce the share, make it exactly 1/10! - asked the mathematician. The robber reduced the pile somewhat. “Now it’s really 1/10 here,” he commented. “And you,” the mathematician turned to the first two robbers, “now you can be sure that there is even less than 1/10 in the pile.” “Of course, they removed a little from it,” they confirmed. - Well, great! Then I’ll ask the next one,” and he chose a new robber, “do you think there’s more than 1/10 here?” And so the mathematician interviewed everyone, offering to reduce the share of those who believed that it was too large. When the last robber was questioned, the mathematician again addressed everyone. - So, there couldn’t be anyone left among you who thinks that this pile now contains more than 1/10 of the loot. “Naturally,” answered the robbers. Everyone who thought the part was big has already reduced it. - Wonderful! And who was the last to reduce this share? “I am,” responded the young robber. - So, when you divided, you were sure that this was 1/10. – I am still sure of this. After all, no one reduced it after me. - So take it for yourself! You think that this is 1/10 of the production, but the rest are sure that this is no more. - Yes! – the robbers happily agreed. They began to understand. - Well, good! Now we have a simpler task: divide the remainder into 9 parts. Let's start the same way.
