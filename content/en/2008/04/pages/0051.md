OLYMPICS

3. On sides AB and BC of triangle ABC, points K and M are chosen, respectively, so that KM || AC. The segments AM and KS intersect at point O. It is known that AK = AO and KM = MC. Prove that AM = KB. M. Murashkin

4. The tournament, in which 20 athletes participated, was judged by 10 referees. Each player played each other once, and each match was officiated by exactly one referee. After the end of each game, both participants took a photo with the referee. A year after the tournament, a stack of all these photographs was found (it is unclear from the photograph who the players are and who is the referee). It turned out that not everyone can be defined as an athlete or a referee. How many such people could there be? B.Frenkin

5. Place 9 points on the plane so that no 4 lie on the same line, but out of any six there are 3 that lie on the same line. (On figure draw all the lines on which three marked points lie.) A. Hakobyan

6. The player has M gold and N silver coins. At the beginning of each round, the player bets some coins on red, some on black (you don’t have to bet anything at all on one of the colors, some coins don’t have to bet anywhere). At the end of each round, the dealer announces that one of the colors has won. The dealer gives the bet on the winning color to the player, doubling the number of coins of each type, and takes the bet on the losing color for himself. The player wants to have exactly three times more coins of one type than another (in particular, he would be content to be left completely without money). At what M and N will the croupier not be able to stop him? A. Chebotarev

9th grade

1. Two KVN teams participate in a game of four competitions. For each competition, each of the six judges gives a score - an integer from 1 to 5; the computer finds the arithmetic average of the marks for the competition and rounds it to the nearest tenth. The winner is determined by the sum of the four values ​​obtained by the computer. Could it turn out that the sum of all the scores given by the judges is greater for the losing team than for the winning team? A. Gorbachev

2. A cyclist travels along a ring road, moving in one direction. Every day he drives 71 km and stops overnight on the side of the road. There is an anomalous zone 71 km long on the road. If a cyclist stops there for the night at a distance of one kilometer from one boundary of the zone, he wakes up in the opposite place of the zone, at a distance of one kilometer from its other boundary. Prove that no matter where the cyclist begins his journey, sooner or later he will fall asleep or wake up in this place. G. Kolyutsky

3. Let AL be the bisector of triangle ABC, O be the center of the circle circumscribed about this triangle, D be a point on side AC such that AD = AB. Prove that lines AO and LD are perpendicular. R.Zhenodarov

4. Let's call a complication of a number the addition of one digit to it at the beginning, at the end, or between any two of its digits. Is there a natural number from which

Is it impossible to get a perfect square with a hundred complications? A. Gorbachev

5. Vasya has 100 bank cards. Vasya knows that one of the cards contains 1 ruble, the other 2 rubles, and so on, the last one has 100 rubles, but he does not know which card has how much money. Vasya can insert the card into the ATM and request a certain amount. The ATM dispenses the required amount if it is on the card, does not dispense anything if there is no such money on the card, and eats the card in any case. However, the ATM does not show how much money was on the card. What is the largest amount Vasya can be guaranteed to receive? I. Mitrofanov

6. Show that there is a convex figure bounded by circular arcs, which can be cut into several parts and from them two convex figures bounded by circular arcs can be folded. I. Ivanov-Pogodaev, A. Malistov

10th grade

1. The auditorium has the shape of a regular hexagon with a side of 3 m. A snatch meter is installed in each corner, which determines the number of sleeping students at a distance not exceeding 3 m. How many sleeping students are there in the classroom, if the sum of the snatch meter readings is 7? A. Kaibhanov

2. Andrey and Boris are playing the next game. Initially, there is a robot on the number line at point p. First, Andrey says the distance by which the robot should move. Then Boris chooses the direction in which the robot moves by this distance, etc. For what p can Andrey ensure that in a finite number of moves the robot gets to one of the points 0 or 1, regardless of Boris’s actions? D. Baranov

3. All integers from –33 to 100 inclusive were placed in some order and the sums of each two adjacent numbers were considered. It turned out that there are no zeros among them. Then for each such sum we found a number that is its inverse. The resulting numbers were added. Could the result be an integer? I. Bogdanov

4. Prove that if a certain polynomial with integer coefficients takes values ​​at k integer points among the numbers from 1 to k – 1, then for k ³ 6 these values ​​are equal. M. Malkin

5. Altitudes AA¢ and CC¢ of acute triangle ABC intersect at point H. Point $B_{0}$ is the midpoint of side AC. Prove that the intersection point of lines symmetrical $BB_{0}$ and $HB_{0}$ with respect to the bisectors of angles ABC and ANS, respectively, lies on the line A ¢ C ¢. A. Zaslavsky

6. Natural numbers are colored in N colors. There are infinitely many numbers of each color. It is known that the color of the half-sum of two different numbers of the same parity depends only on the colors of the terms (for example, the half-sum of blue and red is always yellow). a) Prove that the half-sum of numbers of the same parity of the same color is always painted in the same color. b) For what N is such a coloring possible? A. Shapovalov
