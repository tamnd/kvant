REVOLUTION

In 1989, a certain intern at one of the Japanese universities, while studying the effect of an electron beam on GaN films, once forgot to turn off the installation at night, and in the morning he discovered an unusually bright glow of the sample - the activation of the impurity that turns GaN into a p-type semiconductor had taken place. (How often in the history of science do discoveries happen that were made due to the negligence, forgetfulness, illiteracy, laziness of their authors! This is not a call to forget to turn off the installations at night; another important thing is not to overlook a happy accident.) A few years earlier, researchers from Moscow University obtained a similar result. However, the scientific world did not notice both publications, gallium nitride was no longer in fashion, everyone immediately switched to zinc selenide, and soon turned away from it as well. (It’s amazing how deep, independent minds suddenly, as if on command, drop everything and run towards some bright flash, which often turns into a mirage. Or maybe they, as a whole, are not so “deep and independent”?) Nakamura saw the main thing in the publications - the activation of gallium nitride and its transformation into a p-type semiconductor is possible. He found a successful technological implementation for this procedure, which became the basis for the production of “blue” LEDs in 1993. And soon he elegantly used the traditional developments of the Nitsche company: if a suitable phosphor is applied to the “blue” chip, then, thanks to the conversion of part of the light flux into green-yellow-red tones, it is possible, as a result, to obtain white light. Thus, LEDs made a serious bid to invade lighting technology, and since 2000–2001, the LED revolution has received tremendous acceleration. Let us remember that the “hero” of all these pathetic events is a modest, miniature LED chip with an area of ​​2–4 $мм^{2}$ and a fraction of a millimeter thick. But its internal structure is a bouquet of super achievements in solid state physics and technology. Gallium nitride can only be obtained in the form of thin films, so a silicon carbide wafer is taken as a substrate (it is structurally close to GaN) and several dozen layers of GaN and its derivatives InGaN and AlGaN are grown on it. These structures are called superlattices; in them, the composition of the film changes over distances commensurate with the size of the atoms, and none of the regions is actually homogeneous and independent of its neighbors. The properties of superlattices differ significantly from the properties of the same semiconductors in the form of massive samples or in the form of thick films. Superlattice technology has opened up inexhaustible possibilities for the creators of new materials unknown and inaccessible to nature. Upon closer examination, the emitting layer turns out to be significantly inhomogeneous in area; indium additives accumulate in small formations, occupying no more than 10% of the film. These are the so-called quantum dots - the “last squeak” of the semiconductor fashion, which, however, is already twenty years old. It is through quantum dots that the LED current flows, and radiation is generated in them; everything else in the crystal is like a pedestal under a statue, it seems that it is not necessary, but it is impossible without it. Modern LEDs are the clearest illustration of the power of nanotechnology; only thanks to it, the efficiency of converting electricity into light is close to 50% for white light, and for spectrally homogeneous colors it is even higher. Let's return to earthly affairs; there were many interesting events here. For several years, the company “Nitsche” managed to keep anyone away from its technology and make super profits by selling ready-made chips, but when billions of dollars are at stake, neither patents nor courts can save you from industrial espionage, and secrets are divulged. Nakamura moved to the United States, and the company’s legal battles with the defector began, and in September 2006, Nakamura received the prestigious Millennium Prize (1 million euros) - the world recognized his leadership.

Epilogue

It is a thankless task to write the history of a revolution during the revolution itself. As soon as the author comes up with some kind of scheme for presenting events, when suddenly - bam! - a discovery appears that crosses out all his constructions. If you give in to unbridled fantasy, you can expect a lot. For example, the creation of semiconductor structures with such a variety of energy levels that will make it possible to obtain any glow and controllably change it by external commands. Then there will be no need for phosphors, luminous efficiency will increase, the “quality” of light will improve, and the durability and temperature stability of LEDs will increase. Perhaps tomorrow one of our readers will turn this fantasy into reality. So let’s not summarize, the topic remains open...
