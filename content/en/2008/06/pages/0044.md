XXXIX International Physics Olympiad

This year, 376 schoolchildren from 82 countries arrived in Vietnam for the International Physics Olympiad. The Russian national team included: Andrey Zeleneev - Kirov, Physics and Mathematics Lyceum, teachers-mentors Vladimir Sergeevich Zagray, Mikhail Vladimirovich Gyrdymov, Pavel Buslaev - St. Petersburg, "Physico-Technical School" at the Physico-Technical Institute. A.F.Ioffe, teacher-mentors Artem Vladimirovich Savelyev, Mikhail Georgievich Ivanov, Khariton Matveev - Moscow, Lyceum 1581, teacher-mentors Lidiya Viktorovna Buchneva, Alexander Rafailovich Zilberman, Alexander Mikhailovich Kiselev, Leonid Samoilov - Saratov, Physics and Technology Lyceum 1, teacher-mentors Pravdina Lyudmila Veniaminovna, Tatarkov Harry Nikolaevich, Melnikov Igor - Chelyabinsk, Lyceum 31, teachers-mentors Ivan Aleksandrovich Igolevich, Maxim Leonidovich Karmanov, Sergey Grigorievich Lisitsyn. Our team was led by Professor of the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (MIPT) Stanislav Mironovich Kozel and MIPT Associate Professor Valery Pavlovich Slobodyanin. The Russian delegation included MIPT Associate Professor Dmitry Anatolyevich Aleksandrov and Honored Teacher of Russia Ivan Aleksandrovich Iogolevich as observers. Participation in the Olympiad of observers took place thanks to the support of the Russian Foundation for the Promotion of Education and Science and the Board of Trustees of Lyceum 31 of the city of Chelyabinsk. For many years, the preparation of the team begins more than a year in advance and is carried out according to a standard scheme on the basis of the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology. At the Olympiad, participants were offered three theoretical problems and two experimental tasks. Each theoretical problem was scored out of 10 points, and the experimental tasks were scored out of 9.5 and 10.5 points. Thus, the maximum number of points that each participant in the Olympiad could score was 50. The comparative results of the performance of the 16 best teams at the Olympiad are as follows:

It should be noted that the volume of Olympiad tasks increases from year to year, and this increase is mainly due to an increase in the calculation part. This year at the Olympiad, the overload of calculations for theoretical round tasks exceeded all reasonable limits. This resulted in most participants receiving less than a third of the maximum theory points. During the experimental round, participants were asked to master the differential thermometric method, widely used in precision measurement technology. In the first part of the experiment, the differential thermometric method was used to determine the solidification temperature of a pure crystalline substance provided in an amount of 20 mg. In the case of such a small mass of matter, the conventional method gives too large an error. When completing the task, it was necessary to conduct several preliminary experiments and construct a series of graphs to determine the installation parameters and estimate errors. During the training camp, our guys were well prepared for tasks of this kind. For the first part of the experiment, they scored 97% of the possible points. This is an extremely high result. In the second part of the experimental task, participants examined the operation of the solar cell and determined its efficiency using the differential thermometric method. When completing this part of the task, participants had to demonstrate knowledge of the laws of thermodynamics and the ability to process the results of the experiment. Using graphs, it was necessary to prove that theoretical formulas correctly describe the operation of the installation, and to perform a series of complex photometric measurements.
