INFORMATION

Department of “Morality, Law, Law” (law and civics)

Schoolchildren in grades 8–11 and “Collective Student” groups are offered two courses. 1) One-year course “Conversations about human rights, morality, law, law and state.” The course provides modern ideas about the basic concepts related to law, law and the state, talks about the basics of Russian legislation, and human rights. Examples of court cases are analyzed and general cultural examples related to the focus of the course are given. 2) One and a half year course “Conversations about the foundations of democracy.” We suggest taking the courses in this order. And only high school students, if they do not have time to complete both courses in a row, but definitely want to take the second course, can start directly from it. Those wishing to study must provide their full postal address (email address, if any), last name, first name and patronymic, how many classes have been completed. When assessing the entrance work, we take into account the age (basic education) of the applicant. Be sure to include a stamped, self-addressed envelope in your letter (so we can respond to you) and the answers to the questions below. The deadline for submitting the entry work is June 1, 2009. “Collective student” groups are accepted without introductory work.

Questions

1. Why do we need parties that represent a known minority of the population? 2. Why, despite the indignation of the majority of tram (or other public transport) passengers, does the driver wait until the opportunity opens up for safe passage past an obstructing car, in which there may be only 1-2 people? 3. Compare the concepts of “law” and “legislation”. Which one seems broader to you? 4. Which of the following names was included here “by mistake”: Koni A.F.; Kornilov L.G.; Plevako F.N.; Alexandrov P.A.; Spasovich V.D.? 5. Sort the questions asked above by difficulty (for you personally).

Department of History

Studying at the department will allow everyone, including residents of the most remote cities and villages, to broaden their horizons and prepare for entering a university. Those who successfully complete the course receive a diploma. Why do you need to study history? First of all, it's just interesting. It is interesting to know how people once lived, what they dressed in, what they ate, what they read, how they got married, what they fought for and “what they got into.” Secondly, it's useful. Only by knowing the past can one understand the present and predict the future. We will help you figure it out. Experienced teachers write books especially for you. You will be one of the first to know the latest news from the world of history! We will keep in constant contact with you. According to our books, you will perform special tasks and tell us what you have unearthed. After all, in essence, the work of a historian consists of these excavations. A historical archaeologist, digging through the earth and sand, finds grains of bygone times; a historian-archivist digs through a pile of papers and

retrieves from archives and even from private correspondence everything that can allow him to understand the image of the times; a theoretical historian, like a fascinating novel, reads archaeological tables, dry reports, statistics and uses them to reconstruct the living fabric of a bygone life. The historian has a special profession: he is an investigator, prosecutor and lawyer of the time in one person. The introductory task for the department is completed on a double sheet of paper. The deadline for submitting the entry work is June 1, 2009. “Collective student” groups are accepted without introductory work upon the application of the leader.

Exercise

1. Guess who it is: $\cdot$ A temple was erected over his coffin. $\cdot$ Andrei Rublev’s icon “Trinity” and Nesterov’s painting “Vision to the Youth Bartholomew” are dedicated to him. $\cdot$ Son of a poor Rostov nobleman. $\cdot$ As a child, he had an unusual interest for the 14th century - a passion for reading and writing. $\cdot$ In the 20th century they would have said about him “out of this world, a black sheep.” $\cdot$ A possible career for such a young man in Ancient Rus' - a bookworm in the temple, a copyist of books, a compiler of chronicles... if not for his ascetic nature. $\cdot$ In his youth - a hermit. He set up a cell in the forest on a steep hill and retired into seclusion, taking a vow of silence, monasticism, and celibacy. $\cdot$ Founder and abbot of the Trinity-Sergius Monastery. $\cdot$ Established the holiday of the Trinity in Rus' (during the years of the yoke - a symbol of the unity of Rus'). $\cdot$ By personal example, he captivated the masses of Russian people to undeveloped lands. $\cdot$ Contributed to resettlement to areas inaccessible to Horde raids. $\cdot$ One after another, his disciples go to remote places in the wasteland and establish monasteries - the spiritual and cultural centers of Rus'. $\cdot$ His disciples built 40 monasteries, students of disciples - $60. \cdot$ His followers - Savva Storozhevsky, Kirill Belozersky and others. 2. Describe, in no more than 7 sentences, the political portrait of the chairman of the first Soviet government.

Attention! OL VZMSH is recruiting for the “Social Studies” course. The course includes the following disciplines: philosophy, sociology, political science, theory of state, Russian government, law, economics. Students are sent original teaching aids created on the basis of many years of experience of the course authors. Knowledge testing is carried out using a generally accepted testing system. The course program lasts 1 year. The training is correspondence in nature and aims to give school graduates - both large cities and small villages - deep knowledge in social disciplines, prepare them for successfully passing the Unified State Exam and admission to humanitarian universities. To enroll in a course (both an individual program and a “Collective Student” program), you must submit an application before June 1, 2009. In the application, please indicate your last name, first name, patronymic, your full home address (with zip code!), and the class in which you will study from September 1, 2009.
