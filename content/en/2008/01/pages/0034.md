Is it so familiar to you?

thermal equilibrium

Of course, good! After all, thermal phenomena are among the first to be studied in school. And behind the fragments from scientific works placed here, one can immediately guess the tasks of using the heat balance equation or laboratory work on finding the temperature of mixed liquids. And in everyday life, we are constantly faced with either determining the temperature of our body using a thermometer, or preparing a bath at a comfortable temperature - for which we mix hot water with cold, or adding milk to scalding tea or coffee - without waiting for it to cool down. In general, thermal equilibrium is so clear and simple! However, already in high school, when becoming familiar with the laws of thermodynamics, this simplicity ceases to seem so obvious. How would you react, for example, to the idea of ​​the “heat death of the Universe”, which would lead to a universal desire to equalize temperature? It turns out that all attempts to explain why this did not happen over the unimaginably long history of our universe were in vain until the creation of the general theory of relativity. Another example of the non-triviality of the concept of thermodynamic equilibrium and its deep connection with other branches of science is Le Chatelier’s principle given in the epigraph. You will encounter a different formulation of it, for example, when studying electromagnetic induction or chemical processes, which will confirm its validity beyond thermal phenomena. We cannot ignore the problems that arose in classical thermodynamics during the transition to the study of open systems and nonequilibrium processes occurring in both living and inanimate nature. After all, the emergence of a wide variety of structures, their “self-organization,” and, ultimately, the very appearance of life do not agree with established ideas about the destruction of everything harmonious and ordered during the evolution to equilibrium, chaotic states. This is how far thinking about seemingly simple questions can take us. Therefore, when thinking about them, do not miss their deep content behind the external simplicity.
