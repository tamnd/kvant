A.PETRUNIN

To my son Nicodemus

IMAGINE THAT YOU ARE SITTING IN CLASS AND YOU ARE bored and bored and really want to do geometric constructions: draw, for example, a triangle, find the centers of the inscribed and circumscribed circles and the orthocenter in it, draw the Euler line and all that. But here’s the problem: the compass and ruler were left at home. This is the situation that two origamists, the Italian Benedetto Skimemi and the Japanese Hamyaki Hujita, probably found themselves in 15 years ago. (Origamists are people who are fond of the ancient Japanese art of origami - folding various animals, birds, lanterns, etc. from a sheet of paper.) Suddenly it dawns on them: “But the fold of a sheet of paper is straight.” For example, if you take a segment on a piece of paper and bend the sheet so that the ends of the segment are connected, and then smooth the sheet carefully on the desk, then the bend forms a median perpendicular to the original segment (Fig.1,a).
