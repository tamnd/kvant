Application

Isotopes are varieties of a chemical element with different numbers of nucleons in their nuclei. Since they are the same chemical element, they have the same number of atomic electrons, and therefore nuclear protons. The numbers of neutrons in their nuclei are different. All isotopes of one element are located in one cell of the periodic table, hence their name: “iso” - identical, “topos” - place. The most famous isotopes are the hydrogen isotopes deuterium and tritium. Currently, about 1,400 isotopes are known, 357 of them are natural isotopes of 92 chemical elements, the remaining isotopes are obtained artificially in various nuclear reactions. Many isotopes are radioactive and are used in a variety of fields of science and technology (so-called labeled atoms). Therefore, the problem of isotope separation arose back in the 30s of the last century. Due to the identical chemical properties of isotopes of one element, it becomes necessary to use the difference in the physical properties of isotopes and their compounds, resulting from the difference in the masses of their nuclei.
