This method is widely used for the separation of small quantities of isotopes, but for the industrial separation of uranium isotopes it has proven to be very difficult and uneconomical. Until the 70s of the last century, the method of gas diffusion through porous partitions was used. At sufficiently low pressures, when the mean free path of molecules is much larger than the pore diameters, the diffusion rate is determined only by the average speed of thermal motion. A small difference in molecular mass produces a small difference in speed. The uranium hexafluoride gas that passes through the partition becomes a little poorer in the heavy isotope and a little richer in the light isotope. With a sequential process of passing through hundreds of partitions, the output can be a gas sufficiently enriched in a light isotope. In a rotating gas, an equilibrium distribution of the concentration of molecules is established along the distance from the axis of rotation, just as it is established depending on the height in the earth’s atmosphere. Only in a rotating tube the role of gravity is played by the centrifugal force of inertia, directed radially from the axis of rotation. The increase in concentration with distance from the axis of rotation occurs faster for particles of greater mass, therefore, in the outer part of the tube, the ratio of the concentrations of heavy and light particles turns out to be greater than near the axis of rotation. To obtain a noticeable difference in concentrations, a huge rotation speed is required - about 1000 rps. The main difficulty in this method was not so much in ensuring a high rotation speed, but in transferring the enriched mixture from one centrifuge to another without stopping the rotation. All these issues were successfully resolved by 1974, mainly thanks to the experimental and organizational talent of one of the leaders of these works, Isaac Konstantinovich Kikoin. L. Belopukhov

Psychological "Curl"

(For the beginning, see page 4 of the cover)

At the beginning of August 2008, the 28th World Congress of Puzzle Lovers will take place in Prague. Hundreds of “smart” toys will be brought to the Czech Republic from dozens of countries around the world, and then puzzle experts from Europe, America and Japan will evaluate them and name the best inventions of the year. Among the new puzzles will be “Curl” by Muscovite Kirill Grebnev. Thanks to its originality, it will certainly attract the attention of puzzle lovers, especially since at the previous congress in Australia, two of Kirill’s puzzles were named among the best inventions of 2007. This was the second time in the history of the conventions that two puzzles from the same author were among the winners. Kirill Grebnev's new puzzle is shown at figure. To make a “Curl” yourself, take three pieces of steel or copper wire with a diameter of 3–4 mm. For the version of the puzzle shown here, take pieces of wire 25, 22 and 14.5 cm long. On the longest one, intended for the staple, mark a point dividing it into segments of 9 and 13 cm. Holding the wire with pliers at this point, bend the ends of the wire so that the distance between them is 12 mm. Then the rings are bent at the ends of the staples. Taking a photo of the puzzle allows you to use the image as a template: gradually bend the wire with pliers, and then apply the pieces to the photo to check the correct dimensions. Solving a puzzle usually begins with picking it up: with your left hand by the bracket, and with your right hand by the handle. After that, holding the end of the handle, they begin to move it along the bracket, trying to push the ring through the curl and unhook the handle from the bracket. As a matter of fact, nothing else can be done - there are too few movements allowed by the design. Let us immediately warn you that none of these attempts will bring you any closer to a solution. The first mistake of everyone who begins to solve this puzzle is that they grab the wrong end of the handle with their right hand (we are talking about right-handed people). This ingenious psychological trick was specially invented by the inventor to lead you away from the correct decision, to force you to fruitlessly move the handle back and forth along the staple, trying to push it into the curl. The correct way is not to move the ring towards the curl, but to release the end of the handle that you are holding on to so stubbornly and push it through the loop of the curl. If this movement is performed correctly, then you can find the further solution yourself using two photographs on the 4th page of the cover, which show the intermediate steps leading to solving the puzzle. We wish you success! A. Kalinin
