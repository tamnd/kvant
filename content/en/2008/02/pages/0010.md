Energy of nuclear fission reaction A feature of the atomic nucleus is that in a very small region of space (100,000 times less than the space of an atom) a small number (from 1 to 250) of massive (compared to an electron) objects - nucleons - are concentrated. According to modern theory, each nucleon is three quarks of different types, held together by nuclear glue - gluons. Without delving into the mysteries of the quark-gluon world, to consider the issue of nuclear energy it is enough to use the model idea of ​​the nucleus as a sum of nucleons, some of which are positively charged protons, and the other part are uncharged neutrons. Protons and neutrons are united by special short-range attractive forces that do not depend on the presence or absence of an electric charge. But it is natural that electric (Coulomb) repulsive forces also act between charged protons. Stability of nuclei, i.e. the presence of stable atoms (from helium to lead) is determined by the balance of opposing electrical and nuclear forces. For atomic nuclei with a proton number (element number) greater than 82 (lead), the balance is slightly upset. In these nuclei, “extra” protons have a probability of being separated and repelled from the nucleus, taking neutrons with them. This is the mechanism of radioactive alpha decay.
