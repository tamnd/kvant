The kinetic energy of alpha, beta and gamma particles is the energy of radioactive transformations in nuclei. Natural radioactivity was first discovered (by A. Becquerel) in 1896, and a little over 40 years later the fission reaction of the uranium nucleus was discovered. The energy released in this reaction is primarily the kinetic energy of the reaction products - the “halves” of the uranium nucleus A and B. The energy of neutrons and gamma particles is added to it. The origin of the kinetic energy of the fragments is explained by the action of electrical forces $отталкивания.^{3}$ Calculation for the fission energy of all nuclei contained in 1 kg of uranium gives a value of the order of $10^{14}$ J, or, in terms of the explosive energy equivalent mass of TNT, – 5 kilotons. This value is the upper limit of the released energy. But, firstly, not all the uranium in the bomb has time to react. Secondly, in nuclei with a smaller number of nucleons the role of nuclear attractive forces is greater than for heavy nuclei. This means that nuclear forces during fission counteract electrical forces and weaken their effect.

Nuclear bomb and nuclear power plant

For a long time this weapon was called an atomic bomb, which has no physical meaning. As a result of atomic (molecular) transformations, energy is released during the explosion of ordinary, not nuclear explosives. To create a nuclear bomb, in addition to solving theoretically fundamental issues, partially discussed above, it was necessary to carry out many practical tasks. I will list them briefly: – processing of natural uranium in order to extract from it the isotope uranium-235 of high purity (0.9999); – production of plutonium-239 from uranium-238 under the powerful influence of neutrons; – creation of a structure in which the connection of individual blocks having a size smaller than the critical one into a single block would occur quite effectively; – creation of a completely new type of test site; – creation of means of delivering a bomb to the point of explosion. Separating uranium-235 from uranium-238 is a very difficult task. After all, the chemical properties of these nuclei are the same. Therefore, only physical and mechanical processes that use small differences in the masses of these isotopes are suitable. For example, methods are used that use different diffusion rates and different deflections of ions in a magnetic field, but the centrifugation method turned out to be the most profitable. All this work was led by I.K. Kikoin, one of the most brilliant experimental physicists, for many long and most important years. I note that each operation of enriching a mixture with the desired isotope must be repeated not two or three times, but tens and even hundreds of times. Isotope separation plants are kilometer-long rows of identical machines that produce increasingly pure products. The devices circulate gaseous uranium hexafluoride, which still needs to be obtained from uranium ore and purified from all kinds of foreign impurities - and these are also kilometer-long rows of identical devices. The most effective, from an economic point of view, is the method of separating isotopes using centrifuges. In the 1970–80s, unique centrifuges were created in the USSR, which still have no analogues in the world. Suffice it to say that the rotation speed of a uranium-containing vessel reaches 1000 revolutions per second. Imagine what extraordinary mechanical properties these vessels must have and what the bearings must be like! The production of plutonium is somewhat simpler than the methods of separating uranium isotopes. Plutonium is formed when the uranium-238 nucleus absorbs a neutron and the subsequent beta transformation of uranium first into neptunium, and then into plutonium. This is a different chemical element, and its isolation from a mixture with uranium can be done chemically. But the only way to create powerful neutron fluxes is with a uranium nuclear reactor, which uses a slow, non-explosive fission process because the reactor contains mostly ordinary uranium-238. A small fraction of the uranium-235 isotope provides the fission process and neutron flux to convert uranium-238 into plutonium. You noticed, of course, that this is a description of a nuclear power plant. After all, the energy released during the fission process in the form of kinetic energy of fission products heats the entire environment. Energy is collected by a coolant (water or liquid metals), which, outside the reactor core, ultimately releases it to electric current generators. In the design of a nuclear reactor, the main thing is to ensure reliability in the stationarity of the process. Automation is quite capable of coping with this task.
