Task 3 In physics, relationships between physical quantities are expressed in the form of equalities, the left and right sides of which must be of the same type, i.e. have the same dimensions. For example, it is impossible for the right side of the relation to have the dimension of length, and the left side to have the dimension of time. Using this fact, it is sometimes possible to derive the desired physical relationship without solving the problem analytically. For example, in order to find the time during which an object falls from a height h with a constant acceleration g, it is necessary, using the problem parameters h and g, to construct a quantity that has the dimension of time. It is easy to see that this can be done in the only way: T = a h g. Note that this solution includes an undetermined dimensionless coefficient a, which cannot be determined by the method under consideration. This coefficient can be equal to 1, 1/2, $3, \pi$ or any other real number. This method of obtaining physical relationships is called the dimensional analysis method. In the dimensional analysis method, dimensionless coefficients are not important. Fortunately, in most physical problems these coefficients are of order 1, and their exclusion does not change the order of the physical quantity. Thus, dimensional analysis when solving the problem posed above gives the following answer: $T^{=} h g$. Typically, the dimension of a physical quantity is expressed in terms of the dimensions of four basic physical quantities - M (mass), L (length), T (time), and K (temperature). The dimension of an arbitrary value x is written as [x]. For example, the dimensions of velocity v, kinetic energy $E_{k}$ and heat capacity $C_{V}$ are expressed as follows: $[ v ]^{=} LT^{-} 1, [ E_{k} ]^{=} ML^{2} T^{-} 2$ and $[ C_{V} ]^{=} ML^{2} T^{-} 2 K^{-} 1$.

2. Physics of Black Holes In this part of the problem, try to determine some properties of black holes using the method of dimensional analysis. According to Hawking's theorem, “black holes have no hair,” i.e. all physical characteristics of a black hole, which are discussed in this part, are determined only by its mass. One of the most important physical characteristics of a black hole is the surface area of ​​its “event horizon.” (The event horizon defines the boundary of a black hole. Inside the region defined by the event horizon, the gravitational field is so strong that not even light can escape beyond this region.) You are asked to find the relationship between the mass m of a black hole and the area A of its event horizon. This area depends on the mass of the black hole, the speed of light and the gravitational constant. As in paragraph 1.3, this relationship can be represented as $A = G^{\alpha} c^{\beta} m^{\gamma}. 2.1.$ Use the dimensional analysis method to determine $\alpha, \beta$, and $\gamma$. (0.8 b.) From the above it follows that the area of ​​the event horizon of a black hole increases with increasing its mass. From a classical point of view, nothing can leave the event horizon of a black hole, and therefore in all physical processes the area of ​​the black hole's event horizon can only increase. By analogy with the second law of thermodynamics, Bekenstein proposed to consider that the entropy S of a black hole is proportional to the area of ​​its event horizon, i.e. $S = \eta A$. The hypothesis can be made more plausible by using a different approach. 2.2. Using the thermodynamic definition of entropy $dS^{=} dQ \theta$, determine the dimension of entropy.
