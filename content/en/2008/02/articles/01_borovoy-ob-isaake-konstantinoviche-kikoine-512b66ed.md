Task 3 In physics, relationships between physical quantities are expressed in the form of equalities, the left and right sides of which must be of the same type, i.e. have the same dimensions. For example, it is impossible for the right side of the relation to have the dimension of length, and the left side to have the dimension of time. Using this fact, it is sometimes possible to derive the desired physical relationship without solving the problem analytically. For example, in order to find the time during which an object falls from a height h with a constant acceleration g, it is necessary, using the problem parameters h and g, to construct a quantity that has the dimension of time. It is easy to see that this can be done in the only way: T = a h g. Note that this solution includes an undetermined dimensionless coefficient a, which cannot be determined by the method under consideration. This coefficient can be equal to 1, 1/2, $3, \pi$ or any other real number. This method of obtaining physical relationships is called the dimensional analysis method. In the dimensional analysis method, dimensionless coefficients are not important. Fortunately, in most physical problems these coefficients are of order 1, and their exclusion does not change the order of the physical quantity. Thus, dimensional analysis when solving the problem posed above gives the following answer: $T^{=} h g$. Typically, the dimension of a physical quantity is expressed in terms of the dimensions of four basic physical quantities - M (mass), L (length), T (time), and K (temperature). The dimension of an arbitrary value x is written as [x]. For example, the dimensions of velocity v, kinetic energy $E_{k}$ and heat capacity $C_{V}$ are expressed as follows: $[ v ]^{=} LT^{-} 1, [ E_{k} ]^{=} ML^{2} T^{-} 2$ and $[ C_{V} ]^{=} ML^{2} T^{-} 2 K^{-} 1$.

Energy of nuclear fission reaction A feature of the atomic nucleus is that in a very small region of space (100,000 times less than the space of an atom) a small number (from 1 to 250) of massive (compared to an electron) objects - nucleons - are concentrated. According to modern theory, each nucleon is three quarks of different types, held together by nuclear glue - gluons. Without delving into the mysteries of the quark-gluon world, to consider the issue of nuclear energy it is enough to use the model idea of ​​the nucleus as a sum of nucleons, some of which are positively charged protons, and the other part are uncharged neutrons. Protons and neutrons are united by special short-range attractive forces that do not depend on the presence or absence of an electric charge. But it is natural that electric (Coulomb) repulsive forces also act between charged protons. Stability of nuclei, i.e. the presence of stable atoms (from helium to lead) is determined by the balance of opposing electrical and nuclear forces. For atomic nuclei with a proton number (element number) greater than 82 (lead), the balance is slightly upset. In these nuclei, “extra” protons have a probability of being separated and repelled from the nucleus, taking neutrons with them. This is the mechanism of radioactive alpha decay. The energy released in this reaction is primarily the kinetic energy of the reaction products - the “halves” of the uranium nucleus A and B. The energy of neutrons and gamma particles is added to it. The origin of the kinetic energy of the fragments is explained by the action of electrical forces $отталкивания.^{3}$ Calculation for the fission energy of all nuclei contained in 1 kg of uranium gives a value of the order of $10^{14}$ J, or, in terms of the explosive energy equivalent mass of TNT, – 5 kilotons. This value is the upper limit of the released energy. But, firstly, not all the uranium in the bomb has time to react. Depending on the design and purpose of the bomb, the efficiency ranges from 10–30%. Secondly, in nuclei with a smaller number of nucleons the role of nuclear attractive forces is greater than for heavy nuclei. This means that nuclear forces during fission counteract electrical forces and weaken their effect.

Nuclear bomb and nuclear power plant

For a long time this weapon was called an atomic bomb, which has no physical meaning. As a result of atomic (molecular) transformations, energy is released during the explosion of ordinary, not nuclear explosives. To create a nuclear bomb, in addition to solving theoretically fundamental issues, partially discussed above, it was necessary to carry out many practical tasks. I will list them briefly: – processing of natural uranium in order to extract from it the isotope uranium-235 of high purity (0.9999); – production of plutonium-239 from uranium-238 under the powerful influence of neutrons; – creation of a structure in which the connection of individual blocks having a size smaller than the critical one into a single block would occur quite effectively; – creation of a completely new type of test site; – creation of means of delivering a bomb to the point of explosion. Thermonuclear weapons

The thermonuclear reaction is the opposite of the fission reaction of heavy nuclei in terms of the role of interaction between particles. This is a synthesis reaction, i.e. combining isotopes of hydrogen into a heavier helium nucleus. In this case, the electrical repulsive forces are positive

But these forces turn on only when the particles approach a distance of the order of $10^{-} 15$ m. And in order to overcome electrical repulsion, the connecting nuclei need to have a speed of the order of tens of thousands of kilometers per second, which corresponds to a temperature of tens and hundreds of millions of degrees. This is why the fusion reaction is called a thermonuclear reaction. You can impart such speed to particles in different ways. For example, you can focus hundreds of streams of fast electrons in one place so that they transfer their kinetic energy to the nuclei. You can use powerful laser radiation. One of the promising ways is to accelerate nuclei with a powerful electric field (spark discharge, lightning). But all these ways are not suitable for nuclear weapons, which must be quite portable. The only way is to place a nuclear bomb inside a container containing light elements, say the hydrogen isotopes deuterium and tritium (or lithium deuteride). The kinetic energies (or temperatures) necessary to begin the thermonuclear fusion process are provided by the fireball of a nuclear explosion. The fusion process that has begun will support itself, since the products of the fusion reaction will have greater kinetic energy than the original nuclei. In reality, however, everything turned out to be not so simple. For powerful thermonuclear bombs, it was necessary to come up with schemes for the additional “production” of neutrons from fission reactions of uranium isotopes (except for the primary “ignition” bomb) and the compression of hydrogen “raw materials” by radiation from a secondary nuclear uranium explosion. This principle is called radiation implosion. At the same time, the nuclear fuel supply was fully used. Successful tests of the new design in 1955 at the Semipalatinsk test site and in 1961 at the Novaya Zemlya test site opened the era of the arms race and at the same time raised the question of ending this race. The resolution of this contradiction may have been one of the reasons for the breakdown of our state that occurred in 1991. In 1996, an international agreement was finally reached to stop all nuclear weapons testing, as well as the use of nuclear explosions for peaceful purposes.
