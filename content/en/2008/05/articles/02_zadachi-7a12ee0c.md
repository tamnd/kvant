KVANT 2008/№5

Tasks

1.

Is it possible to arrange the numbers from 1 to 15 in the cells of the table $3 \times 5$ so that in all rows the sums of numbers are the same, and in all columns the sums of numbers are the same? V. Dotsenko

⟦column⟧

4.

Is it possible to saw off a corner from a cube $10 \times 10 \times 10$ so that the cut has the shape of a triangle with sides 2, 3, 4? A. Shen

2.

31 people are sitting at a round table. Some of them are knights who always tell the truth, and the rest are liars who always lie, and there is at least one liar. Each was asked: “How many of your neighbors are liars?” (meaning the neighbor on the left and the neighbor on the right). Everyone gave the same answers. What is the largest number of knights that could be at the table? D. Kalinin

5.

3.

2008 was added to some natural number, then 2008 was added to the result again, and so on. Is there necessarily a number starting with 1 at some point? S. Dorichenko

These tasks are primarily intended for students in grades 6–8.

Illustrations by D. Grishukova

There are six weights whose masses are 1 g, 2 g, 3 g, 4 g, 5 g and 6 g. Each weight is labeled with a mass in grams, but the labels may be mixed up. How can you find out in two weighings on a cup scale whether there are incorrect inscriptions among them (it doesn’t matter which ones)? S. Tokarev
