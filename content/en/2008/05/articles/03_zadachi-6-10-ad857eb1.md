Competition named after A.P. Savin

"Math 6–8"

We continue our next competition on solving mathematical problems for students in grades 6–8. Send solutions to problems within a month after receiving this issue of the journal to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant» (marked “Competition “Mathematics 6–8”). Be sure to include your name, class, and home address. As before, we welcome participation in the competition not only by individual schoolchildren, but also by mathematical clubs. We ask circle leaders to provide an email address or contact phone number. According to tradition, the winning clubs of the correspondence competition are invited to the final in-person tournament.

Summer tournament named after A.P. Savin “Mathematics 6-8”

In a convex polygon, the sum of obtuse angles is $2008^\circ.$ How many sides does this polygon have? A. Shapovalov

From June 26 to July 2, 2008, a summer tournament of mathematical battles named after A.P. Savin was held near the city of Sudislavl, Kostroma region. This is the 14th such tournament, and its popularity is growing every year. The organizers of the tournament were the educational program “Big Change” (director G.V. Kondakov) and the recreation center “Berendeyevy Polyany”. Only the size of the base forced the organizers to limit themselves to 32 teams in grades 6–9. In addition to Muscovites, who made up the lion's share of the teams, teams from Ivanovo, Kirov, Kostroma, Leningrad region, Magnitogorsk, Kharkov and Chernogolovka took part in the tournament. The teams were divided into 5 leagues: four main leagues by class and another league that united the four remaining teams of 6th and 7th classes.

On the first day, a new mathematical game $«Абака»^{1}$ was played as a warm-up, which aroused great interest among schoolchildren. On the second day, the older schoolchildren began round-robin matfight tournaments (5 rounds), the younger students had an oral team Olympiad to divide them into leagues, and 4 rounds of matfights began the next day. In the 9-grade league, the intrigue remained until the last: the teams of Magnitogorsk (leader N.S. Nikiforova), Kostroma (leader D.A. Kalinin) and “Kvantik-1” from Moscow (leader I.A. Nikolaev) beat each other in a circle and defeated the other 3 teams. The blitz battle brought Kostroma to first place, Quantik-1 to 2nd, leaving Magnitogorsk in 3rd place. In the 8-grade league, the struggle was less intense: with a noticeable margin, having won all the battles, the Eureka team from Kharkov (leader A.L. Bershtein) won, 2nd and 3rd places went to Moscow

Of these, we tried to choose those where there is, albeit a small, miracle: a little more follows from the conditions than can be expected, or there is an unexpected twist and brevity in the solution. Each problem indicates which classes it is most suitable for and who its author is. Difficult tasks are marked with asterisks, especially difficult ones are marked with two asterisks.
