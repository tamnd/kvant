Therefore, the difference in energies between the two levels of the atomic nucleus should be greater, the higher the floor of the house on which the nucleus is located. The photon energy is conserved. On the other hand, the frequency $\omega$ of a photon flying in a static gravitational field, and therefore its total energy $e = h \omega$, should not change. As a result, a photon emitted on the lower floor of a house during a transition between two nuclear levels will not be able to cause a reverse transition in the same core on the upper floor. This theoretical prediction was confirmed in the 1960s with the discovery of the Mössbauer effect, which allows tiny shifts in nuclear levels to be measured. But the wavelength changes. A photon flying in a static gravitational field, like a flying stone, retains its total energy e and frequency $\omega$. But its momentum, and therefore its wavelength, changes with the distance to the gravitating body. Refractive index. As the photon moves away from the source of the gravitational field, its speed increases, tending to c, and when approaching the source, it decreases. The gravitational field, as a transparent medium, has a refractive index. This clearly explains the deflection of light in the field of the Sun and in the gravitational lenses of galaxies. A decrease in the speed of a photon near the Sun was discovered in experiments to measure the delay of radar echoes from planets. Clocks or stopwatches. Let clocks A and B be located nearby on the first floor. If you lift clock A to the second floor first, and, say, the next day - clock B, then clock A will be ahead of B. But they will be equally good as synchronized stopwatches. When each point in space is equipped with its own clock, then essentially we are talking not about clocks, but about stopwatches. If we now measure the frequency of light using local stopwatches, it would be lower the higher the floor.

Epistemics and linguistics

Epistemics. Epistemics or, as they used to say, epistemology is the theory of knowledge and cognition. It is obvious that the issues discussed in this article relate not only to physics, but also to epistemics. Physics and semantics. But what does the science of language - linguistics and its branch of semantics - the science of words and symbols have to do with physics? Here it is appropriate to recall Fock’s statement mentioned at the beginning of the report. "Glued Concepts" In Newton's Principia, the concepts of mass and matter (substance) are “glued together”: “mass is proportional to density and volume.” In Einstein's articles, mass is “glued” with inertia and gravity

(inertial and gravitational masses). And energy is “glued” to matter. Archetype. According to dictionaries, an archetype is a historically original form (protoform), original concept, word or original type (prototype). Pauli was very interested in the concept of archetype, and in 1952 he published an article on the influence of archetypal ideas on the creation of natural science theories by Kepler. Perhaps the concept of mass is just such an archetypal concept that “glued together” the concepts of matter, inertia and gravity. Atom and archetype. “Atom and Archetype” - under this title, a book containing correspondence between Wolfgang Pauli and the leading German psychoanalyst Carl Jung, which lasted from 1932 to 1958, was translated from German into English. They discussed, in particular, the material nature of time and the possibility of communicating with people who lived centuries and millennia ago. It is widely known that Pauli took the effect that bears his name seriously: when he entered an experimental laboratory, the instruments in it broke down. Poets about terms. D. Samoilov about words: “They are wiped like glass. And this is our craft.” V. Mayakovsky: “The street is writhing, tongueless. She has nothing to shout or talk about.” The lack of precise terms and the inability to use them is compensated by many authors by the abundant use of parasitic words like “rest mass,” which give the texts smoothness and “energy,” just as words like “pancake” do in ordinary modern speech. How to teach physics. It is necessary to “wipe” and “unstick” the terms. The “umbilical cord” connecting modern physical theory with the previous “mother” theory must be carefully cut off when teaching. (In the case of the theory of relativity, the “mother” was the “centaur”, consisting of Maxwell’s field theory and Newton’s mechanics, and the “umbilical cord” was the relativistic mass.) Let us recall the title of F. Klein’s famous book “Elementary Mathematics from a Higher Point of View”. The landscape of modern physics must be looked at not from the ravine of history, but from the top of the principles of symmetry. It seems to me that it is unacceptable to present the dependence of mass on velocity as an experimental fact, hiding from the student that it is an interpretative “factoid”. (According to dictionaries, a factoid is similar to a fact, but is considered reliable only because it appears in printed texts.)
