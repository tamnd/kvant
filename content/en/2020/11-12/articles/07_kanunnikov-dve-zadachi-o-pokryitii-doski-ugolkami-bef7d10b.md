Two problems about covering a board with corners

A.KANUNIKOV

ONE OF THE COMMON club topics is tiling, cutting, coloring. A typical problem on this topic is formulated as follows: “Can a given figure consisting of cells be cut into figures of a given type?” (see, for example, [1]). In this article we will solve two similar research problems about covering a rectangular board with corners. Problem 1. What size boards m u n can be cut into corners of three squares? Obviously, the number of cells mn must be a multiple of 3, which means that m or n is a multiple of 3. Let us consider a similar problem in which this restriction is lifted. Problem 2. Which boards $размеров^{m} u^{n}$ can be covered with corners of three squares evenly in several layers? (Each cell is covered with the same number of corners.) In the answer to this problem, m and n may no longer be a multiple of 3 (in this case, the number of layers will be a multiple of 3). The author saw an interesting solution to problem 2 for the board 5 u 7 in the book [2] when he was a schoolboy. It is not easy to come to this decision.
