Stealth technologies The second example concerns the development of aircraft designs that are invisible to detection systems, which in theory could turn them into a kind of absolute weapon (later these approaches were used in other systems, in particular on submarines). The word stealth translated from English means secrecy. In this example, a stealth aircraft will be likened to a “mask” that guarantees invulnerability from radars, and the radars themselves will be likened to a “virus” that is trying to see the aircraft and “sting” (activate the destruction systems). But is it possible to create an absolute “mask” of a large size? In the method of detecting flying objects by radar, the signal from the radar station goes into space and is reflected. An image appears on the radar, allowing one to judge the effective dispersion area. If there is no back reflection of radio waves, then the object is invisible, the effective dispersion area is zero. Stealth technology for aircraft with a small effective dispersion area was initially based on two principles: there is no back reflection, there is absorption in the body material. The diode signal (arrow above), when propagating inside the aircraft body, is reflected from structural elements and absorbed in its material. There is no signal return. It would seem that by changing the design of the aircraft body, moving from a smooth, rounded shape to an angular one (again, something similar to coronavirus spikes), and using absorbent coatings, the problem would be solved. However, the implementation of these principles in real designs of stealth aircraft only served as an impetus for the rapid development of both detection systems and stealth technologies. At the same time, the cost of creating detection systems turned out to be many times less than the cost of further steps towards invisibility and invulnerability.
