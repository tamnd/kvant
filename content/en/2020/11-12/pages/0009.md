We will rely heavily on the data of the American immunologist and popularizer of science Erin Bromage, but with verification of this data from other sources. Note that estimates of the settling speed of droplets containing viruses were given in the article by A. Stasenko “Don’t sneeze: a pandemic!” («Kvant», 2020, No. 4). The infectious dose at which a person becomes ill is about 1000 virus particles. This figure is very approximate. It depends on the person’s health status, immune status, age, etc. But it is important to note that in the case of COVID-19, it is not single viruses that lead to infection, but a large number of them obtained in a short period of time. The body's immune system can most often cope with single viruses. A cough is accompanied by the release of several thousand small droplets containing viruses, of which the larger ones fall to the ground, and the smallest ones hang in the air. When you sneeze, tens of thousands of even smaller droplets are released into the air. The total number of viruses released (according to Bromage) when coughing or sneezing is estimated at hundreds of millions. When breathing, especially calmly, a significantly smaller number of viral droplets are released through the nose; Bromage gives a figure of several tens of viral particles per minute. If all these particles land on an uninfected person, the infectious dose accumulates within about an hour of being near the infected person. The patient's conversation increases the number of droplets released by approximately an order of magnitude. In general, the most dangerous ways to spread coronavirus are sneezing and coughing. In this case, the use of masks by both sick and healthy people can radically change the situation. Some food for thought about the benefits of masks is provided by table (Fig.6), built on the basis of published preliminary results of the work of Finnish researchers.

The effectiveness of using masks increases if they are worn by both an infected and a healthy person. The results are even better when people wearing masks are kept at a distance from each other (the risk is close to zero at a distance of more than 1.5–2 m). Let us note an important feature of the pictures from the table: in addition to a mask, a healthy person also wears glasses. After all, the mucous membranes of the eyes are one of the important channels for the penetration of the virus. Even worse: the virus, once in the eyes, persists for a very long time. This is confirmed by data from Chinese scientists: according to their estimates, people who regularly wear glasses are five times less likely to become infected with coronavirus. So, as in previous cases, there is no guarantee, but the “effective area of ​​infection” when using masks in crowded places can still be greatly reduced.
