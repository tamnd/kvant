The same applies to the absorption of radio waves. For radio waves of different wavelengths, their absorption efficiency is different. What's the result? The development of stealth technologies has led to the fact that they have become an important element for new generations of aircraft, missiles and submarines, but they do not solve the problem of absolute protection. But progress towards stealth has been enormous. On new generation aircraft, measures are being taken to reduce visibility both in the radio range and in the visible, infrared and sound ranges. As a result, the effective dispersion area for a large subsonic strategic bomber (without stealth technologies) turns out to be of the order of $100^{м} 2$, for a fighter - of the order of $10 м^{2}$, and for modern stealth aircraft it is even an order of magnitude smaller. But it turned out that stealth aircraft are significantly less maneuverable and slower. As a result, the competition between super-maneuverability and stealth with the rapid development of radar equipment continues. It is curious that the concept of effective dispersion area introduced above can be likened to the concept of interaction cross section in physics, by which the parameters of elementary particles and the nature of interacting fields are judged. Moreover, the cross section of the same object, say a proton, in the case of interaction with different particles can vary greatly. Unlike the many fields of interaction of elementary particles in the Standard Model - strong, electromagnetic, weak and gravitational - in the science of metamaterials only the electromagnetic field operates. And instead of a set of truly elementary particles of the Standard Model - quarks, leptons, gauge bosons and the Higgs boson - a set of simple electromagnetic resonators is used.
