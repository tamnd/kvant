Breathe for your health!

A. MINEEV

THIS ARTICLE WILL TALK ABOUT the effects of oxygen and carbon dioxide on humans - separately and together. Some real intrigue comes from looking at the problem both from the outside - from the inhaled air, and from the inside - inside the body itself. Or, more scientifically, both from the side of external respiration - the exchange between the atmosphere and cells in the lungs, and internal respiration - processes in the cells and tissues of the body. The average pressure of the earth's atmosphere at sea level is approximately $p_{атм} = 760$ mmHg. Art. Oxygen accounts for 160 mmHg. Art. or approximately 21%. Oxygen is partially absorbed by the body, carbon dioxide is formed as a result of chemical oxidation reactions. The composition of inhaled and exhaled air is shown in the table.

Table 1. Composition of inhaled and exhaled air

Why are these numbers interesting? Nitrogen and argon are not used by the human body (they are inert). The degree of oxygen absorption is low, about 0.25. After inhaling, the body exhales most of the oxygen back. Carbon dioxide is practically absent in the inhaled air and is actively formed during oxidative reactions in the body. The percentage of oxygen absorption by the body (21% - 16% = 5%) turns out to be close to the percentage of carbon dioxide formation (4%).

The inertness of nitrogen and argon during metabolic processes in the body has led to the temptation to abandon them altogether under conditions of prolonged stay in a confined space. American astronauts followed this path in their first space flights, switching to breathing pure oxygen. Moreover, the pressure in the case of using only $O_{2}$ was significantly lower than atmospheric pressure and amounted to 260–280 mm Hg. Art. However, as the duration of space flights increased in such a purely oxygen atmosphere, astronauts began to develop problems with the respiratory tract. In addition, a purely oxygen atmosphere is a fire hazard. Russian cosmonauts from the very beginning used an air composition close to that on Earth, which required a more complex air regeneration system. Currently, when flying in space and sailing on submarines, the earth's atmosphere is used.

In our case, the problem of choice - which is primary (in other words, what triggers processes in the human body): oxygen or carbon dioxide - is solved as follows. Previously, oxygen was considered primary - after all, it is the main source of energy, giving impetus to all processes in the body. But now the pendulum of choice has swung towards carbon dioxide. They gradually came to the conclusion that the primary, triggering mechanism is the accumulation of carbon dioxide in the body. The accumulation of $CO_{2}$ in the body during the breakdown of fats and proteins in cells gives a signal to the brain that carbon dioxide needs to be removed from the cells - it “sits” on red blood cells and moves to the alveoli of the lungs. $O_{2}$ “sits” on the vacant seats in the “train” of red blood cells and spreads throughout the body. Therefore, the modern view of the breathing process is as follows: first, carbon dioxide is exhaled, and then oxygen is inhaled. At the same time, along with carbon dioxide, excess oxygen is exhaled.
