PHYSICAL

Respiration requires both gases, which alternately “saddle” the red blood cells. In this case, venous blood is colored dark red with carbon dioxide, and arterial blood is colored bright red with oxygen. The average ratio between the amount of carbon dioxide and oxygen in the body of a healthy person is approximately 3:1 (6% $CO_{2}$ and 2% $O_{2} ).$ Interaction “outside” and “inside”. So, carbon dioxide is necessary for human life. Maintaining a certain level of $CO_{2}$ in the body is also important. And its deficiency and excess are harmful. Too high accumulation of $CO_{2}$ is possible in a bad way ventilated areas: with a large percentage (more than 0.08–0.1%), its level in the body also increases (the consequences of this situation were discussed above). A lack of carbon dioxide in the blood (less than 4%) is also dangerous (see Fig.4). In what cases can such a shortage occur? A typical example is rapid breathing: too much $CO_{2}$ is exhaled and little remains in the body. carbon dioxide, oxygen is firmly “attached” to the red blood cells. And even when there is a lot of oxygen in the blood, it turns out to be bound and poorly supplied to the body’s tissues. If in such a situation you breathe even more often, this will only worsen the situation. What to do? Movement, gymnastics, sports in the air or in a well-ventilated room - all this increases the content of $CO_{2}$. Capillaries expand and even new networks of capillaries are formed, the blood flow increases, oxygen is better separated from the blood. hemoglobin and enters the cells... Let's give another example of the importance of breathing less often: When running, stayers are recommended to hold their breath for as long as possible so that the “second wind” opens and they can run further. Providing first aid “mouth to mouth.” When providing first aid to a person in the event of loss of breathing, one of the effective methods is artificial respiration using the “mouth to mouth” method together with indirect cardiac massage. The rescuer should exhale into the victim’s mouth through gauze or a handkerchief.
