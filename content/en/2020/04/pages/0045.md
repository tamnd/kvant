XLI Tournament of Cities

Spring Tour Objectives

Basic option

8–9 grades

$1. (4)^{1}$

The map of Quadrlandia is a square of $6 \times 6$ cells. Each cell is either a kingdom or a disputed territory. There are only 27 kingdoms, and 9 disputed territories. The disputed territory is claimed by all neighboring kingdoms and only by them (i.e., cells adjacent to the disputed one on the side or top). Could it be that for every two disputed territories a different number of kingdoms are claimed? M. Evdokimov

2. (4) What is the largest number of different integers that can be written in a series so that the sum of every 11 consecutive numbers is equal to 100 or 101? E. Bakaev

3. (4) On the diagonal AC of the rhombus ABCD, a parallelogram APQC is constructed so that point B lies inside it and the side AP is equal to the side of the rhombus. Prove that B is the point of intersection of the altitudes of triangle DPQ. E. Bakaev

4. (5). The integer n is such that the equation $x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2} - xy - yz - zx = n$ has a solution in the integers x, y, z. Prove that then the equation $x^{2} + y^{2} - xy = n$ also has a solution in integers x, y. A.Yuran

5. (5) On board $8 \times 8$ there are two identical pieces in cells a1 and c3 (see figure). Petya and Vasya take turns walking, Petya begins. On his turn, the player selects any piece and moves it either vertically up or horizontally to the right by any number of cells. The winner is the one who makes a move to square h8.

(4) Discussing the winter holidays in class, Sasha said: “Now, after I flew to Addis Ababa, I celebrated the New Year in all possible hemispheres of the Earth, except one!” In what minimum number of places did Sasha celebrate the New Year?
