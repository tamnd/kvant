7. (12) For what k is it possible to paint several cells (a finite number greater than zero) on a white checkered plane black so that on any checkered vertical, horizontal and diagonal there are either exactly k black cells or no black cells at all? A. Dinev, K. Garov, N. Belukhov

Oral round for 11th grade

1. The line contains 2020 natural numbers. Each of them, starting from the third, is divided by both the previous one and the sum of the two previous ones. What is the smallest value that the last number in the line can take? A. Gribalko

2. At the heights $AA_{0}, BB_{0}, CC_{0}$ of the acute unequal triangle ABC, respectively, points $A_{1}, B_{1}, C_{1}$ are marked so that $AA_{1} = BB_{1} = CC_{1} = R,$ where R is the circumradius of triangle ABC. Prove that the circumcenter of triangle $A_{1} B_{1} C_{1}$ coincides with the incircle center of triangle ABC. E. Bakaev 3. 40 cells were marked on the checkered plane. 5. On a sphere of radius 1, a triangle is given, the sides of which are arcs of three different circles of radius 1 with a center at the center of the sphere, having lengths less than $\pi$, and the area is equal to a quarter of the area of ​​the sphere. Prove that four copies of such a triangle can cover the entire sphere. A. Zaslavsky

6. Given an endless supply of white, blue and red cubes. Any N of them are placed in a circle. The robot, standing anywhere in the circle, walks clockwise and, until one cube remains, constantly repeats the following operation: destroys the two closest cubes in front of itself and places behind itself a new cube of the same color if the destroyed ones are the same, and a third color if the destroyed ones are of two different colors. Let's call the arrangement of cubes good if the color of the cube remaining at the very end does not depend on where the robot started from. Let us call N successful if, for any choice of N cubes, all their arrangements are good. Find all successful N. I. Bogdanov
