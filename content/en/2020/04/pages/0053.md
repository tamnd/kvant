2. The resistor begins to melt (10 points) When current is passed through a wire-wound resistor, it heats up, and its temperature varies nonlinearly depending on the flowing current. At low currents, which for typical resistors are on the order of 10 mA, the temperature of the resistor T is practically indistinguishable from the ambient temperature $T_{0}$. When the current increases to a certain limiting value $I_{max}$, the resistor very quickly (almost instantly) heats up to a very high temperature and melts (burns out). Let the resistance of a certain resistor at room temperature and a current of 1 mA be equal to 10 Ohms. It is known that the value of the limiting current for this resistor is $I_{max} = 1,35$ A. Find the resistance of the resistor and the voltage across it at a current $0,8I_{max}$. We can assume that the resistance of the resistor depends linearly on temperature: $R ( T ) = R_{0} ( 1 + \alpha ( T - T_{0} ) )$, where $R_{0}$ is the resistance of the resistor at temperature $T_{0}, \alpha$ is an unknown coefficient. The heat transfer power is proportional to the temperature difference between the resistor and the environment. Changing the resistance of the resistor does not affect the amount of current in the circuit. P. Kryukov

3. Rugby (10 points) Rugby is a team game with a ball. The object of the game is to bring the ball into the opponents' in-goal, which is located behind the goal line (Fig. 6). The player with the ball may pass to another player on his team, but the pass must not be made forward relative to an imaginary line parallel to the goal line and passing through the player (line a on figure). You can only pass sideways or backwards relative to this line. Players of the opposing team have the right to stop the player with the ball. The figure shows a fragment of a field with three rugby players: A and B are players from one team, C is a player from the opposing team. Player C is about to stop player A, so he passes to player B. To make an accurate pass, player A first slowed down, and at the moment of the throw his speed is directed towards the goal line and is equal to v = 2 m s. Let the speed of player B be 8 m/s when moving along any trajectory. The position of players A, B and C on the field shown in figure corresponds to the moment of the throw. We can assume that after the player throws the ball, it moves in a straight line at a constant speed. 1) At what minimum speed (relative to himself) must player A throw the ball for player B to receive the pass and reach the opponent’s in-goal in the minimum time? (2 points) 2) Let player A pass the pass to player B with a relative speed of 6 m s. Player B receives the ball in motion and runs to the goal line in time t. Prove that the minimum value of time $t_{min}$ is achieved when, after player A’s throw, the ball flies parallel to the goal line. (4 points) 3) Find the time $t_{min}$. The statement from point 2) can be used without proof. (4 points) A. Bychkov
