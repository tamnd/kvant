In the course of these studies, a powerful direction in the theory of plasticity was formulated with numerous applications to various branches of industry and technology, which took a leading position in modern mechanics. It is also important to note that during the same difficult war years, original experimental installations were created. A number of problems related to projectile throwing and its interaction with armor were considered by N.A. Slezkin. The problem of strengthening the cables of air barrage balloons and increasing the efficiency of their operation was solved by Kh.A. Rakhmatulin. This led to the creation of the theory of transverse impact on a flexible tensile thread and to the theory of propagation of elastic-plastic waves, which was widely developed after the war. Among other tasks that Kh.A. Rakhmatulin dealt with for the needs of the defense industry and the front, we must mention the aerodynamics of bombs and shells, as well as barrage balloons, the aerodynamics of a parachute, including a particularly complex deployment mode. To increase the maneuverability of tanks and self-propelled guns with long tracks, M.A. Kreines, as part of the design bureau of the Bauman Moscow Higher Technical School, solved the problem of creating circuits of complex regular gear mechanisms that control the movement of the tracks. The complex task of creating effective methods for deciphering radiograms and other enemy reports was solved in the Navy system by a group of Mechanics and Mathematics employees (A.O. Gelfond, G.I. Dvuhsherstov, A.M. Pivovarov, V.G. Cheban and others). Academician N.E. Kochin, based on his theory of a round wing, calculated the movement of an aircraft at low altitude; A.A. Kosmodemyansky was engaged in a similar task. L.I. Sedov, as part of TsAGI, worked on problems of hydrodynamics in relation to the creation of torpedo boats and seaplanes. He carried out original research on low torpedoing from an aircraft with the transition of tor-
