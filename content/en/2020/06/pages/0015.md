Take a raw egg and use a needle to make two holes in it - one larger, the other smaller. By blowing into the smaller one, you can blow out the entire contents of the egg and you will be left with an empty, intact shell in your hands. An egg that was laid at least three weeks ago will float immediately, with part of it protruding from the water. The fact is that as the egg ages, processes of decomposition of the protein and yolk occur in it. These processes are accompanied by the release of hydrogen sulfide, which partially evaporates from the egg through the smallest pores in the shell and partially fills the air cavity in the egg. Therefore, the density of the egg decreases. But you don’t come to the store with a pot of water to check whether the eggs are fresh or not?! You can do it simpler: look at the egg “through the light”: if it shines through, it means it’s fresh, if it’s dark, it means it’s stale. Hydrogen sulfide released in a spoiled egg reduces its transparency. In the old days, stores even had special devices - ovoscopes, in which there were cells for placing the eggs being tested and a light was turned on, which made it possible to see all the tested eggs in the light. ...In conclusion, try the following trick experiment. Take two egg cups and place them close to each other. Place a hard-boiled egg in one of them. Blow strongly into the gap between the egg and the wall of the glass. The egg will jump out of the glass and jump into the next one. If the trick works, try to explain it.
