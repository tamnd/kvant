TRANSLATED FROM LATIN "AB OVO" literally means "from the egg." In a figurative sense, this expression is used when they want to indicate the originality, primacy of something. It was this figurative meaning that the ancients put as the main meaning in the words “ab ovo”. Without knowing it themselves, they thereby resolved in favor of the egg a sophism that had existed since time immemorial: what came first - the chicken or the egg? We will leave this question aside, and will deal with some physical phenomena, choosing as the subject of research... a chicken egg. Who does not remember the fatal cause of the discord between Lilliput and the empire of Blefuscu, described in Gulliver's Travels? It was the decree of the Emperor of Lilliput, ordering all subjects, under pain of death, to break eggs from the sharp end. Gulliver believed that choosing the end from which to break an egg is the master's business. From whatever you want, break it from that. We completely agree with this position, but we still ask ourselves: which end of the egg is easier to break? Solving this problem will help, for example, choose the right tactics in “boiled egg battles,” which used to often arise among children at the Easter table. At the beginning of the war, the problem of increasing the firing efficiency of anti-aircraft guns became very acute. Parameters that complicate the solution of this problem include high maneuverability of the target, which makes it difficult to determine its position, especially in night conditions, differences in aircraft configurations, inaccuracy in knowledge of the dispersion cone of projectile fragments, and others. A rigorous solution to this problem of probability theory leads to complex mathematical calculations and the need to use high-speed computer technology, which was not available at that time. Based on a number of simplifying assumptions, A.N. Kolmogorov proposed an effective calculation scheme that can be used in real combat conditions. On the basis of this theory, which is still taught in military academies today, anti-aircraft firing tables were created. The calculations were carried out in 1942-43 by a small group of employees, graduate students and students, assembled and organized by A.N. Kolmogorov in Moscow. In 1942, he also carried out calculations of the trajectories of aircraft bombs and created tables for bombing from low altitudes at low aircraft speeds in relation to night bombers of the U-2 type. These small training aircraft, based at front-line airfields, turned out to be a formidable weapon for suppressing enemy manpower. Very relevant work on the most advantageous placement of anti-aircraft batteries in the Moscow defense zone was carried out by Professor N.A. Glagolev. In the first years of the war, a very serious problem arose about the strength of artillery shells. According to the standards that existed at that time, projectile bodies were made of alloy steel, with complex heat treatment technology that minimized plastic deformation of the projectile. At the same time, industry evacuated to the east could not supply the required amount of steel and manufacture shells. At the front there was a hunger for shells. On the instructions of the Artillery Committee, A.A. Ilyushin and his colleagues (S.M. Popov and others) conducted extensive research and proved that plastic deformations of the projectile body are not only acceptable, but also useful. This made it possible, after experimental artillery firing, which confirmed the scientist’s conclusions, to switch to mass production of shell bodies from simple steels without heat treatment and steel cast iron, which dramatically improved the supply of shells to the front. All stages of the joint work of the projectile and the barrel were carefully studied, including the process of cutting into the driving belt. Methods for calculating the strength of projectiles and some calculation formulas were included in technological standards and in teaching in the military.
