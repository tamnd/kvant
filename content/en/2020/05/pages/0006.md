With culets with a diameter of 40 microns, pressures of up to 4 million atmospheres can be created - more than in the Earth's core. At the same time, the chamber for the sample has tiny dimensions, and the sample itself, as a rule, is a grain of substance several microns in size, which is tens of times less than the thickness of a human hair (Fig.5). The cell itself, in which the diamond anvils are fixed, is made of metal. It consists of two parts that slide into each other, and the pressure between the culets is increased by gradually tightening the screws holding both parts of the culet together. It is clear that this work is delicate and a hammer has nothing to do on such anvils. Cell preparation, i.e. Aligning the diamonds, preparing the chamber for the sample and putting into it all the components necessary for the experiments is a delicate, almost jewelry-like task, and is carried out by researchers under a microscope.
