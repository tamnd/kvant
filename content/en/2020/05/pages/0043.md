PHYSICS ELECTIVE

Tolya Vtulkin's paradox

S. DVORYANINOV

Part I

Readers are invited to read a science fiction story for future theoretical physicists. Many years have passed since Tolya Vtulkin demonstrated the experimental setup he invented at our school physics club. It seems like it was yesterday... The design is very simple. Its main part is a thin glass tube, but not straight, but curved in the form of a semicircle with radius R. There is also a metal ball 1, the mass of which is m. It is very small and can move freely inside the tube. The tube is mounted on a tripod in a vertical plane in the shape of the letter C (Fig.1). Its upper end B and lower end H are located exactly on the same vertical. When we tried to place the ball at point B, we were unable to do so. And it is clear why: this position of the ball is unstable. And a thin thread 2R long was tied to point B. Other balls, of which there were three, could be tied to this thread. Masses M y

They were different, and the diameters were slightly larger than the diameter of the tube. First, Tolik placed our ball 1 at point H, and tied a light ball 2 to the thread, the mass of which M is significantly less than the mass m. Then he pulled the thread aside and pulled it tight. As a result, the thread is located along the horizontal. This, one might say, was the starting position. – What will happen next if I let go of my ball 2 from my hands? – asked Tolik. The answer was obvious to everyone. The ball in Tolik’s hands has potential energy Mg 2 R. Moving down like a pendulum, it will collide with ball 1 and transfer part of its kinetic energy to it. Ball 1, having some energy, will begin to move up the tube, but, not even reaching the level of point O, it will stop for a moment, then begin to roll down and roll out of the tube. This is how it turned out in experience. For the second time, Tolik tied a heavy ball 2, whose mass M is much greater than m, to a thread. And this situation was understandable. Ball 1, after being hit by ball 2, received such a portion of energy that it was able to rise through the tube to point B, and with a non-zero speed. And indeed, ball 1 flew out of the tube as if from a cannon. After these simple experiments, some began to lose all interest in Tolya’s installation. “Wait, don’t leave, now the most interesting thing will happen,” Tolik exclaimed. – Please look! – with these words, he tied ball 2 with mass m to the thread, moved the thread to the side and pulled it. – What will happen to ball 1, which is now at rest at point H, after I release my ball 2 from my hands? A? What are your guesses? – Tolik addressed everyone. This matter seemed simple to everyone. The potential energy of ball 2 in Tolik’s hands is equal to 2mgR. After the collision, it turns into the energy of ball 1 with mass m. This energy is exactly enough for him to climb up the tube to point B.
