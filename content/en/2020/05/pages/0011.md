anvils of the second stage (Fig.8) – secondary anvils. In such a cell, pressure is transmitted to an even smaller surface, which ensures its further increase according to the same principle as in an apparatus with a piston cylinder. This allowed us to achieve pressures of the order of 1 terapascal (1 TPa $= 10^{7}$ atm.), which correspond to the pressures at the center of the giant planets. Until now, this device, in which the 58th facet of the diamond (culet) continues to retain its important purpose, is a champion in creating static pressures. French researchers proposed modifying the profile of the culet itself, allowing one to incorporate (incorporate) a secondary anvil directly into the classical one, which allows pressures to be reached up to 0.5–0.6 TPa. If you now look at the crown of the diamond, it has also undergone changes in design. German researcher Reinhard Boehler proposed giving it a conical shape, which made it easier to strengthen and increased its stability in the cell; at the same time, the flat platform - the optical window into the high-pressure chamber - has been preserved. Our group went even further - we gave the crown a spherical shape, while placing the center of the culet at the optical center of the resulting lens (Fig.9). This change in the corona made it possible to study the structure of single crystals in anvils at high pressures with simultaneous heating, i.e. in conditions where the X-ray and laser beams are synchronously focused on the sample during the experiment. This is technically very difficult to achieve, since X-ray examination of the structure requires rotation of the crystal being studied. If a laser beam falls on a flat surface, then during rotation the angle of incidence changes and, due to refraction in the diamond, the laser beam loses focus. Due to the spherical design of the crown, the laser beam always hits it at the same angle - perpendicular to the tangent to the spherical surface - and, despite the rotation of the anvil, remains focused on the crystal. In conclusion of the story about the technology and content of modern research in diamond anvils, I would like to note that the overwhelming majority of the total mass of matter, at least in the Solar System, is under high pressure in the bowels of the planets. So studies of the state of matter at high pressures and temperatures are very important steps towards understanding the Universe.
