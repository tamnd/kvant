What happens if in the problem statement the square is replaced with a rectangle? In order not to clutter the drawing, we will remove 'FQK' from consideration. Let's reformulate the problem. Problem 3. Triangles were cut from rectangle ABCD and its perimeter was written inside each of them (Fig.2). Find the perimeter of rectangle ABCD, provided that the perimeter of four Fig. 2 triangles FGHE is equal to 10. Applying the same reasoning with adding and subtracting the perimeters of triangles, we find that the perimeter of rectangle ABCD is equal to 25. But now we want to know: does such a rectangle exist? When discussing problems 1 and 3 with friends, a question arose about the large spread in the values ​​of the perimeters of the quadrilateral FGHE and the square ABCD. Such a design seemed impossible. The perimeter of the quadrilateral FGHE, equal to 10, is suspiciously small compared to the perimeter of the square (rectangle) ABCD. Further calculations confirm the correctness of the stated guess. Let's start our study with a definition. We call a quadrilateral inscribed in a rectangle (square) if each of its vertices lies on one of the sides of the rectangle (square). In our problem, the quadrilateral FGHE is inscribed in the rectangle ABCD.
