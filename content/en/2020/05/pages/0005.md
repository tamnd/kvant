The maximum force limit is determined by the compressive strength of the material from which the parts of the device transmitting pressure to the test sample are made. A modern device with a piston cylinder (Fig.1,a) is the simplest device used to create a pressure of approximately 35–40 thousand atmospheres - the maximum pressure that does not destroy the piston itself, made of tungsten carbide. The principle of operation of this device - increasing pressure when transmitting force from a larger surface to a smaller one - has been known since ancient times. Ancient wine and oil presses worked on the same principle. The ingenious design of modern pressure generating devices reduces the load on their parts and thereby increases the maximum operating pressure when using materials with similar strength characteristics. Thus, pressure up to 250–300 thousand atmospheres is created in laboratories using powerful hydraulic presses and multi-punch devices (Fig.1,b), in which the test substance is compressed not from two sides, as in an apparatus with a piston cylinder, but from several at once. It is clear that the size of the test sample at such pressures is very small - from fractions of a millimeter to several millimeters. To create pressures of several million atmospheres that interest scientists, a new solution was required. This solution was the diamond anvil cell. How is it structured? I believe that not every reader held a jewelry diamond in their hands and not everyone was interested in the peculiarities of its cutting. A diamond (from the French brillant - brilliant, sparkling) is a diamond that has been given a special shape through processing: brilliant cut (Fig.2). There are two main parts to a round diamond: the top and bottom – the crown and the pavilion. The crown has the largest face at the top, the so-called platform, plus another 32 faces, together making up 33 faces of the crown. The pavilion is stretched down and has 24 sides. Sixteen wedges and eight main faces of the pavilion converge at one point - the tenon, the lower apex of the diamond. Thus, a round “perfect cut” diamond has 57 facets. However, scientists need exactly the one that is not necessary in a jewelry diamond - the 58th small facet in the place of the tenon, parallel to the platform. This is a culet. It acts as a diamond anvil (Fig.3). It was the 58-sided diamond that became the first effective anvil in a high-pressure cell, in which there are two such anvils - upper and lower (Fig.4).
