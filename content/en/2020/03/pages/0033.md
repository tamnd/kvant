Another bright surprise is captured in the photograph presented on figure 12. It turns out that at the same depth h in the same Borda tube there are two completely different stable flow regimes: laminar, as in figure 6, and turbulent, as in figure 12! Which regime will arise depends on the starting conditions. If the rubber plug (with a slight lateral taper) is removed from the tube from the outside at the start, a turbulent regime occurs, and if it is in the water column from the inside, a laminar regime occurs. Simple home experiments, like those presented in the article, can not only answer hundreds of your own questions, but also serve as the basis for some new concept of turbulence. And in general, hydraulic or aerodynamics should be perceived as an interesting area for creative thinking. Let's go back to the experiments with the Borda nozzle. If we take not a round tube, but a tube (made of tin) with a rectangular 1:3 cross-section, then the relation 2s = S is preserved. What will the stream look like?
