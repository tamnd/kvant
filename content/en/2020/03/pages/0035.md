A total of 13 strikes were counted, with coincident strikes being perceived as one. What time did the clock strike? 7. 56 mathematicians and an integer number of times fewer physicists went on the hike. They settled in several tents, each with as many people as there were tents. Ten machines produce identical parts weighing 10 g each. One of the machines deteriorated and began producing parts weighing 5 g. How to find a deteriorated machine by simply weighing the parts? 10. Is it possible to place a pan on the edge of a round table so that only 1 3 of the area of ​​its bottom is in contact with the surface of the table? 11. According to a survey conducted in the class, it turned out that 20% of students interested in mathematics also show interest in physics, and 25% of students interested in physics also show interest in mathematics. It's interesting that...

...already at the very early stage of its formation, mathematics interested people as geometry and algebra, as evidenced by rock figure and Cro-Magnon ornaments, as well as numerical amulets reflecting the connection with the seven-day lunar week. ...the level of development of mathematics in Babylon and Ancient Egypt ensured remarkable achievements of surveyors, astronomers and builders - just remember the famous pyramids!

⟦column⟧

...mathematical science in its modern understanding was founded in the period from the 6th to 4th centuries BC in Ancient Greece, and the level of rigor and depth of understanding there and then were such that they were again achieved only in European science of the 19th century. ...in the school of Pythagoras, theoretical discussions about mathematical models of natural phenomena came first - experiments and observations were considered a secondary matter. Zeno believed that mathematics could solve all the problems of physics. Archimedes understood perfectly well that mathematics cannot develop in isolation from the practical needs of people. ...Faraday did not write a single formula more complex than proportion, nevertheless, we owe him the creation of one of the basic concepts of physics - the concept of field; he also introduced a figurative representation of the field - a picture of lines of force. ...the founder and head of the St. Petersburg mathematical school P.L. Chebyshev in the middle of the 19th century for the first time introduced mathematical methods into the theory of mechanisms, pointing to an analytical direction in solving problems of this theory. ...at the beginning of the 20th century, the famous physicist, astronomer and mathematician James Jeans actively protested against the inclusion of group theory in the university training program for physicists, believing that “certainly no physicist will ever need it.” However, group theory soon became almost the first branch of mathematics used in quantum physics.
