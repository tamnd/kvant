Based on oral traditions and, apparently, written evidence that has not reached our time, four ancient historians recorded that the execution of Christ took place on the 13th of Nissan, and his resurrection on the 15th of Nissan, 30 AD. In early Christian communities, the custom was established to annually celebrate the 15th of Nissan of the Jewish calendar as the holiday of Bright Resurrection. In almost all European languages, this day was called “Easter”, very similar to the Hebrew “passover”. Naturally, the Jewish 15th of Nissan fell on different days in the Julian calendar. The oral traditions clarifying this date said that it was after the vernal equinox and the first full moon after that. And in 325, the first Christian council (the congress of all bishops - leaders of the Christian communities of the empire), organized by Emperor Constantine in the city of Nicaea and therefore receiving the name of the Council of Nicaea, established the canon of celebrating Easter on the first Sunday after the first new moon after the vernal equinox. According to the Julian calendar, the spread of Easter day was 36 days - from March 20 to April 25. Accordingly, all the religious days and institutions associated with Easter moved along the calendar - spring and summer fasts, the day of the Holy Spirit, Trinity Day, etc. It is not for nothing that they are called transitional in contrast to the constant ones in the calendar (Nativity of Christ, autumn fasting, the Annunciation, etc.). But when real astronomical events, and above all the spring equinox, began to noticeably (by 10 days) diverge from the canon of celebrating Easter according to the Julian calendar, the need for calendar reform became inevitable.

some cathedrals. At the last of them, a project for changing the calendar prepared by the Italian doctor and astronomer Luigi Lilio was considered. The essence of the project was quite simple. Luigi Lilio (lat. Aloysius Lilius) did not use the apparatus of “continued fractions” (see the article “Calendar and Astronomy”), he simply selected not 1 4, but 97,400 as an addition to 365 days. Thus, over 400 years, the number of leap years should not be 100, as in the Julian calendar, but 97. Period at 400 years old, Luigi Lilio was chosen without any mathematical or astronomical justification, but for reasons of convenience in introducing a new calendar. In order for the calendar to agree well with the astronomical year, it was enough to remove three days from 100 leap years every 400 years. It was only necessary to agree on which three leap years would become simple (without February 29). The logical proposal was to take those years whose first two digits are not a multiple of four. For example, the year 1600 remained a leap year in the reform project, like 1604, 1608,..., 1696, but 1700 should no longer be a leap year. The same applies to 1800 and 1900. And the year 2000 will become a leap year again. And in order to “align” the calendar with astronomical time, it was necessary at some point to “remove” 10 days from the calendar. This was the most difficult thing about the reform for ordinary people to understand. And not just simple ones. To implement the reform throughout the Christian world, an authority was needed above the authorities of the rulers of individual states. In the 1570s, only the Pope, the head of the Catholic denomination of Christianity, had such authority. But despite the council's approval of the reform project, for 14 years Popes Pius IV and Pius V did not dare to take active action. And only Gregory XIII (pope from 1572 to 1583), and even then not immediately after his election, but a month before his death on February 24, 1582, issued a decree (bull) entitled “Among the Most Important” (“Inter gravissimas”).
