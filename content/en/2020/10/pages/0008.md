Carbon nanotubes are created in laboratories in a variety of ways: by tearing pieces off a block of carbon using a laser or electric arc, or by deposition of suitable carbon vapor. In fact, we have been producing them unknowingly for centuries, but it is only with the advent of improved instruments that we have been able to detect these nano-objects. Nanotubes, fullerenes and other graphene derivatives have electrical and optical properties that excite the minds of physicists and in the future may find unique applications, for example, in solar cells.
