Let KL and AD be the diameters of the circle (ABC) (Fig.2). Then AKDL is a rectangle whose center O is the center of the circle. Let us denote by l that of the symmetry axes of the rectangle AKDL that is parallel to AL. Note that AL is the bisector of angle BAC. Hence $P AL^{\cap} ON$, where N is the midpoint of AC. The perpendicular bisectors OM and ON to the sides AB and AC make equal angles with AL, therefore OM and ON are symmetrical about l. This means that points P and $Q KD^{\cap} OM$ are symmetrical with respect to l, thus AKQP is a rectangle, and points A, K, Q, P lie on circle Z with diameter AQ. But since AM A QM ( AB A OM ), the point M also lies on Z. The problem is solved. Note that the new, fifth point Q, diametrically opposite to point A in the circle we need, helped “lay” four points on one circle (this method of proving inscribedness is described in the article by E. Bakaev, P. Kozhevnikov and I. Yakovlev “Perpendiculars and another sign of an inscribed quadrilateral” in «Kvant» No. 5–6 for 2015). Let us present a diagram of another solution (it was invented by some participants in the Caucasian Olympiad, where this problem was proposed). On ray CA we plot a segment CS equal to AB 2 (Fig.3). From the equality CS BM we can deduce the equality of the triangles KCS and KBM and the fact that the points A, K, S, M lie on the same circle (you can read more about this, for example, in the article by A. Polyansky “Sparrows on the guns!” in «Kvant» No. 2, 2012). To solve the problem, it remains to understand that points A, S, P, M lie on the same circle. If R is the projection of P onto AB, then NS RM AC AB 2, where N, as in the first solution, is the midpoint of AC. Therefore, right triangles PNS and PRM are congruent (obtained by rotating around P). Hence SPM NPR 180 q BAC, which completes the proof. Note that in the proof we were again helped by a new, fifth point on the circle, this time point S. In conclusion, we note that a statement similar to the statement of this problem is also true: A, M, L and $ON \cap AK$ lie on the same circle. P. Kozhevnikov

M2612. The playing field is a horizontal checkered stripe $1_{¥} 2019$. Petya and Vasya play such a game. At the beginning of the game, Petya chooses n natural numbers and writes them on the board, after which Vasya places a chip in one of the cells. Then, on each next move, Petya calls a certain number s, which is written on the board, and Vasya moves the chip to s cells, if possible, to the right or to the left - according to his choice. If it is impossible to move the chip neither left nor right by s cells, then the chip remains in place. For what minimum n can Petya play so that the chip reaches all the cells of the strip in a finite number of moves? Answer: n 2.
