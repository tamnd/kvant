Vyacheslav Viktorovich Proizvolov

It’s been a year since the remarkable mathematician Vyacheslav Viktorovich Proizvolov passed away. Readers «Kvant» know him primarily as a challenging composer with a unique flair and taste. From the late 1990s to the mid-2000s, Vyacheslav Viktorovich was in charge of the Problem Book «Kvant». An unforgettable, elegant author's handwriting is always visible in his bright tasks. In this and upcoming issues we will recall some of his remarkable tasks. At a seminar for mathematics teachers dedicated to the memory of Vyacheslav Viktorovich, his daughter, mathematics teacher Alexandra Vyacheslavovna Drobotova, spoke. Here is the text of her story.

VYACHESLAV VIKTOROVICH was born shortly before the war, in 1939. One of his first memories and impressions was surprise: he was sitting on his grandfather’s lap, and his grandfather handed him a piece of plywood and said: “Eat, grandson!” It turned out it was a waffle! The first steps in the scientific field can hardly be called successful. Dad told how he went to first grade: he left the house, hid his briefcase in the neighboring yard and went about his business. By lunchtime I would pick up my briefcase and return home. When the secret became clear, loud retribution followed. And then the learning process developed in approximately the same spirit. Dad became interested in mathematics in a pioneer camp, after the 8th grade. A friend suggested solving several logical problems, Slava solved it and, fascinated, asked: “What is this?” It turned out to be mathematics. As a result, in the graduating class, Andrei Nikolaevich Kolmogorov noticed my dad’s work at the Moscow Mathematical Olympiad. Participants were given several tasks. Having started one of them, dad did not notice or did not see one of the conditions, and the result was a problem with incomplete initial data. For each pair of adjacent same-color cells a and b, we place a wall (unit segment) on their common side. After “building walls”, the mottled path becomes just a path (along neighboring cells) that does not go through the wall. Since no 2 u 2 square consists of four cells of the same color, it is impossible for 3 or 4 walls to converge at one lattice node. This means that at each vertex no more than two walls converge, so the walls form several (non-extendable) pairwise disjoint broken lines (some of which may be closed). If some mottled path connects some cell of the lower row with some cell of the upper row, then its length is at least n - a contradiction. This means that the walls must separate the cells of the lower row from the cells of the upper row. Consequently, some (non-closed) broken line P, consisting of walls, must begin at some node A on the left side of this table, and end at some node B on the right side. At the same time, we note that A and B are not corners (corner nodes) of the table (since no walls meet at the corner). Also note that the first and last links of the broken line P are horizontal. Similar to the broken line P, we define the broken line Q going from the top side of the table to the bottom side. In this case, the first and last links of the broken line Q are vertical. Obviously, the broken lines P and Q are different. It follows from the previous that they must intersect at some internal node of the table, which contradicts our conclusion that various broken lines from the walls do not intersect. The resulting contradiction completes the solution of the problem. N. Belukhov
