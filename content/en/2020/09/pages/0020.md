MATHEMATICAL WORLD

Vyacheslav Viktorovich Proizvolov

It’s been a year since the remarkable mathematician Vyacheslav Viktorovich Proizvolov passed away. Readers «Kvant» know him primarily as a challenging composer with a unique flair and taste. From the late 1990s to the mid-2000s, Vyacheslav Viktorovich was in charge of the Problem Book «Kvant». An unforgettable, elegant author's handwriting is always visible in his bright tasks. In this and upcoming issues we will recall some of his remarkable tasks. At a seminar for mathematics teachers dedicated to the memory of Vyacheslav Viktorovich, his daughter, mathematics teacher Alexandra Vyacheslavovna Drobotova, spoke. Here is the text of her story.

VYACHESLAV VIKTOROVICH was born shortly before the war, in 1939. One of his first memories and impressions was surprise: he was sitting on his grandfather’s lap, and his grandfather handed him a piece of plywood and said: “Eat, grandson!” It turned out it was a waffle! The first steps in the scientific field can hardly be called successful. Dad told how he went to first grade: he left the house, hid his briefcase in the neighboring yard and went about his business. By lunchtime I would pick up my briefcase and return home. When the secret became clear, loud retribution followed. And then the learning process developed in approximately the same spirit. Dad became interested in mathematics in a pioneer camp, after the 8th grade. A friend suggested solving several logical problems, Slava solved it and, fascinated, asked: “What is this?” It turned out to be mathematics. As a result, in the graduating class, Andrei Nikolaevich Kolmogorov noticed my dad’s work at the Moscow Mathematical Olympiad. Participants were given several tasks. Having started one of them, dad did not notice or did not see one of the conditions, and the result was a problem with incomplete initial data. Dad spent all the time allotted for the Olympics on this task and conducted a whole study. See-

Well, it was lucky, because he received an incentive bonus for solving this problem. In 1956, Vyacheslav Viktorovich entered the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics at Moscow State University. During the first years he worked with Andrei Nikolaevich Kolmogorov and Pavel Sergeevich Alexandrov. Or rather, of course, academics worked with him. Somewhere around this time, the so-called Proizvolov theorem appeared - something related to topology. When I pestered Vyacheslav Viktorovich to explain the essence of the theorem to me, he frowned and waved his hand. In the third year, it was time to choose a supervisor.
