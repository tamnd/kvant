It would seem, how does some extraneous circle help? If its center were given, then the construction of a perpendicular would be obvious (see, for example, lines 3 and 4 in method 2). And since we don’t have a center at our disposal, we can only ignore this circle. Right? No, not like that! Paradoxically, the presence of even an “extraneous” circle gives new options for constructing a perpendicular straight line.

Method 4. Inscribed quadrilateral

The circle given in the condition is drawn in blue (Fig.4). Let us draw a line AB, as well as a circle with center A through point B. Direct AF is the desired one. Proof. The quadrilateral ABDF is a cyclic quadrilateral, so the sum of the opposite angles is 180q. Since BDE rests on the diameter BE, it is straight. Therefore, BAF is also direct.

Method 6. Two circles and one straight line

Again we start from an arbitrary center of the circle, but draw the circle itself through point A (Fig.6). Let D be the second point of its intersection with the given (blue) circle. Now we construct (second line) a circle with center B and radius BD. This circle will intersect the two previous circles at points E and F. Then the straight line EF intersects the first of the circles we have constructed at a point G for which the angle GAB is a straight line.
