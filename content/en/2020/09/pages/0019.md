Many applied aspects of “getting out of the woods” can be found in the book [6]. Are there any unsolved problems in this science? Certainly! They are associated both with different forms of forests and with other formulations of the problem itself. For example, in practice we are not so much interested in a path that always provides an exit, but rather one that provides an exit with a high probability. And this path can be much shorter! Sometimes the problem is posed to minimize the average exit time, i.e. mathematical expectation.

In conclusion, we invite you to try your hand at solving a few more problems. 7. The forest has the shape of a unit circle. Prove that the shortest path out of the forest is a segment of length 2. 8. The forest has the shape of a circle with radius 10, and a person is in the forest at a distance of 1 from its border. a) Does the path from problem 5 provide a way out of the forest? b) Is there a shorter way? 9. The forest has the shape of a right angle, and the person is at a distance of 1 from its top. Is it possible to leave the forest after walking a distance of no more than 3? 10. The forest has the shape of a square. Is it possible to exit it by traveling a distance less than the diagonal of the square? 11. The forest has the shape of a regular triangle. Is it possible to exit it by traveling a distance less than the side of the triangle? In the last four problems, the authors do not know which path is the shortest.
