PROBLEM WORKER

Next, we will prove that there is always a motley path of length at least n. Let's assume the opposite. For each pair of adjacent same-color cells a and b, we place a wall (unit segment) on their common side. After “building walls”, the mottled path becomes just a path (along neighboring cells) that does not go through the wall. Since no 2 u 2 square consists of four cells of the same color, it is impossible for 3 or 4 walls to converge at one lattice node. This means that at each vertex no more than two walls converge, so the walls form several (non-extendable) pairwise disjoint broken lines (some of which may be closed). If some mottled path connects some cell of the lower row with some cell of the upper row, then its length is at least n - a contradiction. This means that the walls must separate the cells of the lower row from the cells of the upper row. Consequently, some (non-closed) broken line P, consisting of walls, must begin at some node A on the left side of this table, and end at some node B on the right side. At the same time, we note that A and B are not corners (corner nodes) of the table (since no walls meet at the corner). Also note that the first and last links of the broken line P are horizontal. Similar to the broken line P, we define the broken line Q going from the top side of the table to the bottom side. In this case, the first and last links of the broken line Q are vertical. Obviously, the broken lines P and Q are different. It follows from the previous that they must intersect at some internal node of the table, which contradicts our conclusion that various broken lines from the walls do not intersect. The resulting contradiction completes the solution of the problem. N. Belukhov

$Ф2613.^{1}$ To study the cyclone, which was moving north at an average speed of 20 km/h, two unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) with different flight missions were sent from the same airfield. Each device had to, remaining at a distance of 20 km horizontally from the center of the cyclone and moving at a constant speed relative to the surrounding air of 20 m/s at a certain altitude (the same for both devices), fly around the center of the cyclone. One device is clockwise (when viewed from above), and the second is counterclockwise. At the moment of launch, the distance from the airfield to the center of the cyclone was exactly 20 km. After 15 hours of flight, both UAVs almost simultaneously landed at the same airfield, located north of the airfield from which they took off, and throughout the flight, each strictly followed the flight program stored in the computer memory. How many circles around the center of the cyclone did one of the UAVs make if the second always moved in a straight line relative to the ground and did not make a single circle?

If one of the vehicles moved in a straight line, this means that its speed relative to the ground was the same as the speed of the cyclone center, i.e. 20 km h. In other words, in a translationally moving frame of reference, in which the center of the cyclone is motionless, this apparatus was motionless. Therefore, at a distance of 20 km from the center of the cyclone, the wind speed in this reference frame is 20 m s. And the speed of another device in the reference system associated with the cyclone is equal to the sum of the relative and transfer speeds, i.e. equal to 40 m s. In 15 hours, this device flies such a distance that it can circle the center of a cyclone
