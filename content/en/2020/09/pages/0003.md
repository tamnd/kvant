HOW TO GET OUT OF THE FOREST? WHAT a strange question! If you have a navigator or, at worst, a map and compass, then the problem can be solved simply. But if there is no card and the phone is dead? Then we need to go straight all the time - someday we will get out. However, this strategy is dangerous: if the direction is chosen poorly, then you can go very far into the forest. What to do? Let's try to formulate the problem precisely. So: 1. We don’t know where in the forest we are, and we don’t know the directions. 2. We still know something about the forest - say, its area, shape, etc. 3. We can go along any pre-selected route. For example, walk exactly in a circle with a given center and a radius of 10 km, or, say, walk 5 km in a given direction, then turn 60q and walk 3 km, etc. Question: how should we go so that, having followed our route, we are sure to exit the forest, regardless of the initial position and initial direction of movement? Do we know that the diameter of the forest does not exceed 10 km? The diameter of a figure is the distance between the furthest points of the figure. Thus, any limited figure has a diameter, and not just $круга.^{1}$ This means that we know that the forest is not very large: the distance between any two of its points does not exceed 10 km. Solution. We walk 10 km in a straight line. That's it, we will definitely come out of the forest - either by the end of the path, or even earlier. Otherwise, the forest will contain a segment 10 km long inside itself, which means that the diameter of the forest will be more than 10 km. So is it that simple? We go in a straight line at random - and that’s the whole “strategy”? Yes. Moreover, this path is optimal. This means that no path less than 10 km long can guarantee a way out of the forest. In fact, we will walk along any path less than 10 km long. Let's denote its length by d. Now let's cover this path with a very narrow strip. If A and B are any pair of points on our path, then the length of the segment AB does not exceed the length of the path section from A to B (since the shortest path between two points is in a straight line), and therefore AB d d. So, the diameter of our path, if considered as a flat figure, does not exceed d. Since d is 10 km, if the strip is narrow enough, its diameter will also be less than 10 km. Now let’s imagine that the forest is our strip. The diameter of the forest is less than 10 km, but we never left it (after all, the strip contains our entire path inside it). Of course, it was a simple situation. But it's more complicated.
