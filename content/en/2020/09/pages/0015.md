The forest has the shape of an infinite strip of width 1. What is the shortest way out of such a forest? Solution. We will follow Zalgaller's article. We will need two auxiliary statements. We will call a curve without self-intersections an arc. It is clear that the shortest path must be sought precisely among the arcs. We will call an arc convex if it, together with the segment connecting its ends, bounds a convex figure. Lemma 1. There is a path of minimal length. It is a convex arc. What is there to prove? Well, of course there is a shortcut, that’s obvious! Unfortunately, in some minimum-maximum problems the optimal solution is not achieved. Moreover, the existence of a solution is sometimes the most difficult to prove. But you cannot not prove it - otherwise you can come to absurd conclusions (see several examples in [2]). This task is no exception. Therefore Lemma 1 is very important. But we will not prove it here, but will accept it without proof and refer the interested reader to the article [3]. Let us now find out what properties the shortest curve should have, let's call it L. We will need the concept of a supporting straight convex figure. This is a straight line that has common points with a given figure, but the entire figure lies on one side of the straight line. For example, the reference lines of a circle are tangents. And the supporting lines of a triangle are three lines containing its sides, as well as lines passing through its vertices and not having other points with the triangle. Lemma 2. The ends A, B of the shortest curve L do not coincide with each other, and L is located between the perpendiculars to the segment AB at its ends. Proof.
