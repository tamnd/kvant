The shock wave causes the plasma in the bunch to move in an orderly manner, and at the front there is an intense conversion of the energy of the ordered motion of the plasma into the energy of chaotic motion of particles. And if the particle is at the front of the shock wave, it acquires energy, i.e. accelerates. What happens next? The particle is carried away by the plasma flow from the shock wave front “downstream”. If the particle returns to the front of the shock wave, its energy will increase even more. Can a particle return? Yes, and more than once. It happens like this. A magnetic field is frozen into the plasma clot; it is inhomogeneous due to the inhomogeneous density of the clot. In some regions, the inhomogeneous magnetic field is directed in such a way that it deflects a flying particle towards the front of the shock wave, and the particle returns to the front. The deflection of a particle is proportional to the magnitude of the field and inversely proportional to the energy of the particle - the larger it is, the weaker the deflection. With any of these methods of acceleration, the particle simultaneously gains and loses energy. Energy losses occur when the particle is in a region penetrated by a magnetic field with curved lines of force. The fact is that if the field line bends, then the particle has a velocity component perpendicular to the magnetic field. And then the trajectory of the particle is curved under the influence of the Lorentz force. A particle that moves along a curved path emits energy, and the energy of the particle itself decreases. Lines of force can be straight, but only on limited segments. And even if the particle moved along a straight line of force, there would still be energy losses, and here’s why. The section where the line is straight ends, and then the line of force bends. And in a field with such lines of force, the trajectory of the particle is curved, the particle, moving, emits energy and its energy decreases. Near supermassive black holes, particles are still accelerated to ultra-high energies. This was shown by a theoretical analysis of processes in which a particle simultaneously gains and loses energy. Both ways of accelerating particles near a black hole - through an induced electric field and through the Fermi mechanism - have been identified theoretically, “on paper”. Observations will show which of them is more important in nature.
