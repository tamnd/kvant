MATHEMATICAL

It is theoretically possible that this is still not a complete set of all options. I personally find it hard to believe that the jury prepared a separate option for each of the 48 participants, especially since their number was not known in advance. The idea of ​​a multi-choice Olympiad was to eliminate the possibility of cheating - even then, at the very first Olympiad, attention was already paid to this, which, however, is not surprising. However, eight options should have been enough - Alexandrova recalls that the participants were seated in several classrooms in the main building of the university. Apparently, the organizers wanted to ensure that in each room there were schoolchildren with different options for conditions - then such an organization of the oral round looks quite reasonable. Of course, there is a non-zero probability that the number of options could be more, say ten or twelve. In such a case, however, it would be difficult to explain why the publication did not contain a complete set of problems. As for the problem of coloring a cube, it cannot be completely ruled out that it was included in the list of training tasks sent to schools, based on the solution of which schools formed their “teams” for the second round (this school stage was quite deservedly called the first round of LMO-1934). Such an assumption could theoretically explain the recollection of one of the participants that this problem was the very first problem of the Olympiad. However, I honestly admit that this hypothesis does not seem plausible to me. In article [2] it is mentioned that the list of problems of the first round consisted of “90 problems from various sections of algebra, geometry and trigonometry”; by the way, many of them are also listed in the appendix to this article under the heading “Exercises for students”; another list of training tasks is given in the previous issue (1934, No. 3) of the same magazine “Mathematics and Physics in Secondary School”; There is not a single non-standard task on these lists.

WORLD

⟦column⟧

If we were talking about the second round of the Olympiad, then it, like the third round, was compiled using a multivariate method, which again excluded the possibility of using such a task. It is possible, however, that the question about the cube was one of the additional problems that were offered to schoolchildren who had solved all the problems in the main version. Be that as it may, we can now say with fairly serious grounds that the question of the problems of the main options for the final round of the first city mathematical Olympiad in history has practically been resolved. Fans of mathematical competitions can finally get acquainted with many problems of the Leningrad Mathematical Olympiad of 1934. And who knows, maybe among those who read these lines there will be descendants of Leningrad schoolchildren, teachers, students or professors, in whose long-forgotten family archives problems or photographs from Olympiads of other pre-war years are still stored. Let's hope that we won't have to wait almost another ninety years until the next publication dedicated to the history of the Leningrad City Mathematical Olympiads. However, at worst, we always have the option of a time machine.
