But since the linear velocities of different points on the surface of each cone are different, there is slipping between the points of the cones touching each other and sliding friction forces must act. Indeed, the points of the driving cone, located further from its axis, have higher linear velocities and should be ahead of the points of the driven cone with which they contact. And the points of the driving cone, located closer to its axis, have lower linear velocities and should lag behind the points of the driven cone with which they contact. This means that at different points on the surface of each cone, the friction force acts differently. The driving cone is subject to a braking friction force from the driven cone at those points that are ahead of the driven points, and an accelerating friction force at those points that lag behind the driven points. However, the total frictional force acting on the driving cone must be braking, since it rotates uniformly when the moment of external forces acts on it. From the condition of equality of the moment of external forces and the moment of the total friction force, one can find the points of the cones that have the same linear velocities (section of equal velocities).
