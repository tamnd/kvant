Physics in the language of functions and graphs

Even our very distant ancestors understood that the phenomena around them were interconnected. But how can this connection be precisely expressed? Even in Ancient Greece, scientists, having come close to the concept of functional dependence, were unable to formulate it. It took almost another fifteen hundred years for the study of correspondences between variable quantities, the study of the trajectories of moving points, the introduction of coordinates and letter designations to lead mathematicians to the ability to write down the laws of nature in the form of functions, as well as to represent them graphically. Thus, physics (and not only it!) was provided an invaluable service, which significantly strengthened its research potential. Unfortunately, schoolchildren often neglect the use of graphs. But in vain! Graphs expand our understanding of physical processes, make them visual, and sometimes make it much easier to solve problems. Let's try to convince you of this in this issue of Kaleidoscope.
