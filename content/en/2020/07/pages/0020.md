It was clear that it was not possible to unambiguously resolve this small and, generally speaking, not very significant discrepancy. We could only hope that when our distant descendants invent a time machine, one of them would visit Leningrad in the spring of 1934, and then grateful humanity would finally have at its disposal a complete list of problems for the first city mathematical Olympiad in world history. However, almost thirty years later, when preparing a reissue of the LMO collection, I undertook additional searches on the Internet - mainly with the goal of finding some photographs from those years. And quite soon (in January 2020), almost by pure chance, I came across the release of the collection “Mathematical Enlightenment”, published in Moscow in 1935. This book published an article by I.I. Chistyakov [2] under the long official title “Mathematical Olympiad of the Leningrad State University named after. A.S. Bubnova.” Needless to say, I was pleasantly stunned and delighted. Firstly, Chistyakov indicated the exact date of the Olympiad (Wednesday, April 18). Secondly, he described in more detail the organization of the Olympiad, and also reported exactly how many schoolchildren took part in the second and third (final) rounds - 307 and 48 people, respectively. Thirdly, and this was the most important thing, eleven tasks of the Olympiad were listed in the appendix to this note. For the first time in many years, at least some problems of the first mathematical Olympiad in the USSR were found! The issue of attribution was also completely omitted from the article, but in this case we were talking about work written in hot pursuit of the competition that had just passed. It was natural to assume that either the author of the article himself or the editors of the collection received a list of tasks that was impossible to achieve.

However, the very fact of discovering this note served as an impetus for me to further search. Less than a few months had passed, in mid-May 2020 (at least there was some benefit from the sad coronavirus quarantine), while continuing to “wander” through the vastness of the world wide web, I discovered a copy of the journal “Mathematics and Physics in Secondary School” (the predecessor of the journal “Mathematics at School”) for 1934, in which a similar article by the same author was published [1]. The title of this article was more lapidary (“Results of the Leningrad Mathematical Olympiad”), and with the exception of some minor details, it practically did not differ from the article [2]. However, in this version, immediately after the main text there was an appendix containing a set of problems for the third round of LMO-1934! In the memoirs already mentioned above, M.L. Alexandrova wrote that each participant in the final round of the Olympiad received two problems - the first in algebra, the second in geometry. This is exactly what this appendix to article [1] looks like - it consists of eight options for two tasks. It is necessary to point out that for some unknown reason (didn’t fit? Lost? accidentally missed while typing?) task 5(a) is missing - however, it is possible that this task is contained in the list from the appendix to the article [2] - see below tasks 1934.X, 1934.Y and 1934.Z.
