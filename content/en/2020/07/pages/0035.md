It's interesting that...

...ancient science left very few curveballs for future generations. The main ones are the circle, as well as the ellipse, hyperbola and parabola, which appeared in the works of the ancient Greek scientist Apollonius of Perga. ...one of the first attempts to construct a system of rectilinear coordinates is associated with the name of the 14th century French mathematician and physicist (and economist!) Nicolas Oresme. ...Oresme's repeatedly published treatise may have influenced future scientists, including Descartes, who clearly expressed the idea of ​​the functional dependence of variables and the geometric expression of this dependence, i.e. function graphics. ...the problem of finding the displacement accomplished by uniformly accelerated motion was first solved graphically in the 17th century by Galileo Galilei.

⟦column⟧

...Galileo believed that the chain line along which flexible inextensible threads and freely suspended chains sag coincides with a parabola. This statement was refuted by Huygens, who also found out that a cycloid, and not a circle, as Galileo believed, has the property that a body sliding along it without friction oscillates with a period independent of the initial position. ...comets moving from afar into the Solar System and D-particles flying past the atomic nucleus during its bombardment move along hyperbolas around the Sun; the law of radioactive decay reflects the exponential nature of this process; Many galaxies, including ours, are twisted in logarithmic spirals. It is almost impossible to observe these dependencies “live”... ...but in some cases the graphs of functions are visible, one might say, with your own eyes. Thus, a metal ruler, compressed at the ends, bends along a sinusoid (another example is in Microexperiment). In a narrow gap formed by two vertical glass plates lowered into water, a hyperbola of the free surface of the liquid clearly appears. By tilting a cylindrical glass glass filled with water to the middle, we get an ellipse. There are many such examples, try to find them around you.
