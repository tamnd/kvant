PROBLEM WORKER

"QUANTA"

Can students come up with a strategy that will guarantee they all pass?

We will describe how students can come to an agreement so that everyone can pass the exam. Let's look at a specific student, Vasily. Let Vasily imagine that the teacher will give him a “fail” grade, and everyone who answers after him will give a “pass” grade. Then, as an answer, Vasily will name the total number of “pass” grades received by the students in this case. In other words, if k students received a “failed” grade, then Vasily will name the number 99 - k. Let us prove that by adhering to this strategy, all students will pass the exam. If all students receive a pass grade, then they have achieved their goal. Otherwise, consider the last student Peter, who received a “failed” grade. Since everyone after Peter received a “pass” grade, Peter answered the question correctly. Thus, the inspector will replace all grades with “passed”. Comment. Let us show that the students' strategy is the only possible one. Let's assume that the first few students (perhaps no one) adhere to the specified strategy, and Vasily is the first to name a number that does not correspond to the described strategy. Then the teacher can give him a “failed” grade, and give everyone after him a “pass” grade. In this case, Vasily gave the wrong answer, and everyone who received the “failed” grade (they answered before Vasily) named a number that is greater than the actual number of “passed” grades. D. Afrizonov

M2580. Given a convex quadrangular pyramid with vertex S and base ABCD, and there is a sphere inscribed in this pyramid (i.e. located inside the pyramid and touching all its faces). The pyramid was cut along the edges SA, SB, SC, SD and the faces SAB, SBC, SCD, SDA were bent outward onto the plane ABCD so that the polygon AKBLCMDN was obtained, as shown in figure 1. Prove that the points K, L, M, N lie on the same circle.

Let us make a homothety with center at S, which takes the inscribed sphere to a sphere tangent to the plane ABCD on the opposite side; thus we obtain an excentric sphere (Fig.2). Let us denote the points of tangency of this excentric sphere with the planes ABCD, ABS, BCS, CDS and DAS by $T, T_{K}, T_{L}, T_{M}$ and $T_{N}$, respectively. Let us show that KT = LT = MT = NT, i.e., that K, L, M and N lie on the same circle with center T. Note that the quadrilaterals $SBT_{K} A$ and KBTA are symmetrical with respect to the outer bisector plane of the dihedral angle between faces ABCD and ABS; it follows that $ST_{K} = KT$. Likewise, $ST_{L} = LT, ST_{M} = MT$ and $ST_{N} = = NT$. In addition, $ST_{K} = ST_{L} = ST_{M} = = ST_{N}$ as segments of tangents to the excircle sphere drawn from S. This means that the segments $ST_{K}$, KT, $ST_{L}$, LT, $ST_{M}$, MT, $ST_{N}, NT$ are equal, and the problem is solved. Comment. A similar statement is true for the described n-gonal pyramid for any n. The above decision remains in effect. In addition, the general case can be reduced by descending n Æ n - 1 to the case n = 4.
