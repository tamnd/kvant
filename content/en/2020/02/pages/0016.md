For rhythmic reasons, one would like to place these stresses in the middle of the interval between strong syllables, and this means that the four-syllable size tends to be independent. Non-independent four-syllable meter is a very common phenomenon. If you doubt its existence (as some poets doubted - polemics with them constitute the main content of the above-cited article by L.E. Lyapina), ask any student of a music school - and he will tell you that the four-beat meter is found very often in music, in particular in songs: I was in the field but there was no grass... Oh, how I love my little cow... (all this is a dependent (4,3)-meter, it, like three-part, very typical for Russian song). If this doesn’t convince you, look through Pushkin. Here is a sample of (4,2)-meter from his youthful poems: In the caves of Helicun I was never born; In the name of Apollo, Tibellus christened... Pushkin’s famous “Winter Road” (On the boring winter road...) also seems to me to be written in four-syllable meter; however, the composer Sviridov chose to read these verses as two-syllable. Moreover, often (one might say, as a rule) only part of the poem is subordinated to a four-syllable meter, and transitions from a four-syllable rhythm to a two-syllable one and back can carry a semantic or figurative load. This can be easily seen, for example, in Nekrasov’s poem “Who Lives Well in Rus'” (non-independent (4,2)-meter). Five-syllable sizes. And indeed, many lines of this kind are subordinated to the five-syllable meter: Oh, you guys ecu, good fellow... or That’s not the same with his wife, then not brbt with his sister - A good fellow with a red damsel. A more consistent five-syllable meter (although also not without digressions) can be found in fakes of the tale style, in stylizations written by professional poets, for example, in Lermontov’s “Merchant Kalashnikov”: How the daredevils and Moscow fighters gathered, gathered, On the Moscow River, for a club fight... And the Tsar came with his friends. With $боя^{’}$ frames and guards... All this, by the way, is (5.3)-size. It would seem that everything is clear. But let me give you a completely different example. In the last year of his life, Gumilyov wrote the poem “Dragon”, consisting of 12 parts of 5 quatrains each. I will give the first part in full, describing the sunrise on the ocean shore, and you will determine the size. Due to the fresh waves of the ocean, the Red Bull raised its horns. And the fallow deer of the fog ran under the rocky shores.

Under the rocky shores, in the noisy damp shadow, they settled on the moss like silvery pearls.
