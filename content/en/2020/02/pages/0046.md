Armed with the Simson straight line, let us return to the proof of Theorem 1. On figure 8 we see that points $A_{1}, B_{1}$ and P are projections of point I onto lines containing the sides of the triangle A c B c C. Since points $A_{1}, B_{1}$ and P lie on the same straight line, then by Theorem 2 point I lies on the circumcircle of the triangle A c B c C. Since CI is the bisector of angle C, and I is is the midpoint of the arc A c IB c, then P is the midpoint of A c B c (IP is the height in the isosceles triangle A c IB c ). Thus, the use of the Simson straight line allows us to take a fresh look at the solution to Problem 1. Note that such a hidden Simson straight line in problems is not such a rare occurrence.

A little history

Here we can stop and rest a little. It turns out that task 1 has its own history. Recently, the first author of this article came across an old issue of the journal “Mathematics at School” for 1996. It turned out that in the same year at the All-Russian Olympiad in grade 10, our problem 1 was given in different notations. The magazine presented two solutions to this problem. The first - vector, purely computational - took up about half a page of printed text, the second used the properties of projective transformation and double relations. I wondered if there were more accessible geometric solutions. The fruits of these thoughts are before you. Now let's go back from 1996 more than 20 years ago, to 1973. So, “Problem book «Kvant», problem M178, author – Igor Fedorovich Sharygin. Here Problem 1 is given in a more general form: instead of the center of the inscribed circle, you can consider any point on the bisector! It turns out that the circle itself has nothing to do with it. For convenience, we give the formulation in our notation. Task 2 (generalization of task 1). From some point T of the bisector of angle C of triangle ABC, we drop perpendiculars $TA_{1}, TB_{1}$ and $TC_{1}$ to its sides BC, CA and AB, respectively. Let P be the intersection point of lines $TC_{1}$ and $A_{1} B_{1}$ (Fig.9). Prove that CP bisects side AB.

Exercise. Solve this problem. Note. Use task 1.
