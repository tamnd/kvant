CALENDAR

But for intermediate cycles - the seasons themselves (in our opinion - quarters) and their shares (months), nature gave another cycle - the change of phases of the Moon. Today we call them new moon, first quarter, full moon and last quarter. In the quarter phases we see half of the lunar disk illuminated by the Sun. In partial phases, the Moon appears to us as a curvilinear circular segment, similar to a sickle. For many peoples, the word “month” appeared for the period of complete change of lunar phases, which at the same time meant the name of the Moon itself. The calendar month has become more convenient than the quarter (season). For example, in spring we can distinguish the time of rains, the time of sowing and the time of the first weeding, and in the summer - the time of haymaking, the time of harvest and the time of fruit ripening. It is convenient to divide the month into four parts (coinciding with the lunar phases), each of which has 7 days. This is where the habitual counting of days by week lurks! Such a spontaneous weekly-lunar calendar is usually called simply lunar. But when the ability to count to a hundred, and then to a thousand, arose, this rough calendar began to be refined. First of all, this related to the ratio of the lengths of the year and months. Among the people, those who kept count of days, months and years began to stand out. Even two hundred years ago, it was already possible to find peoples on earth whose calendar was kept by notches on a tree or by notches on a soft stone (exactly as Robinson did on his uninhabited island). It is possible that it was precisely such people who were priests and, along with keeping track of time, organized the entire spiritual life, predicted events, created traditions and customs, and directed all the economic activities of their tribe.

Ancient astronomical achievements

The priests were the first to draw attention to the inaccuracy of the relationship between the solar and lunar periods. It would seem that according to a simple calculation, there should have been exactly 12 lunar months in a year - 4 seasons of 3 months each

It turned out that the ratio of the period of the lunar cycle of phase changes to days is not an integer, but is equal to approximately 29.5, therefore, the duration of each phase is not exactly 7 days. These non-integer relationships of natural periodic processes are the main reason that at least a dozen different calendars operated on our planet, in which the main task was to coordinate the 12 lunar months with the annual solar cycle. Such calendars are called lunisolar and solar. But for them it was necessary to know the solar and lunar periods of the day quite accurately.
