So, for the numbers allocated to figure 7, we have: 42 = ( 25 + 48 + 63 + 32 ) 4.) For long Over the millennium of development of mathematics, many ways of multiplying numbers have been invented. The Italian mathematician of the late 15th and early 16th centuries, Luca Pacioli, in his treatise on arithmetic, gives 8 different methods of multiplication. Here are two of them, in my opinion, the most interesting. In the first, which is called the “small castle” (Fig.8), the digits of the upper number, starting with the highest, are multiplied in turn by the lower number and written in a column with the required number of zeros added, and then the results are added up. The advantage of this method over the usual one is that the digits of the most significant digits are determined from the very beginning, and this can be important for rough calculations. The second method has an equally romantic name: “jealousy” (or “lattice multiplication”). A lattice is drawn into which the results of intermediate calculations are then entered (in fact, the required numbers from the multiplication table). This lattice is a rectangle divided into squares, each of which is divided by a diagonal (Fig.9). “Such a lattice on-
