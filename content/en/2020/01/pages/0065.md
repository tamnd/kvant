But it may also happen that in the warm air flow droplets of fog evaporate and the fog dissipates. All this can be observed in a simple experiment that requires a coffee pot, a frying pan and a small piece of rubber tubing. We insert the tube tightly into the spout of the coffee pot, pour water into it and place it on the stove. When the water boils, a white stream of water mist will begin to escape from the tube. Let's bring (using tweezers) the end of the tube to a cold frying pan standing on the adjacent burner. The fog will rise above it and partially settle in drops on the cold surface of the metal. But if you heat the pan, the fog will disappear.
