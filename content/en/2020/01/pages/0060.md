KVAN T 2020/№1

We are used to saying that the Moon revolves around the Earth. In fact, both the Moon and the Earth rotate around a common center. If this rotation did not exist, then under the influence of gravity the Moon would fall to the Earth (more precisely, the Earth and the Moon would fall on each other - after all, the Moon also attracts the Earth). How does this rotation happen? Let's imagine that the masses of the Earth and the Moon are the same. Then they will move around a point that lies in the middle of the segment connecting their centers. Now let's mentally “increase” the mass of the Earth. Then the center of rotation will shift towards the Earth. Since the mass of the Earth is much greater than the mass of the Moon (81 times), the center $вращения^{С}$ turns out to lie inside the globe (but it is necessarily displaced relative to the center of the Earth$^{О).}$ This rotation significantly affects the pattern of tides. Water recedes from the center of rotation. (Something similar happens in a glass of water, when, stirring the water with a spoon, you set it into rotation - the water moves away from the center of the glass and rises at the edges.) Now is the time to explain the principle of operation of the lunar brake. It turns out that the water humps are not on the line connecting the centers of the Earth and the Moon (as shown for simplicity in figure 1), but are somewhat shifted to the side (Fig. 2). This happens for the following reason. The earth, rotating around its axis, carries with it the water in the ocean (this happens because there is friction).
