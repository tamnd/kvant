Observations over the fog

A.BOROVOY

HAVE YOU LOOKED AT HOW THE EVENING MIST APPEARS OVER THE RIVER? It does not appear immediately, but first thickens over whirlpools and riffles. Individual light clouds in the moonlight resemble female figures in white clothes. And you begin to understand our ancestors, who created so many legends about mermaids. We also encounter descriptions of this sometimes amazingly beautiful, and more often alarming and mysterious natural phenomenon on the pages of works of fiction. In the margins of this and the next page are several prose and poetic quotations that describe the fog. Try to guess who their authors are. How often you can admire the fog depends on the terrain. For example, for residents of Newfoundland, an island located off the coast of Canada in a place where the warm Gulf Stream collides with the cold Labrador Current, fogs are constant guests. For the third part of the year, including almost all summer days, the island is covered with thick haze. Probably, even the most beautiful description of fog is unlikely to evoke admiration among the residents of Newfoundland. The real “land of fogs” is England, “Foggy Albion”. In our country, the coasts of the northern seas - the Baltic, Okhotsk, and the Kola Peninsula - are especially rich in foggy days. If natural fogs are rare where you live, do not forget that at home you can see them literally at every step. A white cloud emerging from the spout of a boiling kettle is fog. In everyday life we ​​call it steam, but this is incorrect, since water steam is a colorless gas. If you quickly open a bottle of sparkling water, smoke will appear above the neck at the first moment - this is also fog. On a frosty day outside, fog swirls over an open water surface, over ice holes or ice holes, rushes into the room through an open window, and appears when breathing (again, we are used to saying “breath steam”).
