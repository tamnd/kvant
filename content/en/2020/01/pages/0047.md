the one at the table - with or without a typewriter. At one or two o'clock in the afternoon there was an afternoon snack consisting of milk or kefir with bread. After afternoon tea we worked a little more, but usually went for a long walk or, in winter, skiing, until 4 o'clock in the afternoon. Then we went to bed for half an hour. At 5 o'clock there was lunch. After lunch, we sometimes did some more work, usually secondary work: rewriting or the like. The evening was devoted to reading, music, and receiving guests. Before going to bed, we also liked to take a short walk. We went to bed around 10 o'clock. But, of course, when you work and begin to achieve a solution to some important problem, everything recedes into the background, there is no longer any daily routine. – You, like many mathematicians, love serious music. Tell us why. – Your remark about many mathematicians who are interested in serious music seems correct to me. If you go to a concert hall, especially the Small Hall of the Moscow Conservatory, you will see a disproportionate number of mathematicians there. Apparently, there are some deep connections between mathematical creativity and a real interest in music. But it seems to me quite difficult to find out and explain these connections. I will note, however, that my friend Pavel Sergeevich Aleksandrov said that for him every direction of mathematical thought, topic for creative reflection was associated with one or another specific piece of music. Among my favorite composers I would name, first of all, Mozart, Schumann, and, of course, the greatest musicians - Bach, Beethoven. – Linguists and literary scholars have paid attention to your publications on poetry. What can you say about this less common combination: mathematics and poetry? – I would like to divide this question into two, since my passion for poetry has the same involuntary, spontaneous nature.

As for my scientific works on the metrics and rhythm of Russian verse, they really attracted the attention of literary experts, but still this is a rather special area of ​​research, in which it is not necessary for everyone to be interested. – Do you go in for sports? Which one? – I have never been involved in competitive sports. If I'm not mistaken, I've only competed in a 10-kilometer race on skis three times in my life. But I have always been very fond of long walks on foot and skis, and I have taken long trips by kayak or boat. I really like swimming and hiking in the mountains. In all these activities, I appreciate not only their health benefits, but the joy of communicating with nature that they bring. I have always loved swimming in the surf. On sunny March days I like to do long ski runs in just shorts. During such March ski runs, I like to swim in the middle of snowdrifts shining in the sun in a river that has just opened up from ice. However, I do not necessarily advise you to imitate me in all this - you can simply sign up for some sports section that attracts you. – Andrey Nikolaevich, what would you like to wish our readers? – I myself am a scientist, and, of course, first of all, I wish our readers to make one or another contribution to science, big or at least small. I note, however, that if all our readers began to write independent scientific works, then scientific journals would not withstand such an onslaught. Therefore, I will also express a more modest wish - that your school passion for mathematics will be useful to you in later life. In «Kvant» we are trying to show you how diverse the applications of mathematical science are.
