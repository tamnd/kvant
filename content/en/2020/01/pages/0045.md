OUR INTERVIEWS

Conversation with Andrei Nikolaevich Kolmogorov

We are in an old wooden house in the village of Komarovka near Moscow, where Andrei Nikolaevich usually spends the end of the week. Bright, modestly furnished room. In one of the corners there is an old but high-quality record player with special shelves for records. The walls are lined with shelves of books. In the middle of the room there is a large table with many books, reprints of articles, manuscripts, art albums. Andrei Nikolaevich sits by the window at a small desk. Next to the typewriter and neatly folded, scribbled sheets of paper there is a tape recorder on which our conversation is recorded. We bring to your attention a transcript of this conversation. – Andrey Nikolaevich, we often hear about the increasing specialization of science. At the same time, it is known that you were involved in such widely separated areas of mathematics as probability theory and algebraic topology, mathematical logic and the theory of dynamical systems. What do you think is the future of science – universality or specialization? – Mathematics is great. One person cannot study all its ramifications. In this sense, specialization is inevitable. But at the same time, mathematics is a unified science. More and more new connections arise between its sections, sometimes in the most unexpected ways. Some sections serve as tools for other sections. Therefore, confining mathematicians within too narrow limits must be disastrous for our science. The situation is made easier by the fact that work in the field of mathematics is, in principle, collective. There must be a number of mathematicians who understand the interconnections between the most diverse areas of mathematics. You also need, at least in general terms, to understand the connections between your special field of research with related fields, to understand that, in essence, scientific work in mathematics is a collective work. – What can you say about the relationship and connections between applied and pure mathematics? – First of all, it should be noted that the very distinction between applied and pure mathematics is extremely arbitrary. Questions that seem to belong to pure mathematics and have no applications very often unexpectedly turn out to be important for various applications. On the other hand, when studying applied mathematics, a scientist almost inevitably comes across related questions that are solved by the same methods, attracting him with their logical beauty, but, strictly speaking, no longer receiving direct applications. Probably, in the practical work of a mathematician it is necessary to show due breadth. There is no doubt that mathematicians must, it is their duty, to deal with all those questions that are urgently imposed by questions of practice. If related questions, even if they do not have immediate applications, are attractive, if only because of the beauty and naturalness of the problems that arise, then, of course, they also need to be addressed.
