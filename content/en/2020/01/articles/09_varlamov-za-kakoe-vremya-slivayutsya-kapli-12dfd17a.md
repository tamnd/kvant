HAVE YOU EVER SITTED AT A Plate Of Clear Chicken Broth Covered With Specks Of Golden Fat? If you didn’t have an appetite, then you probably tried to chase these specks around the plate, tearing apart the partitions between them or, conversely, connecting the specks together, watching how slowly they merged, taking the shape of a circle. Another observation from the same series is the fusion of mercury droplets from a broken thermometer (be careful - this is an unsafe activity, since mercury is very poisonous). True, here you don’t even have time to blink your eye before two droplets form one. What determines the time of merging of liquid droplets? Before we try to answer this question, let's talk a little about the reason for the merging of drops - the surface tension of the liquid. Moreover, we will try to look at this phenomenon from an energy point of view. Molecules located in a thin layer of liquid near the surface are in special conditions. The fact is that they have neighbors identical to them only on one side of the surface, in contrast to molecules inside a liquid, surrounded on all sides by their own kind. We are used to saying that the Moon revolves around the Earth. In fact, both the Moon and the Earth rotate around a common center. If this rotation did not exist, then under the influence of gravity the Moon would fall to the Earth (more precisely, the Earth and the Moon would fall on each other - after all, the Moon also attracts the Earth). How does this rotation happen? Let's imagine that the masses of the Earth and the Moon are the same. Then they will move around a point that lies in the middle of the segment connecting their centers. Now let's mentally “increase” the mass of the Earth. Then the center of rotation will shift towards the Earth. Since the mass of the Earth is much greater than the mass of the Moon (81 times), the center $вращения^{С}$ turns out to lie inside the globe (but it is necessarily displaced relative to the center of the Earth$^{О).}$ This rotation significantly affects the pattern of tides. Water recedes from the center of rotation. (Something similar happens in a glass of water, when, stirring the water with a spoon, you set it into rotation - the water moves away from the center of the glass and rises at the edges.) The energy of the electromagnetic field is determined at each point in space at each moment of time. An electromagnetic wave transfers energy, and the concept of energy flux density can be introduced. This is what we call a quantity equal to the field energy flowing per unit time through a unit surface. Each wave is also characterized by a phase. If, when two light beams are added, the difference in their phases remains the same all the time, we say that we are dealing with coherent beams. In the interference pattern resulting from the addition of two coherent beams, a spatial redistribution of light energy occurs. In the bright bands the energy is greater than the sum of the energies of the stacked beams; in dark stripes, on the contrary, less. The excess energy in the light stripes is just compensated by its lack in the dark ones.
