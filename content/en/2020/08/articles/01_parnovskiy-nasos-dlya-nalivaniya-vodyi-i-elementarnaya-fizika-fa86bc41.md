S. PARNOVSKY

PUMP IN THE KITCHEN? THIS IS RARE unless it is something plumbing or part of a water purification filter. But you can still remember kitchen appliances that use a pump. In its simplest form, it is designed for pouring ordinary drinking water, delivered in large bottles (Fig.1). A couple of presses on the lid, which serves as the handle of a hand pump, and water is already pouring into the placed container. The physics behind this is very simple: a pump drives air into a plastic bottle, creating excess air pressure in the upper part, above the water. This pressure is transferred to the water, and after it exceeds a certain value, the water will begin to Fig. 1. Water bottle flow from the tube.
