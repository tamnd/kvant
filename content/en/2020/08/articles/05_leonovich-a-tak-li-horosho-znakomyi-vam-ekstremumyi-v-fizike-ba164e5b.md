extremes in physics

How, having a certain amount, choose the maximum quantity of the necessary goods in the store; How to spend the minimum time preparing for an exam, counting on a high grade? But the tasks are more serious: reduce to the limit the weight of the device sent to Mars; produce as many products as possible using limited equipment; achieve the lowest transport costs when delivering goods; launch a rocket to maximum altitude with a given fuel supply... This is nothing more than a problem of searching for the largest and smallest values, maxima and minima, generally extrema, or an optimization problem. The last centuries of physics and mathematics - sometimes at odds, more often “side by side” - have struggled with methods for solving them and, without exaggeration, have achieved excellent results, advancing them into economics, chemistry, and medicine...

It's interesting that...

...the extreme properties of the circle and the ball in Ancient Greece were known from the 4th century BC. However, problems of this kind were then studied only by geometric methods, and each of them required a specific technique for its solution. ...general approaches to the study of extremal problems “provoked” by practice began to appear only in the 17th century, which led to the creation of differential and integral calculus - a powerful research tool in natural science. ...Vincenzo Viviani, an Italian scientist, a student of Galileo, who built a pendulum clock based on his teacher’s idea, and, on behalf of Torricelli, carried out the famous experiment to prove the existence of atmospheric pressure, was famous

From the idea of ​​the shortest path taken by light, he came to the statement about the minimum time spent passing through an inhomogeneous medium, which allowed him to derive the law of refraction of light. ...confirmation that nature uses extreme solutions is the shape of the cells of a bee honeycomb, the construction of which uses the least amount of wax for a given volume, or the shape of a soap film, limited by two wire rings and having the smallest surface area. ... while studying the movement of the centers of gravity of bodies, Torricelli relied on the principle that now bears his name and states that a mechanical system in a state with the lowest potential energy is in equilibrium. ...of all the balls rolling along various smooth grooves from one specific point to another, the ball sliding along the cycloid (or brachistochrone) will be the fastest to reach the target. The creation of the calculus of variations began with the solution of this and similar problems arising in geometry, mechanics, and physics. ...the further development of the theory of extremal problems in the 20th century was caused by the needs of astronautics, energy, and transport, which led to the rapid growth of such scientific fields as linear programming, optimal control and catastrophe theory.
