Ball number 3 is first held at the top of the inclined plane and then released from external influence. The ball begins to slowly roll, but it does so very (and very) slowly. What could be the reason for such a slow movement? Experiment 3. Ball number 4 begins to move from the top position on the inclined plane downwards in jumps, simulating the movement of the body in “steps”. If the ball in the upper position is turned in a direction perpendicular to the inclined plane at a certain angle and released again, then the behavior of the ball when rolling does not change. What could cause such a jerky motion of the ball? Experiment 4. The experiment involves balls numbered 5 and 6. The balls are placed on a horizontal surface, and each ball is independently installed on this surface at rest in only one position. By applying your hand to the top of each ball, they deflect it to some sufficiently large angle from the vertical. After this, ball number 5 very quickly (!) returns to its original state. If the same ball is deflected from the vertical in some other direction and released, then it immediately returns to its original position. Ball number 6, after removing the external influence, oscillates for quite a long time and only then returns to its original state of rest. In addition, if you hold both balls in your hands, then the first sensation indicates that the masses of the balls are quite significant, and no movements of bodies are felt inside the balls. Why do the balls behave so differently before returning to their original state of rest? Experiment 5. The photo on figure 1 shows a ball number 7 lying motionless on a horizontal surface.
