6 (“Vasiliev’s Game”). There are 20 numbers written: 1, 2, …, 20. Two players take turns putting “+” or “–” signs in front of these numbers (the sign can be placed in front of any free number). The first one strives to ensure that after arranging all 20 signs, the sum of all numbers is as small as possible in absolute value. What is the largest absolute sum that the second player can secure for himself? (Sixth All-Russian Olympiad, 1966, Voronezh)

7 (“Skaters”). After the performances of 20 skaters, each of the 9 judges, at his own discretion, distributes places among them from 1st to 20th. It turned out that for each skater the places assigned to him by different judges differ by no more than 3. Let's calculate the sum of places received by each skater and arrange these numbers in ascending order: $с_{1} d с_{2} d с_{3} d... d с_{20}$. What is the greatest value that $с_{1}$ can have? (Second All-Union Olympiad, 1968, Leningrad)

9 (“Nerve Networks”). In an endless chain of nerve cells, each cell can be in one of two states: “rest” and “excitement”. If a cell is excited at the moment, then it sends a signal that, after a unit of time (say, one millisecond), reaches both cells neighboring it. Each cell is excited if and only if it receives a signal from one of its neighboring cells; if signals arrive simultaneously from both sides, they are extinguished and the cell is not excited. For example, if at the initial moment t 0 three neighboring cells are excited and the rest are left alone, then the excitation will spread, as shown in figure 2. (Excited cells are black.)

Let only one cell be excited at the initial moment t 0. How many cells will be in an excited state after 15 ms? in 65 ms? in 1000 ms? in t ms? What will happen if the chain is not infinite, but contains only N cells connected in a circle (Fig.3) - will the excitation be maintained indefinitely or will it die out? (“Problem book «Kvant», Fig. 3 problem M19)

10 (“Parallelepipeds”). There are 4 points given in space that do not lie in the same plane. How many different parallelepipeds are there for which all these points serve as vertices? (Seventh All-Union Olympiad, 1973, Chisinau)
