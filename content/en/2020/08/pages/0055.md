3. Molting of apple-eaters (7 points) In the spring, apple-eaters molt, each of them tries to shed all their needles. But due to the characteristics of the body, apple-eaters shed needles only in packs: an apple-eater can shed 3 needles with any number of needles, no less than 3; in addition, with an even number of needles, he can shed half of them, and with an odd number of at least 5, he can shed 4 needles. Moreover, if the number of needles on an apple-eater after its change is divisible by 3, then it grows 4 new needles, and if it is not divisible by 3, but is divided by 7, then 6 new needles. There are 4 molting apple eaters in the enclosure. At the moment, the first one has 31 needles, the second one has 73, the third one has 19, and the fourth one has 122. How many of them have the ability to reset all the needles (the number of resets is not limited)?

5. First-graders (15 points) Seven first-graders stand in one row. Each of them can be in one of two states: 1 – looking at the phone, 2 – chewing a sandwich. It is known that the far right first-grader can be transferred from an observing to a chewing state by simply taking the phone out of his hands and slipping a sandwich (and vice versa). The state of other first-graders can be changed only if the first-grader closest to the right is looking at the phone, and the other first-graders on the right (if there are any) are chewing sandwiches. In 5 seconds you can change the state of only one first-grader. At first, all first-graders look at the phone. Since it's lunchtime, it's necessary to put them all in a chewing state. In what minimum number of seconds can the teacher do this?
