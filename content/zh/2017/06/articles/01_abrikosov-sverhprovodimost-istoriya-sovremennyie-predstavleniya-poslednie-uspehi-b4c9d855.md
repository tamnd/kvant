The phenomenon of superconductivity was discovered by the Dutch physicist Kamerlingh Onnes in 1911. Kamerlingh Onnes was the first to obtain liquid helium, and he used it to create cryostats - devices in which the temperature can be maintained at very low temperatures. In particular, he decided to check the correctness of the ideas that existed at that time about the behavior of electrical resistance at low temperatures. Measuring the resistance of mercury, Kamerlingh Onnes discovered that it abruptly vanishes at a temperature of about 4 K. This phenomenon was called superconductivity, and the temperature of transition to the superconducting state was called critical. Currently, many superconductors are known with a wide variety of critical temperatures, from fractions of a degree to about 100 K.
